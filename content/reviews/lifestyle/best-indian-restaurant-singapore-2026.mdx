## What to Look for in an Indian Restaurant

**Google rating and review volume.** A minimum 4.5-star Google rating with at least 100 reviews is the baseline for a reliable Indian restaurant in Singapore. Review count matters: a restaurant with 4.9 stars from 1,700+ reviews, like Kalam Restaurant, provides stronger evidence of consistent quality than one with 5 stars from 12 reviews. Check that recent reviews (last 3 months) maintain the same standard as the overall rating.

**Cuisine type and specialisation.** Indian cuisine encompasses hugely different regional styles. North Indian restaurants focus on tandoor cooking, rich curries, and leavened breads; South Indian restaurants specialise in rice plates, dosas, idlis, and lighter gravies. A restaurant that does both may do neither particularly well. If you have a specific dish in mind, biryani, dosa, or korma, check whether the restaurant specialises in that style before booking.

**Vegetarian and dietary options.** India has one of the world's highest rates of vegetarianism, and many Indian restaurants in Singapore reflect this with extensive meat-free menus. If dietary requirements are important to your group, confirm before visiting whether the restaurant uses shared cooking surfaces or stocks for vegetarian dishes. Dedicated vegetarian restaurants like Pasi Rusi and Butter & Spice Katong eliminate the ambiguity.

**Setting and price range.** Indian restaurants in Singapore span a wide range, from casual canteen-style to formal fine dining. Expect to pay SGD 15–30 per person at casual venues and SGD 60–120 at fine dining restaurants like Spice Mahal. The setting also affects suitability: Boat Quay restaurants like Shahi Maharaja are well suited to client dinners, while neighbourhood bistros like Tani Bistro suit relaxed evenings with friends.

**Reservation policy and wait times.** The best Indian restaurants in Singapore, particularly in Little India, fill up on weekends and public holidays. Restaurants rated 4.8+ with high review counts tend to be busy. Check whether the restaurant takes reservations online or by phone, and book in advance for groups of four or more. Walk-in availability is generally better on weekday lunches.
