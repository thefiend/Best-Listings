Finding the best fine dining restaurants in Singapore means navigating a dense field of Michelin-starred kitchens, celebrity chef outposts, and independently-run tasting menus, all competing at the highest level. We evaluated more than 20 fine dining venues across Singapore, scoring each on Google rating, review volume, cuisine quality, ambiance, and value relative to price point. This list ranks the top 10 options for special occasions, business dinners, anniversary meals, and everything in between, ranked by overall guest satisfaction and consistency in 2026.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Restaurant Fiz, a Southeast Asian tasting menu restaurant at Tanjong Pagar with a 4.9-star rating across 397 Google reviews.
- **Most reviewed:** VUE at OUE Bayfront holds 1,714 Google reviews at 4.7 stars, making it the most-reviewed fine dining venue on this list.
- **Best for special occasions:** Restaurant JAG at Robertson Quay scores 4.8 stars across 640 reviews and specialises in seasonal French cuisine driven by a single herb each menu cycle.
- **Singapore hosts 3 restaurants with two or more Michelin stars**, including Odette at the National Gallery, cementing the city's status as one of Asia's top fine dining destinations.
- **Expect to spend SGD 180 to SGD 450 per person** for a tasting menu at a top-tier Singapore fine dining restaurant, with wine pairing adding SGD 80 to SGD 200 on top.
- **A common mistake:** Booking the most Michelin-starred restaurant rather than matching the cuisine style and format to your occasion. A Japanese omakase and a French tasting menu are very different dining experiences.

## What to Look for in a Fine Dining Restaurant

**Google rating and review volume together.** A 4.9-star rating on 50 reviews is less reliable than a 4.7-star rating on 800 reviews. For fine dining, where you may be spending SGD 300 to SGD 500 per person, look for restaurants with both a high rating and a review count above 100. The volume shows the rating holds up across a diverse range of guests and occasions.

**Cuisine identity and format.** The best fine dining restaurants in Singapore have a clear and distinctive culinary identity. Ask yourself whether the restaurant is building something genuinely its own, or assembling familiar fine dining tropes. Restaurants like Restaurant Fiz and Seroja have a specific point of view rooted in this region. That specificity is usually a reliable indicator of kitchen conviction and consistency.

**Tasting menu versus à la carte.** Most serious fine dining in Singapore operates on a tasting menu format, which means the kitchen controls the evening's progression. This is excellent for occasions where you want a complete experience, but it removes individual choice. If your group has strong dietary preferences or significant restrictions, contact the restaurant before booking and confirm the kitchen can accommodate you properly.

**Setting suitability to occasion.** The right restaurant depends on who you are dining with and why. VUE's rooftop setting suits a celebration or a guest who wants to see the Singapore skyline. Ammakase's CBD location suits corporate entertaining. Pangium's Botanic Gardens setting suits a romantic or contemplative dinner. Match the environment to the occasion before choosing on food reputation alone.

**Reservation lead time.** The best fine dining restaurants in Singapore book out weeks to months in advance, particularly for Friday and Saturday evening sittings. If you need a specific date, contact the restaurant as far in advance as possible. Many restaurants release cancellations within two to three days of service, so checking again shortly before your target date is also worthwhile.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does fine dining cost in Singapore?

Tasting menus at the top fine dining restaurants in Singapore range from SGD 180 to SGD 450 per person for food. Wine pairing typically adds SGD 80 to SGD 200 per person. Set lunch menus at the same restaurants are often available for SGD 80 to SGD 160, offering the same kitchen quality at a lower price point.

### Which Singapore fine dining restaurants have Michelin stars?

Odette holds three Michelin stars and is consistently ranked on Asia's 50 Best Restaurants list. JAAN By Kirk Westaway, Cloudstreet, and Restaurant JAG have each held one or more Michelin stars in recent years. Restaurant Fiz, despite not yet holding a Michelin star, outperforms most starred venues in guest satisfaction ratings and is widely expected to receive recognition in coming guides.

### How far in advance do I need to book fine dining in Singapore?

For top-rated restaurants including Restaurant Fiz, Odette, and JAAN, book at least four to eight weeks in advance for weekend evenings. Weekday sittings and lunch services often have more availability. Most restaurants open reservations one to three months out on their own websites. Cancellations do come available, so checking the reservation system close to your target date is a useful strategy.

### What is the best fine dining restaurant in Singapore for a first-time visitor?

Restaurant Fiz offers the best combination of distinctive cuisine, strong guest satisfaction, and a culinary identity rooted in Singapore and the surrounding region. For a visitor wanting to understand this part of the world through food, it is a more rewarding choice than a French tasting menu that could exist in London or Paris.

### Are there fine dining options in Singapore with good vegetarian or vegan menus?

Several restaurants on this list accommodate vegetarian tasting menus with advance notice, including Restaurant JAG (which structures menus around botanicals and herbs), Odette, and Seroja. Contact each restaurant directly at least 48 hours before your reservation to confirm what can be prepared.

### What is the difference between omakase and a tasting menu?

A tasting menu is a sequence of courses chosen by the chef, typically in a European kitchen context, progressing through different preparations and flavour profiles. Omakase is a Japanese term meaning "I leave it to you": the chef prepares whatever is best that day, often at a counter where guests watch the preparation. Omakase @ Stevens and Ammakase are counter-format omakase restaurants; Restaurant JAG and Odette are tasting menu restaurants.

### Is fine dining in Singapore child-friendly?

Most tasting menu restaurants in Singapore are best suited to adults and older teenagers who can engage with a multi-course format lasting two to three hours. Some restaurants, including VUE, have more flexible formats that can accommodate families. Contact the restaurant before booking with children to confirm format flexibility and minimum age policies.

### What should I wear to a fine dining restaurant in Singapore?

Most fine dining restaurants in Singapore request smart casual to formal attire. Avoid shorts, flip-flops, and sleeveless tops. For Michelin-starred restaurants and rooftop venues like VUE and JAAN, smart casual at minimum is expected. Check the restaurant's website for specific dress code guidance, as requirements vary.

### Can I get a window table at JAAN or VUE?

Window and view-facing seats at elevated restaurants like JAAN (Level 70) and VUE (Level 19 rooftop) are in high demand. When making a reservation, note your preference in the booking remarks field or contact the restaurant directly. Requests are accommodated where possible but cannot be guaranteed. Arriving for a dinner reservation at dusk or early evening maximises the likelihood of natural light views.

### What are red flags when booking a fine dining restaurant in Singapore?

Unclear or unavailable pricing online, no confirmation email after booking, unresponsive customer service, and very recent negative reviews about service inconsistency are the main warning signs. For special occasions, always confirm your reservation by phone or email within 48 hours of your booking date.
