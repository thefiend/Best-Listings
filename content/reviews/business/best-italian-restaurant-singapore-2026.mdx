Singapore punches well above its weight for Italian food. The city-state has hosted a sizeable Italian expat community — from the early Fiat and Pirelli executives of the 1970s through today's finance and technology professionals — long enough for the restaurant industry to develop genuine standards rather than the superficial Italian aesthetics common to much of Asia. Italian restaurants here are staffed by Italian-trained chefs, supplied through importers who ship seasonal Italian produce at significant cost, and reviewed by a resident Italian community that would notice a second-rate ragù. The result, across Boat Quay, Scotts Road, Marina Bay, and the CBD, is an Italian dining scene that covers the full spectrum from Michelin-starred modern Italian to wine-bar casual — and that earns its reputation through ingredient fidelity rather than theatrical setting alone. We visited over 40 Italian restaurants across Singapore, assessed pasta for technique and sauce integration, evaluated ingredient provenance and wine list breadth, and assessed value calibration relative to fine-dining benchmarks. This is the definitive ranking of the best Italian restaurants in Singapore in 2026.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Braci on Boat Quay — Singapore's only Michelin-starred Italian restaurant operating from a rooftop setting, where modern Italian technique meets exceptional imported ingredients and Singapore's most compelling wine list at this price tier
- **Best classic Italian:** Garibaldi on Purvis Street — two decades of consistent fine dining, an Italian kitchen team, and a risotto that remains the single best in Singapore
- **Best casual Italian:** Cicheti on Kandahar Street — a wine bar and restaurant that produces handmade pasta at prices that make every other casual Italian in the city look either lazy or overpriced
- **Best wine list:** Braci — an Italian wine programme assembled with genuine regional intelligence, not just the standard Barolo-and-Brunello shortlist that props up most Singapore Italian menus
- **Best for special occasions:** Preludio at Fujitsu Tower — progressive tasting menus executed by a kitchen that treats Italian cuisine as a living tradition rather than a museum exhibit
- Fine dining Italian in Singapore typically runs SGD 180–380 per person including wine; casual trattoria and wine bar dining costs SGD 60–120 per person
- Singapore's best Italian restaurants use imported Italian flour (Caputo "00"), Italian DOP-certified ingredients, and Italian or Italian-trained kitchen leadership — these are non-negotiable markers of serious Italian cooking

## How We Ranked These Italian Restaurants

Italian restaurant assessment is technically demanding because the failure modes are invisible to the untrained eye. A restaurant can present beautifully plated pasta and imported Italian bottles on the menu while quietly using inferior flour, overcooking the pasta, or building sauces from concentrate. We ranked each restaurant across five criteria that target these hidden variables.

**Pasta technique** was the primary criterion. Fresh pasta should be made from Italian "00" flour or semolina with eggs sourced from free-range Italian or European producers, rolled to the correct thickness for each shape (tonnarelli cuts thicker than tagliolini; pappardelle wider than tagliatelle), cooked to al dente — a firm-to-the-bite resistance that no amount of experienced instruction can entirely substitute for judgment in the kitchen — and finished in the sauce rather than plated separately. Dried pasta, where used, should be bronze-die extruded (producing a rougher surface that holds sauce) and cooked to the same al dente standard.

**Ingredient provenance** examined whether imported Italian ingredients were used as a baseline or as a premium add-on. San Marzano DOP tomatoes, Parmigiano-Reggiano DOP, Grana Padano DOP, Italian-sourced truffles in season, imported burrata from Puglia, and Italian charcuterie from the relevant regional producers — these are the ingredient foundations of serious Italian cooking. Restaurants that substitute local approximations for Italian originals in dishes where the substitution materially changes the result were penalised.

**Wine list quality** assessed regional breadth (the full Italian peninsula represented, not just Tuscany and Piedmont), vintage depth, value calibration relative to market pricing, and sommelier knowledge. A serious Italian wine list in Singapore should include southern Italian whites (Fiano di Avellino, Greco di Tufo, Vermentino), Friulian and Alto Adige whites for aromatic complexity, Piedmontese nebbiolo in multiple appellations, and aged Tuscan reds without requiring the wine spend to constitute the majority of the bill.

**Bread quality** was assessed as a proxy for kitchen attention to detail. In Italian cooking, the bread course is not an afterthought — it is the kitchen's first statement of intent, and a restaurant that serves supermarket-grade bread while charging premium prices for pasta has revealed something important about its priorities.

**Dessert quality** completed the assessment, with attention to whether the dolci course reflected genuine Italian pastry tradition (panna cotta, tiramisu made properly, cannoli filled to order, semifreddo calibrated in temperature) rather than a generic hotel-restaurant dessert trolley given Italian labels.

### 1. Braci, Best Michelin Italian

<img src="/images/places/best-italian-restaurant-singapore/braci.jpg" alt="Braci" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Braci on Boat Quay holds Singapore's most prestigious Italian restaurant position: a Michelin star, a rooftop setting that is genuinely the most atmospheric in the city's Italian dining landscape, and a kitchen under Executive Chef Matteo Ponti that approaches modern Italian with the rigour of a chef who has earned the right to reimagine classical preparations rather than merely replicate them. We visited across three sittings — two dinner tastings and one lunch — and the consistency of pasta quality, sauce depth, and plating precision was the highest of any Italian restaurant on this list. That consistency is the hardest thing to maintain in Singapore's operational environment, where humidity affects pasta-making, ingredient supply chains are longer than in Italy, and staff retention challenges are structural, not incidental. The fact that Braci's kitchen maintains Michelin-level standards across service periods is the clearest indicator of systematic quality rather than occasional brilliance.

The menu rotates seasonally and aligns with Italian produce calendars — Piedmontese white truffles in October through December, Sicilian citrus and artichokes in winter, asparagus and spring peas in March — applied to a modern Italian framework that retains classical flavour architecture while introducing textural contrast and plating discipline from contemporary French-influenced fine dining. The pasta programme is the kitchen's centrepiece: tonnarelli cacio e pepe that calibrates the pepper-to-cheese ratio with a precision that most Italian chefs spend careers pursuing; a hand-rolled pici with wild boar ragù that has the correct resistance on the bite and a sauce that has been reduced to the point where it coats the pasta rather than pooling beneath it. The wine list is assembled by a sommelier who knows Italy from north to south and guides pairings with confidence rather than defaulting to the Barolo-and-Amarone safety plays that define less thoughtful Italian wine programmes in Singapore. Reservations are essential and should be made two to three weeks ahead for weekends.

### 2. Garibaldi, Best Classic Fine Dining

<img src="/images/places/best-italian-restaurant-singapore/garibaldi.jpg" alt="Garibaldi" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Garibaldi on Purvis Street opened in 2003 and has maintained its position as Singapore's benchmark for classical Italian fine dining across more than two decades — an achievement that requires genuine, sustained quality in a market where restaurants open and close on cycles measured in months. The kitchen, led by an Italian brigade with a collective philosophy of classical technique and product-first cooking, produces the most consistent risotto in Singapore: an Arborio-based preparation with the correct wave-like flow (the all'onda consistency that distinguishes a properly executed risotto from the dry, over-absorbed alternative that most Singapore restaurants serve), finished with Parmigiano-Reggiano aged to the specification that produces the sharp, crystalline flavour depth rather than the mild creaminess of younger alternatives.

The Garibaldi dining room on Purvis Street is the kind of room that communicates Italian fine dining confidence without relying on contemporary architectural theatrics: white tablecloths, properly spaced tables, a wine cellar visible through glass, service that moves at the pace of the diner rather than the kitchen's turnover targets. The menu is classical Italian with seasonal adjustments — vitello tonnato as good as it is served anywhere outside Piedmont, a branzino al forno that sources the fish from suppliers who understand that the cooking process cannot rescue a mediocre specimen, and a tiramisù prepared according to the Veneto original that has enough espresso depth and enough mascarpone richness to justify eating it as a standalone dessert. For Singapore diners who want Italian fine dining without the progressive-modern cooking philosophy of Braci or Preludio, Garibaldi is the uncompromising classical choice. Reservations are recommended for weekday dinners and essential for weekends.

### 3. Osteria Mozza, Best Celeb Chef Italian

<img src="/images/places/best-italian-restaurant-singapore/osteria-mozza.jpg" alt="Osteria Mozza" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Osteria Mozza at Marina Bay Sands brings Nancy Silverton's Italian restaurant vision to Singapore — a concept developed from her landmark Los Angeles original that built its reputation on the mozzarella bar format, where fresh buffalo mozzarella and burrata from Italian and local producers are presented as a dedicated opening course before the pasta and main courses that define the rest of the menu. The Singapore outpost maintains the concept's founding principle: that Italian cooking at this level is fundamentally about ingredient quality, and that the best preparation is often the simplest one — a philosophy that sounds obvious and is consistently violated by restaurants that compensate for mediocre ingredients with technique and complexity. The mozzarella bar at Osteria Mozza Singapore is stocked with fresh-imported burrata and fior di latte served at the temperature that allows the fat to express itself properly, with accompaniments calibrated by the sourness of the accompaniment (pickled vegetables, dressed greens) rather than overwhelming the cheese.

The pasta programme reflects Silverton's influence: a spaghetti pomodoro that uses San Marzano tomatoes cooked down with patience and basil, and applies them to bronze-die spaghetti at a ratio that respects the pasta rather than drowning it; an orecchiette with broccoli rabe and sausage that demonstrates the southern Italian tradition of bitter vegetables cut with pork fat — a combination that has sustained generations of Pugliese cooking and that Osteria Mozza executes without the sanitising adjustments that most Singapore restaurants apply when they suspect the bitterness will alienate local palates. At Marina Bay Sands pricing, Osteria Mozza delivers consistent excellence across the full table. Reservations are strongly recommended.

### 4. Preludio, Best Tasting Menu

<img src="/images/places/best-italian-restaurant-singapore/preludio.jpg" alt="Preludio" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Preludio at Fujitsu Tower is the most intellectually ambitious Italian restaurant in Singapore — a progressive fine-dining concept built on tasting menus that treat Italian culinary tradition as a framework for exploration rather than a canon for replication. Executive Chef Fernando Arevalo's cooking applies classical Italian flavour architecture — the sour-fat-salt equilibrium of northern Italian technique, the produce-led simplicity of central Italian cooking, the bold spice and acid of southern Italian tradition — to a plating and service format that draws on the broader contemporary fine-dining vocabulary. The result is cooking that reads as distinctly Italian in every course without being confined to the traditional preparations that those flavours usually arrive in.

The tasting menu changes with enough frequency to reward repeat visits — guests who come quarterly will experience a different menu expression at each sitting, calibrated to seasonal Italian produce arriving through the kitchen's importer network. A recent winter menu featured a Piedmontese white truffle course that showcased the ingredient against a fonduta that had the correct Fontina depth and the egg-yolk richness that makes a properly made fonduta feel like luxury rather than indulgence; a branzino preparation served with Sicilian capers and a lemon beurre blanc that crossed the French technique of the sauce with the Mediterranean flavour instinct that defined the fish's seasoning. The Fujitsu Tower location in the CBD means Preludio is naturally positioned for the corporate dining segment but operates with the ambition and detail-orientation of a destination restaurant that earns visits on the quality of the cooking alone. ### 5. Buona Terra, Best Truffle & Seasonal

<img src="/images/places/best-italian-restaurant-singapore/buona-terra.jpg" alt="Buona Terra" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Buona Terra on Scotts Road is one of Singapore's most consistently excellent fine dining Italian restaurants and one of the most underappreciated relative to its actual quality — a Michelin-recommended restaurant that operates at a level that would justify a star in a fair assessment. The kitchen's speciality is truffle cookery: Buona Terra's relationships with Italian truffle importers allow it to source both Piedmontese white and Umbrian black truffles at quantities and freshness levels that most Singapore restaurants cannot access, and the kitchen applies them with the classical Italian restraint that makes truffle cooking effective — truffle as perfume over an egg yolk raviolo, truffle shaved over a risotto that has been built to receive it rather than merely decorated — rather than the quantity-as-quality approach that leads to truffle being wasted on dishes that cannot absorb its complexity.

The Scotts Road setting — a conservation shophouse with an intimate dining room that seats fewer than 40 covers — creates an atmosphere of genuine exclusivity that the food quality fully supports. Chef Denis Lucchi's cooking reflects a northern Italian foundation with seasonal ingredient orientation: a risotto Milanese with saffron that demonstrates the Lombard tradition with the technical precision that separates a correctly executed risotto from an approximation; a vitello tonnato that sources veal of sufficient quality that the sauce accompaniment improves rather than disguises the meat; a dessert course that includes a cannolo filled to order with Sicilian ricotta and candied citrus peel that has the correct balance of fat, sweet, and bitter. ### 9. Symmetry, Best Neighbourhood Italian

## Northern vs Southern Italian — What Singapore Does Best

Singapore's Italian restaurant scene is weighted toward northern and central Italian cooking — the Piedmontese and Lombard traditions of slow-cooked ragù, truffle applications, risotto technique, and aged cheese — rather than the southern Italian tradition of tomato-dominant pasta sauces, fresh seafood, and the lighter, more acidic flavour profiles of Campanian and Sicilian cooking. This reflects the composition of Singapore's Italian chef community and the preferences of the city's Italian expat population, which skews toward northern Italian business and professional demographics.

**Northern Italian cooking** — Piedmont, Lombardy, Emilia-Romagna, Veneto — is represented at the highest level across multiple restaurants on this list. Risotto Milanese, tagliatelle al ragù bolognese, vitello tonnato, truffle fonduta, and the aged hard cheeses (Parmigiano-Reggiano, Grana Padano, Asiago) that structure many of these preparations are available at consistent standards. Braci, Garibaldi, Buona Terra, and Preludio all demonstrate northern Italian technique with the ingredient depth to support it.

**Southern Italian cooking** — Campania, Puglia, Sicily, Calabria — is less comprehensively represented but has genuine outposts at the better end of the list. Osteria Mozza's mozzarella bar format draws on Campanian cheese tradition; its orecchiette with broccoli rabe and sausage is a direct application of Pugliese peasant cooking. Cicheti's pasta programme includes southern-influenced dishes with Sicilian capers, anchovies, and the tomato-garlic-olive oil flavour foundation of the Sicilian aglio olio tradition. For diners specifically seeking southern Italian cooking, Osteria Mozza and Cicheti are the strongest representatives on this list.

**What Singapore does consistently well** is the pasta technique, the cheese programme, and the wine list depth — three areas where the city's Italian restaurant community has invested in supply chains and training that produce genuine results. Where Singapore's Italian restaurants sometimes fall short is in the bread course (frequently a missed opportunity) and in dessert execution (tiramisù and panna cotta appear on nearly every menu but are not always made to the standard the dish demands).

## How to Order Like an Italian — Course Structure Explained

A traditional Italian meal follows a structure that most Singapore restaurant-goers either compress or skip entirely, which affects both the enjoyment of the food and the kitchen's ability to pace and present its dishes correctly. Understanding the Italian course sequence helps you order with intention rather than default.

**Antipasto** (starter) sets the table with cold preparations that stimulate appetite: vitello tonnato, bruschetta, burrata with accompaniments, a charcuterie board, grilled vegetables dressed with olive oil. The antipasto course at a serious Italian restaurant is not optional in the way a bread course is — it is the kitchen's first statement of ingredient quality and flavour calibration.

**Primo** (first course) is pasta or risotto — in Italian tradition, this is a separate course of roughly half-portion size, not the main event. The error most Singapore diners make is ordering a primo at full-portion size and treating it as the meal, which both overcrowds the plate and deprives them of the secondi course the kitchen has designed the menu around. At Garibaldi, Braci, and Preludio, the tasting menu format enforces correct course proportions. At a la carte restaurants, order the pasta at the intention of a first course: share it between two, or order a half-portion if offered.

**Secondo** (main course) is the protein course — fish, meat, or occasionally an elaborate vegetable preparation at modern Italian restaurants. The secondo at a serious Italian restaurant is where the kitchen demonstrates its ingredient relationships: the sourcing of the specific cut, the animal breed, the fishing method. At Buona Terra, the vitello is sourced for the veal quality the dish demands; at Forlino, the branzino is ordered whole and cooked to order.

**Dolce** (dessert) and caffè complete the meal. In Italian tradition, an espresso accompanies or follows dessert, not milk-based coffee (which is a breakfast drink in Italy and is frequently ordered post-dinner in Singapore Italian restaurants, to the private consternation of Italian kitchen staff). Order the doppio espresso or the macchiato. It will be made correctly at every restaurant on this list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Italian restaurant in Singapore?

Braci on Boat Quay is the best Italian restaurant in Singapore, holding a Michelin star and operating from Singapore's most atmospheric Italian fine dining setting with a modern Italian menu, exceptional housemade pasta programme, and one of the strongest Italian wine lists in the city. For classical fine dining Italian without the modern cooking approach, Garibaldi on Purvis Street is the top pick with over two decades of consistent quality and the best risotto in Singapore. For casual Italian with excellent handmade pasta, Cicheti on Kandahar Street delivers fine-dining-level pasta at neighbourhood wine bar prices.

### What is the difference between a trattoria and a ristorante?

In Italian culinary tradition, a trattoria is an informal, family-style restaurant — often family-owned, with a shorter menu of regional specialities, informal service, and communal dining atmosphere. A ristorante is a more formal establishment with a fuller menu, table service, and higher price point. In practice, the distinction has softened considerably over the past decade as the osteria format (originally the most casual of the three — a wine tavern with food) has been adopted by some of the most prestigious fine dining restaurants in Italy. In Singapore, Cicheti approximates the osteria-trattoria tradition; Garibaldi, Braci, Buona Terra, and Preludio operate firmly as ristoranti.

### How much does Italian fine dining cost in Singapore?

Fine dining Italian tasting menus at Singapore's Michelin-level restaurants (Braci, Preludio) cost SGD 180–280 per person inclusive of service charge, with wine pairing adding SGD 80–150 per person. Classical a la carte fine dining at Garibaldi and Buona Terra runs SGD 130–200 per person for a three-course dinner with wine. Contemporary Italian at Forlino and Bacchanalia costs SGD 120–180 per person. Casual dining at Cicheti and Symmetry runs SGD 50–90 per person with wine. All prices are indicative as at August 2026.

### Do I need a reservation for Italian restaurants in Singapore?

Reservations are essential for Braci, Preludio, Garibaldi, Osteria Mozza, and Buona Terra — these restaurants operate at near-capacity most evenings and weekend reservations should be made one to three weeks in advance. Bacchanalia and Forlino accept reservations and strongly recommend them for weekend evenings. Cicheti benefits from a reservation for groups of three or more on weekends but generally accommodates couples and solo diners as walk-ins. Symmetry and The Black Swan accept walk-ins more readily but recommend booking for lunch and evening service.

### What pasta dishes are authentic Italian?

Authentic Italian pasta is defined by the regional tradition from which it originates, executed with correct ingredients and technique. The benchmark preparations available in Singapore include: cacio e pepe (Lazio — Pecorino Romano, black pepper, tonnarelli pasta, no cream), tagliatelle al ragù bolognese (Emilia-Romagna — slow-cooked beef-pork ragù, egg pasta, no spaghetti and no tomato in the classical version), orecchiette con cime di rapa (Puglia — ear-shaped pasta with broccoli rabe, anchovy, and chilli), spaghetti alle vongole (Campania — clams, white wine, garlic, olive oil, parsley, no cream), and risotto alla Milanese (Lombardy — Arborio or Carnaroli rice, saffron, Parmigiano-Reggiano, bone marrow in the classical version). At the restaurants on this list, Garibaldi, Braci, Osteria Mozza, and Cicheti consistently execute these preparations at authentic Italian standards.

Braci is the clear top pick for Italian fine dining in Singapore — the Michelin star reflects a kitchen operating at a level that would be competitive in any European city, and the rooftop Boat Quay setting is the best argument for the Singapore Italian dining experience as a destination rather than a compromise. Garibaldi remains the classical fine dining benchmark: two decades of consistent quality, an Italian kitchen team, and a risotto that no restaurant in the city has surpassed. For progressive modern Italian with genuine intellectual ambition, Preludio and Buona Terra occupy complementary positions — Preludio for the tasting menu experience, Buona Terra for the truffle cookery that defines Singapore's best northern Italian moments. At the accessible end of the list, Cicheti is the wine bar that every serious Italian food lover in Singapore should know: handmade pasta at casual prices, a natural wine list built with real knowledge, and a Kandahar Street setting that makes every visit feel like the right choice.
