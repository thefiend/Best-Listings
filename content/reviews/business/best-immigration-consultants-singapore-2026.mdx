## What to Look for When Hiring an Immigration Consultant

**Transparency on fees and scope.** Reputable immigration consultants in Singapore will provide a written fee proposal that clearly specifies what is included: initial assessment, document compilation, application submission, follow-up with MOM or ICA, and whether appeal support is included if the application is rejected. Be wary of firms that quote a low headline fee but add charges at each stage of the process without prior disclosure.

**No guarantees of approval.** It is illegal for any immigration consultant to guarantee PR or EP approval: these decisions are made by government authorities, and no consultant has the ability to guarantee any outcome. Any firm that offers a guaranteed approval or implies a success rate that is not independently verifiable should be avoided. Reputable consultants will give an honest assessment of your approval probability based on your profile but will never guarantee an outcome.

**Consultant credentials and continuity.** Ask who specifically will manage your case and what their qualifications and experience are. A common client complaint in this industry is that the senior consultant presents at the initial meeting but the actual work is done by less experienced staff. Confirm who your day-to-day contact will be and ensure you can reach them directly throughout the process.

**Policy awareness.** Singapore immigration policy changes regularly. EP minimum salary thresholds are updated, PR assessment criteria shift, and new requirements are introduced with limited public notice. Your consultant should demonstrate current knowledge of these changes and their implications for your specific case during the initial consultation: not regurgitate general information that could be found on the MOM website.

**Track record with similar profiles.** Ask specifically about the consultant's experience with applicants who share your background: same nationality, same industry, similar employment history, or similar reason for previous rejection if applicable. Generic success statistics are less meaningful than experience with comparable cases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I apply for Singapore PR?

Singapore Permanent Residency can be applied for by Employment Pass or S Pass holders who have worked in Singapore for at least six months, immediate family members of Singapore Citizens or PRs, and certain other categories. Applications are submitted through ICA's e-PR portal with supporting documents including employment history, tax records, educational certificates, and personal statements. The process takes typically six to twelve months. A professional immigration consultant can significantly strengthen the application through strategic case construction and documentation.

### What is the difference between an EP, S Pass, and Work Permit in Singapore?

The Employment Pass is for foreign professionals earning at least SGD 5,000 per month (higher thresholds apply in financial services). The S Pass is for mid-skilled workers earning at least SGD 3,150 per month, subject to sector-based quotas. Work Permits are for semi-skilled workers in specific sectors including construction, marine, and process, with lower salary thresholds. EP and S Pass holders can apply for PR after a qualifying period; Work Permit holders generally cannot.

### How long does a Singapore PR application take?

ICA's published processing time for PR applications is approximately six to twelve months from submission of a complete application. In practice, processing times vary depending on application volume, the complexity of the applicant's profile, and ICA's current workload. Some straightforward applications are processed in four to six months; complex cases can take longer. Consultants cannot accelerate ICA's internal review timeline.

### What is the success rate for PR applications in Singapore?

ICA does not publish PR approval rates. Based on industry estimates, approval rates vary significantly by applicant profile, nationality, industry, income level, family ties to Singapore, and length of residency. Applicants with strong economic contributions, Singapore-citizen family members, and children enrolled in Singapore schools have materially higher approval odds. Professional assistance consistently improves outcomes for borderline cases by ensuring the application presents the strongest possible case.

### Can an immigration consultant guarantee PR approval?

No. ICA approval decisions are made entirely by the Singapore government, and no consultant has any ability to influence this decision. Any consultant who guarantees PR approval or implies they have special access to ICA is misrepresenting their services. Legitimate consultants can improve your application quality significantly but cannot guarantee outcomes.

### How much do immigration consultants charge in Singapore?

PR application fees typically range from SGD 1,500 to 5,000 depending on case complexity and consultant tier. Employment Pass application fees typically range from SGD 500 to 2,500. ### What documents are needed for an Employment Pass application?

An EP application typically requires a completed Form 8 (or online equivalent), the applicant's passport and educational certificates, the employer's business profile and financial statements, a signed employment contract, and in some cases a detailed explanation of why a Singapore citizen or PR could not fill the role. Requirements vary depending on the employer's industry and the applicant's nationality. An immigration consultant will compile and quality-check all documents before submission.

### Can my family come to Singapore on a Dependant Pass?

Employment Pass holders can apply for Dependant Passes for their spouse and children under 21. S Pass holders earning SGD 6,000 or more may also apply for a Dependant Pass. Parents and in-laws may be eligible for a Long-Term Visit Pass. Each family member's application is assessed separately. A professional consultant can manage all family member applications in a coordinated engagement.

### What is the naturalisation process for Singapore citizenship?

Singapore Permanent Residents are eligible to apply for citizenship after residing in Singapore for approximately two years as a PR. The citizenship application requires evidence of integration into Singapore society, including community involvement, children enrolled in Singapore schools, and economic contribution. Naturalisation applications are assessed holistically by ICA, and there is no fixed eligibility threshold that guarantees approval. Spouses of Singapore citizens may also apply after two years of PR status.

### How often can I reapply if my PR application is rejected?

There is no formal restriction on how many times you can reapply for PR in Singapore. However, reapplying too soon after rejection with an unchanged profile is unlikely to produce a different outcome. The prudent approach is to wait at least 12 to 18 months, use the time to strengthen your profile (additional years of contribution, community engagement, children in Singapore schools), and engage a professional consultant to build a materially different application before resubmitting.
