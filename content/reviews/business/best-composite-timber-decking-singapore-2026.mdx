## What to Look for When Hiring a Composite Timber Decking Contractor in Singapore

**Material specification and WPC brand transparency.** The most important question to ask any composite decking contractor is which brand and profile of WPC board they are using and what the technical specifications are. Not all composite boards perform equally in Singapore's tropical climate. Look for boards with UV-stabilised pigments, drainage channels, and moisture resistance ratings appropriate for permanent outdoor use. A contractor who cannot or will not specify the board brand is a significant risk: unbranded or low-grade boards delaminate and fade within 2 to 3 years.

**Subframe construction quality.** Composite boards are only as durable as the subframe supporting them. Aluminium or galvanised steel subframes outperform timber joists in Singapore's humidity, resisting rot and insect damage that can cause structural failure in timber-framed decks. Ask whether the subframe is timber or metal, and confirm that the joist spacing matches the board manufacturer's recommendation. Boards spanned beyond their rated spacing flex and crack under foot traffic.

**Google reviews and project references.** A 4.5-star rating with 30 or more reviews is the credibility baseline for composite decking contractors in Singapore. More informative than the aggregate score are reviews that mention specific project types, materials used, and whether the final installation matched expectations. Ask the contractor for direct client references from projects completed in the past 12 months.

**Warranty on materials and workmanship.** Reputable composite board brands carry 10 to 25-year material warranties against fading, staining, and structural failure. Ask for the product warranty document before purchasing. Workmanship warranties of 1 to 2 years are standard in Singapore; anything shorter signals low confidence in installation quality. Confirm who is responsible for warranty claims: the contractor, the board distributor, or both.

**Structural capability for complex sites.** Standard balcony and patio decking is within the scope of most contractors on this list. For projects involving level changes, cantilever sections, integration with drainage systems, or installation above waterproofed surfaces, structural competence matters significantly. Contractors with engineering or construction backgrounds, such as SinHao, add value that pure-play decking suppliers cannot match on structurally complex sites.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does composite timber decking cost in Singapore?

Composite timber decking in Singapore typically costs SGD 80 to SGD 180 per square metre for supply and installation, including a standard subframe. A 20 sqm balcony deck runs approximately SGD 1,600 to SGD 3,600. Price varies with board brand, profile (hollow vs solid), subframe material (timber vs aluminium), and site access. Premium branded boards with aluminium subframes sit at the upper end. Budget firmly for the full installed cost, not the board price alone.

### What is composite timber decking and how does it differ from real timber?

Composite timber decking is manufactured from a combination of wood fibre and plastic polymer, typically PVC or polyethylene, compressed into boards that mimic the appearance of natural timber. Unlike natural timber, composite boards do not require annual oiling or sealing, are resistant to rot, insects, and moisture, and maintain their colour more consistently under UV exposure. In Singapore's climate, composite decking outperforms natural timber on durability and maintenance requirements for outdoor applications.

### How long does composite timber decking last in Singapore?

Quality composite decking boards from established brands carry 10 to 25-year warranties against structural failure and significant colour fade. In practice, boards installed with correct subframe spacing and adequate drainage on Singapore installations typically last 15 to 20 years with minimal maintenance beyond occasional cleaning. Boards from unspecified manufacturers may show delamination and colour loss within 3 to 5 years. Always request the product warranty document and manufacturer's name before committing.

### What subframe material should I choose for composite decking in Singapore?

Aluminium subframes are the preferred choice for permanent outdoor composite decking in Singapore. Aluminium does not rust, rot, or attract termites, and retains structural stability through Singapore's humidity and rain cycles. Galvanised steel is an acceptable alternative with slightly lower longevity. Timber joists are lower cost but more vulnerable to rot and insect damage in Singapore's climate, particularly in poorly ventilated under-deck spaces. For balcony or terrace decking expected to last more than 10 years, aluminium subframes are worth the additional cost.

### Do I need approval for composite timber decking in Singapore?

For HDB flats, decking works are generally treated as minor renovation and do not require HDB approval provided they do not involve structural changes, do not encroach on common areas, and do not exceed permitted loads. For condos, MCST approval is typically required before works commence; check with your managing agent first. For landed properties, composite decking at ground level generally does not require planning permission, but any raised structure above 1.2m may require a structural engineer's input. Confirm with your contractor before proceeding.

### How do I maintain composite timber decking in Singapore?

Composite decking requires significantly less maintenance than natural timber. Routine cleaning with water and a mild detergent using a soft brush or mop removes dirt, algae, and mould that accumulate in Singapore's humidity. Avoid harsh chemical cleaners that degrade the board surface coating. For boards with drainage grooves, ensure channels remain clear of debris to prevent standing water. No oiling, sealing, or sanding is required. Annual inspection of subframe fixings and board ends is recommended to catch any settlement or movement early.

### How far in advance should I book a composite timber decking contractor in Singapore?

For well-reviewed contractors with established project pipelines, book 4 to 6 weeks in advance for standard residential projects. During peak renovation season, typically January to April when BTO keys are collected and school holiday renovation projects are scheduled, the best firms fill their slots 6 to 8 weeks out. Complex projects with structural or design elements should be scoped and booked at least 8 weeks ahead to allow time for design approval and material procurement.

### What questions should I ask a composite decking contractor before signing?

Ask: Which composite board brand and profile will you use, and can I have the product data sheet? Is the subframe timber or aluminium? What is the board manufacturer's warranty and who do I contact for claims? What workmanship warranty do you provide? How will the deck drain and how is waterproofing protected at the perimeter? Who will manage the project on site and how often will they be present? Can you provide a reference from a project completed in the past 6 months?

### Are composite decking contractors in Singapore licensed?

No specific licence is required to supply and install composite timber decking in Singapore. Contractors working on HDB properties must hold HDB Renovation Contractor (RC) registration. Contractors performing structural works must engage a Professional Engineer (PE) to endorse structural drawings. MCST-accredited contractors are approved to work in managed properties including most condominiums. When hiring for HDB or condo projects, verify the contractor holds the appropriate registration before works commence.

### What are red flags when hiring a composite decking contractor?

Red flags include: inability to specify the composite board brand or provide a product data sheet, quotes presented as a single lump sum with no subframe or material breakdown, significantly below-market pricing that cannot be explained by board specification, no verifiable physical business address, no examples of completed composite decking projects, reluctance to provide client references from recent projects, and verbal agreements rather than written contracts specifying materials, subframe type, warranty, and payment schedule.
