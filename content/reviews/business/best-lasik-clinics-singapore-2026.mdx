The best LASIK clinics in Singapore combine cutting-edge laser technology with experienced ophthalmologists, transparent patient education, and a rigorous pre-operative screening process that determines whether you are a suitable candidate. Singapore's LASIK industry is among the most sophisticated in the region: clinics compete on technology investment (SMILE Pro, Z-LASIK, iLASIK), surgeon experience, and the breadth of refractive procedures offered beyond standard LASIK: including LASEK, PRK, and implantable collamer lenses (ICL) for patients who are not ideal LASIK candidates. With procedure costs ranging from SGD 3,000 to over SGD 5,000 per eye, and outcomes that are essentially irreversible, choosing the right clinic is one of the most consequential healthcare decisions a patient will make. We evaluated Singapore's leading eye clinics using Google Maps patient review data, surgeon credentials, technology investment, and clinical transparency to rank the 10 best LASIK providers in 2026.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Atlas Eye Specialist Centre (Paragon), 1,314 Google reviews at 4.9 stars, the highest-volume reviewed LASIK clinic in Singapore with a consistently excellent patient experience
- **Best for volume and value:** Eagle Eye Centre (multiple locations), over 800 reviews per location at 4.8 stars, offering a full suite of refractive procedures with an accessible network of clinics
- **Best for surgical precision:** Clearvision Eye Clinic, 632 reviews at 4.9 stars, a boutique specialist clinic recognised for complex refractive cases and personalised surgical planning
- Indicative LASIK pricing in Singapore: standard LASIK from SGD 3,000–3,800 per eye; bladeless LASIK from SGD 3,500–4,500 per eye; SMILE Pro from SGD 4,500–5,500 per eye
- Singapore's ophthalmologists must be SMC-registered specialists; always verify the surgeon's credentials and confirm they personally perform your pre-operative assessment
- Key screening criteria: corneal thickness (minimum ~500 microns), stable prescription for at least 12 months, minimum corneal curvature requirements: a reputable clinic will screen you rigorously before recommending surgery

## How We Ranked These LASIK Clinics

We ranked LASIK clinics using Google Maps patient review data as the primary signal, requiring a minimum rating of 4.6 stars and a minimum of 15 verified reviews to qualify. We weighted review count heavily alongside rating: a clinic with 1,000 reviews at 4.8 stars provides stronger statistical confidence than a clinic with 20 reviews at 5.0 stars. Secondary criteria included: surgeon SMC registration and subspecialty certification (ophthalmology), technology investment (SMILE Pro, bladeless wavefront-guided platforms), breadth of refractive procedures offered, quality and thoroughness of pre-operative screening (assessed through patient review commentary), and patient outcomes as described in verified reviews. Clinics that patients reported pressuring them into procedures they were borderline candidates for were penalised, as were clinics with unresolved regulatory issues. All 10 shortlisted clinics are MOH-licensed and staffed by SMC-registered ophthalmologists.

## What to Look for When Choosing a LASIK Clinic

**Surgeon credentials and subspecialty training.** Ensure the operating surgeon is an SMC-registered ophthalmologist with specific subspecialty certification in cornea and refractive surgery. Ask directly whether the surgeon who conducts your pre-operative assessment is the same surgeon who will perform your procedure: in some clinics, these roles are separated.

**Technology investment and procedure options.** The best clinics offer multiple refractive platforms (LASIK, SMILE Pro, ICL) because different patients are best served by different procedures. A clinic that only offers one type of surgery may not be giving you the most appropriate recommendation for your corneal profile.

**Rigour of pre-operative screening.** A reputable clinic will screen you with corneal topography and pachymetry at minimum, and will tell you clearly if you are not a suitable candidate. Be cautious of clinics that seem eager to proceed without a thorough screening process.

**Post-operative care protocol.** Understand what follow-up appointments are included, how accessible the surgeon is if complications arise, and what the protocol is for managing common post-operative symptoms such as dry eyes or halos. Complications are rare but do occur, and responsive post-operative support matters.

**Patient review authenticity.** A clinic with 500 or more reviews at 4.8 stars provides much stronger confidence than one with 20 reviews at 5.0 stars. Read recent reviews carefully, paying attention to comments about complications and how they were managed: not just the positive outcomes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does LASIK cost in Singapore in 2026?

Standard LASIK in Singapore typically costs SGD 3,000 to 3,800 per eye at private clinics. Bladeless (femtosecond laser) LASIK ranges from SGD 3,500 to 4,500 per eye, and SMILE Pro: the most advanced keyhole refractive procedure: costs SGD 4,500 to 5,500 per eye. Total procedure cost for both eyes is therefore approximately SGD 6,000 to 11,000 depending on the technology chosen. Public sector LASIK at TTSH is available at subsidised rates for eligible Singaporeans and PRs.

### Am I a suitable candidate for LASIK?

Most patients are suitable if they have a stable prescription (unchanged for at least 12 months), are aged 21 or above, have sufficient corneal thickness (typically 500 microns or more), and do not have conditions such as keratoconus, severe dry eyes, or certain autoimmune diseases. The only way to confirm suitability is a comprehensive pre-operative assessment including corneal topography and pachymetry. All clinics on this list provide this assessment before recommending surgery.

### What is the difference between LASIK and SMILE Pro?

LASIK involves cutting a corneal flap using a laser (or microkeratome), reshaping the corneal tissue beneath it, then replacing the flap. SMILE Pro is a more recent flapless procedure that extracts a lenticule from within the cornea through a small incision, requiring no flap and causing less disruption to the corneal surface. SMILE Pro is associated with lower dry eye risk post-surgery and may be more suitable for patients with active lifestyles or dry eye predisposition. Not all patients are candidates for SMILE Pro; your surgeon will advise.

### How long does recovery take after LASIK?

Most patients achieve functional vision within 24–48 hours of LASIK. Vision continues to stabilise over 1–3 months, with the final corrected vision typically achieved by three months post-operation. Some patients experience temporary side effects including dry eyes, halos around lights, and glare: particularly at night: that typically resolve within 3–6 months. SMILE Pro patients often report a slightly faster initial recovery with fewer dry eye symptoms.

### Can LASIK be repeated if my vision changes?

A LASIK enhancement is possible in some cases if the cornea has sufficient residual thickness to safely remove additional tissue. Enhancement rates with modern laser platforms are low (typically 1–5%), and clinics generally include an enhancement provision in their fee structure for a period of 12 months post-surgery. Your surgeon will assess whether your cornea can safely accommodate an enhancement during follow-up evaluations.

### Is LASIK permanent?

LASIK permanently changes the shape of your cornea, and the correction itself does not reverse. However, as part of natural ageing, some patients develop presbyopia (age-related near vision loss) or, later in life, cataracts: conditions unrelated to the LASIK procedure. Most LASIK patients enjoy excellent distance vision for 10 years or more before any age-related vision changes become significant.

### What is an ICL and when is it recommended instead of LASIK?

An implantable collamer lens (ICL) is a procedure where a thin lens is surgically placed inside the eye rather than reshaping the cornea with a laser. ICL is typically recommended for patients with prescriptions too high for LASIK, thin corneas, or significant dry eye disease. ICL is reversible (the lens can be removed), while LASIK is permanent. Costs for ICL in Singapore typically range from SGD 8,000 to 12,000 for both eyes.

### How do I choose between LASIK clinics in Singapore?

The most reliable signals are: verified Google review count (higher is more statistically meaningful than a perfect score on few reviews), surgeon qualifications, technology offered (clinics with SMILE Pro capability are generally more current), and how thorough the clinic is in telling patients they are NOT suitable candidates. A clinic that screens rigorously and turns away borderline candidates demonstrates clinical integrity. Compare quotes from at least two clinics and ask each surgeon to explain specifically why they recommend a particular procedure for your corneal profile.
