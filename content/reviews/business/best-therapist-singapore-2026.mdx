## What to Look for When Hiring a Therapist or Counsellor in Singapore

**Professional registration and credentials.** The baseline for any therapist or counsellor in Singapore is registration with either the Singapore Association for Counselling (SAC) or the Singapore Psychological Society (SPS). For clinical psychologists, look for registration with the Singapore Register of Psychologists (SRP). Any reputable practice will list their therapists' credentials on the website. If credentials are absent or vague, ask directly before booking.

**Specialisation match.** A therapist who specialises in trauma and PTSD is not automatically the best choice for relationship counselling, and vice versa. Match the practice's stated specialisations to your presenting issue. Most practices list the conditions and life areas they work with on their website. If yours is not listed, ask at the initial enquiry whether the therapist has experience with it.

**Therapeutic approach.** CBT (Cognitive Behavioural Therapy), ACT (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy), DBT, and person-centred counselling are the most common modalities in Singapore. These are not interchangeable. Briefly researching which approach is most evidence-supported for your issue before enquiring will help you ask better questions and assess whether the practice is a good fit.

**Fees and session structure.** Private therapy in Singapore typically costs SGD 120 to SGD 300 per 50-minute session. Some practices offer sliding-scale fees for lower-income clients, ask when enquiring. Confirm session length, cancellation policy, and whether the first session is an assessment or a full therapy session, this varies significantly between practices.

**Response time to initial enquiry.** A therapist who takes more than 48 hours to respond to a first enquiry is giving you a signal about how they will manage communication when you are in distress between sessions. Under 24 hours is the standard to expect from a professional practice. This applies whether you contact via phone, email, or website form.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does therapy cost in Singapore?

Private therapy in Singapore typically ranges from SGD 120 to SGD 300 per 50-minute session depending on the therapist's qualifications, location, and specialisation. Clinical psychologists generally charge more than counsellors. Subsidised rates are available at some practices for clients who qualify; ask when making your initial enquiry. Government polyclinics also offer referrals to subsidised mental health services at IMH and restructured hospitals.

### What is the difference between a therapist, counsellor, and psychologist in Singapore?

A counsellor typically holds a graduate diploma or master's degree in counselling and is registered with the Singapore Association for Counselling (SAC). A psychologist holds a master's or doctoral degree in psychology and may be registered with the Singapore Register of Psychologists (SRP). A therapist is a broader term covering both. Psychiatrists are medical doctors who can prescribe medication; psychologists and counsellors cannot. For medication assessment alongside therapy, a referral to a psychiatrist is needed.

### How do I choose the right therapist in Singapore?

Start by identifying your primary presenting issue: anxiety, depression, relationship difficulties, trauma, grief, or something else. Search for practices that list your concern as a specialisation. Check the therapist's credentials and read recent Google reviews. If possible, book a first session with two or three therapists and assess which relationship feels most productive. Therapeutic fit matters more than proximity or price.

### How many sessions of therapy will I need?

Mild anxiety or adjustment difficulties typically resolve in 4 to 8 sessions of CBT or structured counselling. Moderate depression or relationship issues often require 12 to 20 sessions. Complex trauma, personality disorders, or long-standing issues may require ongoing therapy over 1 to 2 years. A good therapist will outline an initial treatment plan after the first or second session, including a realistic timeline.

### Are therapists in Singapore regulated?

There is no mandatory government licensing for therapists and counsellors in Singapore, unlike medical doctors. Professional registration with SAC or SPS is voluntary but recognised as the standard of professional conduct. Clinical psychologists can register with the SRP. The absence of mandatory licensing means the onus is on clients to verify credentials before engaging a therapist.

### What should I ask a therapist before booking?

Ask: What are your qualifications and professional registration? What therapeutic modalities do you use? Have you worked with clients presenting my specific issue before? What does a typical session involve? What is your cancellation policy? How do you handle emergencies or crises between sessions? A professional therapist will answer all of these questions without hesitation.

### Can I get online therapy in Singapore?

Yes. Talk Your Heart Out (TYHO) offers online sessions alongside face-to-face appointments. Several other practices on this list also accommodate online sessions by request. Online therapy is fully effective for most presenting issues except those requiring in-person assessment or crisis intervention. Confirm with the practice whether online sessions are available before booking.

### What are red flags when choosing a therapist in Singapore?

No professional registration or credentials listed. Guarantees of outcomes within a fixed number of sessions. Fees significantly below market rate with no explanation. Dual relationships (the therapist is also a friend, colleague, or employer). Pressure to commit to a long package upfront. A therapist who shares details of other clients. Any of these should prompt you to look elsewhere.

### How far in advance should I book a therapist in Singapore?

Popular practices with small teams often have waitlists of 2 to 4 weeks. If you are in distress and need to be seen quickly, mention this when enquiring and ask whether any therapist has a cancellation slot. Larger practices like Singapore Counselling Centre typically have shorter wait times given their therapist pool. For immediate crisis support, contact IMH's 24-hour helpline at 6389 2222 or Samaritans of Singapore at 1767.

### Does insurance cover therapy costs in Singapore?

Most integrated shield plans and employer group insurance policies in Singapore do not cover outpatient psychological counselling unless it is provided by a psychiatrist or within a hospital setting. Some employers offer Employee Assistance Programmes (EAPs) that include a fixed number of free counselling sessions per year. Check your policy terms or speak to your HR department before assuming coverage.

### Is therapy in Singapore confidential?

Yes. All registered therapists and counsellors in Singapore are bound by professional ethical codes requiring confidentiality. Exceptions exist for mandated reporting situations: imminent risk of harm to self or others, child abuse, and certain court orders. A reputable therapist will explain the limits of confidentiality clearly in the first session.
