## What to Look for When Hiring a Confinement Nanny Agency in Singapore

**Nanny vetting and training standards.** The quality of a confinement nanny depends almost entirely on the agency's vetting and training programme. Ask agencies how nannies are trained (formal certification, internal training, or experience-only), what background checks are conducted, and how long the average nanny on their roster has been placing successfully. Agencies with structured training programmes consistently produce better outcomes than those who rely solely on years of informal experience.

**Replacement nanny policy.** Even carefully matched nannies occasionally do not mesh well with a particular family. Reputable agencies have a clear, written replacement policy: they will replace a nanny if the fit is genuinely poor within a specified period, without additional cost. Ask about the replacement process, timeline, and any conditions before signing an agreement. An agency that cannot articulate its replacement policy clearly is a red flag.

**Booking lead time and availability confirmation.** Popular confinement nanny agencies in Singapore are booked 4 to 6 months in advance. When engaging an agency, confirm that a nanny is actually available for your expected due date window, not merely added to a waiting list. Get this availability confirmation in writing. Due dates change: confirm the agency's policy on flexibility if your delivery is earlier or later than expected.

**Language and dietary compatibility.** Confinement nannies prepare meals and communicate with the mother daily for 28 to 30 days. Language compatibility is important: if the mother does not speak Mandarin or Cantonese, a nanny who communicates primarily in these languages may create friction. Confirm the nanny's language capability and whether it matches the family's primary communication language. For Muslim families or families with specific dietary requirements, confirm the nanny's dietary knowledge and Halal certification status.

**Service scope: what is included.** Confinement nanny scope varies between agencies and individual nannies. Core services include newborn care (bathing, feeding, umbilical care), postpartum meal preparation (3 meals and snacks daily), and overnight baby care. Some nannies also provide breastfeeding support and light household cleaning. Confirm the exact service scope with the agency before confirming and avoid assuming services are included that have not been explicitly confirmed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a confinement nanny cost in Singapore?

Confinement nanny fees in Singapore typically range from SGD 2,800 to SGD 4,500 for a standard 28-day engagement. The agency fee is usually separate from the nanny's fee. Total cost depends on the agency, the nanny's experience level, and the scope of services agreed. Some agencies charge an all-inclusive rate while others quote the nanny fee and agency fee separately.

### When should I book a confinement nanny in Singapore?

Book as early as possible: ideally at 3 to 4 months pregnant. Popular agencies are fully booked for certain due date windows 4 to 5 months in advance. Last-minute availability is possible through specialists like NannySOS but options are more limited. Book early for the best choice of nanny experience level and language match.

### What does a confinement nanny do in Singapore?

A confinement nanny's core responsibilities include: bathing and feeding the newborn, preparing postpartum confinement meals (typically 3 meals and snacks daily for the mother), providing overnight baby care to allow the mother to rest, breastfeeding support and guidance, and umbilical cord care. Some nannies also assist with light household cleaning and older sibling care.

### How long does confinement last in Singapore?

The traditional Chinese confinement period is 30 days. Most Singaporean Chinese families engage a confinement nanny for 28 days, starting from the day of hospital discharge. Some families extend to 44 days for a more extended recovery period. Muslim confinement (Pantang) typically also lasts 44 days following Islamic traditions.

### Do I need to provide accommodation for my confinement nanny?

Most confinement nannies in Singapore are live-in during the engagement period, requiring a bed (not necessarily a separate room: a mattress in the baby's room is common) and basic meal provision. The nanny typically works overnight, handles baby feeding, and rests when the baby rests. Some agencies offer a live-out option where the nanny leaves in the evening, but this reduces overnight newborn care support.

### What confinement food should my nanny prepare?

Traditional Chinese confinement food focuses on warming and replenishing ingredients: sesame oil chicken, pig's trotters in black vinegar and ginger, herbal soups, and rice wine-based dishes. Modern confinement practices may adjust these based on individual health needs and lactation support priorities. Confirm the specific meal plan with your nanny and agency before the engagement begins, and communicate any dietary restrictions or preferences clearly.

### Can I hire a confinement nanny if I plan to bottle-feed rather than breastfeed?

Yes. Confinement nannies serve both breastfeeding and formula-feeding families. If you plan to formula-feed, the nanny will handle bottle preparation, sterilisation, and feeding schedule management. If you plan to breastfeed, confirm with the agency that the nanny has specific breastfeeding support training, including proper latch guidance and breast engorgement management: this varies significantly between nannies.

### What should I prepare at home before the confinement nanny arrives?

Prepare: a sleeping space for the nanny (mattress or bed near the baby), access to the kitchen for meal preparation, a list of any food allergies or dietary restrictions, key baby supplies (diapers, wipes, breast pump if breastfeeding), and a clear communication plan for house rules. Discuss overnight baby care expectations, preferred feeding times, and the mother's recovery priorities in the first day.

Yes. Many families extend their confinement nanny engagement for an additional 1 to 2 weeks, particularly first-time parents or mothers recovering from caesarean delivery. Extensions are subject to the nanny's availability and the agency's scheduling. Confirm extension terms and pricing with the agency at the start of the engagement rather than at the end of the 28-day period when availability may be limited.

### What is the difference between a confinement nanny and a maternity nurse?

A confinement nanny follows traditional Chinese postnatal care practices, including confinement diet preparation, Chinese herbal remedies, and traditional care rituals. A maternity nurse (common in Western countries) focuses on newborn care using modern paediatric guidelines and typically does not prepare confinement meals. In Singapore, "confinement nanny" is the standard term and covers both traditional and modern-trained practitioners.
