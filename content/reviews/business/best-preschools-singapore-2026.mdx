Singapore's preschool sector is regulated by the Early Childhood Development Agency (ECDA), which sets standards for curriculum frameworks, teacher qualifications, and centre safety. The Nurturing Early Learners (NEL) framework and the Singapore Preschool Curriculum Framework guide quality benchmarks across all licensed centres. We evaluated preschools across the island by assessing Google ratings, review volume, SPARK certification status (the ECDA quality mark), teacher-to-child ratios, bilingual education quality, facilities, and fee transparency. This list covers full-day childcare centres, kindergartens, and integrated preschool programmes serving children aged 18 months to 6 years.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Adventure Tree Preschool, a boutique nature-based operator with a 5.0-star rating at its Bukit Timah campus, purpose-built outdoor learning environments, and a curriculum built on forest school principles and inquiry-based learning that no comparable Singapore preschool matches
- **Best for affordability:** PCF Sparkletots, operating over 350 centres island-wide under the PAP Community Foundation with government-subsidised fee structures accessible to most families
- **Best for working parents:** My First Skool (NTUC First Campus), with 150+ centres, infant care from 2 months, 5.0-star rating at its Ang Mo Kio campus across 54 Google reviews, and extended operating hours designed around dual-income households
- ECDA's SPARK certification is the clearest quality indicator for Singapore preschools — centres assessed as "Achieving" or "Sustaining" have met rigorous standards on teaching quality, learning environment, and leadership
- Full-day childcare fees range from SGD 700–2,500 per month before government subsidies; families earning under SGD 12,000 monthly qualify for means-tested KidSTART or CDA-eligible subsidies
- Red flag: preschools unable to confirm their ECDA licence number and SPARK status should be verified against the ECDA's online public register before enrolment

## What to Look for When Choosing a Preschool in Singapore

**ECDA licence and SPARK certification.** Every legitimate preschool in Singapore must hold a valid ECDA licence, verifiable on the ECDA public register. SPARK certification — awarded at "Achieving" or "Sustaining" levels — indicates that the centre has been independently assessed against rigorous quality standards covering curriculum, teaching practices, physical environment, and leadership. Enrolment at a SPARK-certified centre provides greater assurance than enrolment at a merely licensed but unassessed one.

**Curriculum philosophy alignment.** Singapore preschools range from play-based and child-led programmes to structured academic preparation models. Neither is universally superior — what matters is how well the philosophy aligns with your child's temperament and your family's values. Visit the centre while it is operating and observe whether the daily environment matches the promotional materials before committing to enrolment.

**Bilingual programme depth.** Most Singapore families prioritise strong Mandarin development alongside English, and the quality of Mother Tongue instruction varies dramatically between operators. Ask specifically about the qualifications of the Mandarin teachers, the daily instructional time in Mandarin, and whether the programme is delivered by dedicated Mother Tongue specialists or as a secondary responsibility of generalist teachers.

**Teacher qualifications and turnover.** The quality of early childhood education is primarily determined by the practitioners delivering it. Ask prospective preschools about the proportion of their teaching staff holding early childhood education degrees versus diplomas, the average tenure of teachers at that specific centre, and the operator's approach to professional development. High staff turnover is one of the strongest negative predictors of preschool quality.

**Class size and teacher-to-child ratios.** ECDA mandates minimum ratios, but the best preschools maintain ratios that enable significantly more individualised teacher attention than the statutory minimum. Smaller classes allow teachers to observe children closely, adapt their teaching to individual needs, and build the genuine relationships that make early childhood education most effective.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the right age to start preschool in Singapore?

Most Singapore children begin formal preschool at Nursery 1 (N1), typically at age 3. Infant care and toddler programmes are available from as young as 2 months and 18 months respectively for working families needing full-day care. ECDA's research recommends that early childhood education should begin by age 3 at the latest, with earlier high-quality care beneficial for language development, socialisation, and school readiness.

### How much does preschool cost in Singapore?

Full-day childcare fees range from approximately SGD 700 per month at subsidised community operators such as PCF Sparkletots to SGD 2,500 or more per month at premium private preschools. Government subsidies via the Child Development Account, Preschool Housing Support, and income-tested means-tested subsidies can reduce fees materially for qualifying families. Families earning under SGD 12,000 monthly should assess their subsidy eligibility through the LifeSG portal before comparing net fees.

### What is the difference between a childcare centre and a kindergarten in Singapore?

A childcare centre provides full-day care (typically 7am–7pm) for children from infancy through to age 6, combining supervision with early childhood education. A kindergarten is a half-day educational programme for children aged 4–6 (K1 and K2), focused on school readiness. Most working families require full-day childcare rather than kindergarten, while families with a stay-at-home parent may opt for a half-day kindergarten supplemented by home care.

### What is ECDA and why does it matter for preschool selection?

The Early Childhood Development Agency (ECDA) is the statutory board jointly overseen by the Ministry of Social and Family Development and the Ministry of Education that regulates and develops Singapore's early childhood sector. ECDA licences all preschool operators, sets curriculum frameworks, mandates teacher qualification standards, and conducts centre inspections. Choosing an ECDA-licensed centre is mandatory for legal compliance; choosing a SPARK-certified centre provides additional assurance of quality.

### How important is bilingual education in Singapore preschool?

Bilingual education is important in the Singapore context because children begin formal assessed Mother Tongue instruction at Primary 1, and a strong oral and early literacy foundation in Mandarin (for most families), Tamil, or Malay at the preschool stage makes the P1 transition significantly easier. Research on early bilingualism also suggests cognitive benefits from simultaneous dual-language development. Parents who are not themselves proficient in their child's Mother Tongue have particular reason to prioritise preschools with strong and specialist Mother Tongue programmes.

### What should I ask during a preschool visit?

Ask about ECDA licence and SPARK status, the qualifications and tenure of the specific teachers who will teach your child's age group, the daily schedule and ratio of structured to free-play time, the approach to bilingual instruction, how the school communicates with parents about their child's progress, and the procedure for handling incidents and illnesses. Also observe the physical environment — clean, organised, richly resourced spaces with purposeful materials signal consistent programme quality.

### How do I apply for preschool subsidies in Singapore?

Government subsidies for preschool in Singapore are administered through the Child Care Subsidy scheme for full-day childcare. Eligible families must apply through their preschool operator, who submits to ECDA on their behalf. Subsidy amounts are income-tested, with families earning under SGD 3,000 monthly receiving the highest subsidy tiers. Additional support is available through the KidSTART programme for families in the lowest income quartile. The LifeSG app and ECDA's subsidy calculator provide family-specific estimates before enrolment.

### Is it worth paying more for a premium preschool?

Premium preschools typically offer lower teacher-to-child ratios, higher-credentialled teaching staff, richer physical environments, and more specialised curriculum programmes than mid-market operators. Whether the additional cost is justified depends on the specific programme quality and your child's needs. The highest-value investments are in operators — whether premium or mid-market — who maintain high staff tenure, genuine curriculum rigour, and transparent parent communication. Brand prestige without these fundamentals does not justify a premium fee.
