Composite timber flooring in Singapore has moved well beyond the backyard deck. Homeowners now use wood plastic composite (WPC) boards to resurface tired balcony tiles, create a continuous timber look from the living room out to the terrace, and finish rooftop and poolside floors that would rot within a few monsoon seasons if they were natural wood. This guide is for HDB, condo, and landed homeowners who want a timber-look floor that survives sun, rain, and humidity without annual oiling, and who need to know which firms can install the right flooring system. We wrote it because "composite timber flooring" is quoted very differently from one contractor to the next: some sell hollow WPC deck boards, some sell rigid SPC planks for indoor use, and some offer clip-on tiles that sit over existing balcony tiles. At BestThingReview.com, we evaluated 44 Singapore businesses listed under composite timber flooring on Google Maps, checked each firm's published product range, and shortlisted the 10 that combine verified review strength with a genuine composite flooring offering for indoor, outdoor, and indoor-outdoor floors.

## How We Ranked These Composite Timber Flooring Specialists

We started with 44 Singapore businesses returned for composite timber flooring searches on Google Maps and applied hard thresholds before scoring anything. Every business on this list needed a Google rating of at least 4.5 stars, at least 40 verified Google reviews for non-featured firms, and a verifiable Singapore address. We then checked each firm's own website to confirm it actually sells or installs composite flooring (WPC boards, co-extruded composite, or clip-on composite tiles), not just indoor vinyl. Firms were excluded if they had no rating on record, a rating below 4.5, a location outside Singapore, or no composite product on their published range. Remaining firms were scored on rating, review volume, breadth of indoor and outdoor systems, subframe and overlay options, and how clearly they distinguish WPC from SPC. ## What to Look for When Choosing Composite Timber Flooring in Singapore

**Confirm WPC or SPC in writing.** "Composite flooring" can mean wood plastic composite (WPC) for outdoor use or stone plastic composite (SPC) rigid planks for indoor use. They are not interchangeable. SPC is an indoor click-lock plank that should not face rain and direct sun; WPC is built for exterior conditions. Ask the contractor to state the product type, brand, and board profile on the quotation.

**Check the board construction.** Capped or co-extruded boards, where a polymer shell surrounds the composite core, resist staining and fading better than uncapped boards. Tulou and XingFloors both publish this detail. Solid boards feel firmer than hollow ones and handle heavier planters.

**Ask how the floor meets your existing surface.** On balconies, the two common methods are clip-on tiles laid directly on existing tiles, and boards fixed to an aluminium frame or adjustable pedestals sitting above the tiles. Both avoid hacking. Ask how the floor trap is handled and whether the sliding door still clears the new surface.

**Insist on drainage and ventilation.** Composite boards need gaps and airflow underneath. A floor laid tight onto a slab that ponds water will trap moisture and grow mould, so uneven slabs need screeding or a levelled frame first.

**Compare like-for-like quotations.** A useful quote lists board brand and profile, substructure, fixing method, trims, area, removal of existing finishes, and warranties. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does composite timber flooring cost per square foot in Singapore?

Composite timber flooring in Singapore is usually quoted per square foot, supply and install. Tulou publishes a starting price of SGD 18 per square foot, with the final figure depending on specification and substructure. Expect higher rates for capped or co-extruded boards, solid profiles, aluminium frames, and levelled platforms. Clip-on composite tiles over existing balcony tiles are generally the lowest-cost route because they need no frame. Confirm whether removal of old flooring and edge trims are included.

### What is the difference between composite timber flooring and real timber flooring?

Composite timber flooring is made from wood fibres bound with polymers, so it looks like timber but does not absorb water, rot, or attract termites the way natural wood can. Real timber such as Merbau, Chengal, or teak can be refinished, but outdoors in Singapore it needs regular oiling and greys in the sun. Many homeowners use real timber indoors and tone-matched composite outdoors.

### What is the difference between WPC and SPC flooring?

WPC (wood plastic composite) combines wood fibre and plastic and is used mainly for outdoor deck boards and balcony flooring on a frame or clip system. SPC (stone plastic composite) combines stone powder, PVC, and stabilisers into a rigid, waterproof click-lock plank for indoor floors, often with a printed timber-look surface. SPC suits kitchens and living rooms; WPC suits balconies, terraces, and poolside floors.

### Can composite timber flooring be installed over existing balcony tiles?

Yes, in most cases. The two common methods are interlocking clip-on composite tiles laid directly on the existing tiles, and composite boards fixed to an aluminium frame or adjustable pedestals that sit on the tiles. Both avoid hacking and keep the waterproofing membrane intact. The existing tiles should be sound and the floor must still drain freely to the floor trap.

### Do I need HDB or MCST approval for composite timber flooring?

For HDB flats, new floor finishes are generally a standard renovation, but use an HDB-registered renovation contractor, avoid hacking that could affect waterproofing, and follow HDB's renovation guidelines on the HDB website. For condos, most MCSTs require a renovation application, a deposit, and contractor details before work starts, and some restrict drilling on balconies. Always confirm with your managing agent first.

### Does composite timber flooring get hot or fade in the Singapore sun?

Composite flooring can get hot under direct afternoon sun, especially darker colours, so lighter tones are a better choice for west-facing balconies and rooftops. Capped and co-extruded boards hold colour better because the polymer shell protects the core. Expect slight, even colour settling in the first months, not patchy fading.

### How long does composite timber flooring last in Singapore?

A good-quality composite floor installed on a ventilated, well-drained frame typically lasts well over a decade, and premium capped boards are often sold with long manufacturer warranties. Poor drainage, missing expansion gaps, and rotting timber joists cause most early failures. Get both material and workmanship warranties in writing.

### How do I maintain composite timber flooring?

Sweep regularly and wash with water and a mild detergent using a soft brush. Rinse off oil spills quickly and avoid wire brushes that scratch the cap layer. Keep board gaps and the floor trap clear of leaves, and on clip-on systems lift a few tiles every few months to clean underneath. No oiling or sanding is needed.

### Can composite timber flooring be used indoors?

Outdoor WPC deck boards are not normally used for indoor living spaces. For a timber look inside, the usual choices are SPC, hybrid, engineered wood, or vinyl planks, which are designed for indoor installation over a flat subfloor. For an indoor-outdoor look, pair an indoor plank with a tone-matched outdoor composite board and a proper trim at the sliding door.
