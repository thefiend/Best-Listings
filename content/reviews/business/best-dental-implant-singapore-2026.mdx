## What to Look for When Hiring a Dental Implant Clinic

**Verified Google reviews and recency.** A 4.5-star minimum with at least 50 Google reviews is the baseline for implant clinics. More critical than the average is recency: check that reviews have been posted within the past three months. A clinic with 300 five-star reviews but nothing recent may have had a change of dentist or a drop in standards.

**Implant system disclosure.** Ask which implant system the clinic uses before booking a consultation. Reputable clinics use internationally certified brands such as Straumann, Nobel Biocare, Osstem, or Zimmer Biomet and will name the system without hesitation. Clinics that are vague about the brand are frequently using unbranded or lower-tier components.

**Specialist credentials.** Check whether the performing dentist holds a postgraduate qualification in implant dentistry, oral surgery, or periodontology, for example an MFDS (RCPS Glasgow or Edinburgh), an MDS (NUS), or a Fellowship from a recognised dental college. Singapore Dental Council's website lists registered specialists by name and specialty.

**Pricing transparency.** A credible implant clinic provides a written cost estimate covering the implant fixture, abutment, crown, any bone grafting if needed, and post-operative reviews. An all-inclusive quote prevents surprise add-ons at each visit. If a clinic refuses to provide a written breakdown before commencing treatment, look elsewhere.

**Digital imaging capability.** A CBCT (cone beam CT) scan is the clinical standard for implant treatment planning in Singapore. It provides three-dimensional bone density and anatomy data that a standard 2D X-ray cannot, reducing the risk of nerve damage or implant failure. Confirm the clinic has CBCT capability before proceeding, or that they have a referral arrangement with a centre that does.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a dental implant cost in Singapore?

A standard single-tooth implant in Singapore costs SGD 3,000–6,000 all-inclusive (fixture, abutment, and crown). CBCT scanning adds SGD 150–300 if not included. Bone grafting, if required due to insufficient bone volume, adds SGD 500–2,500 depending on the graft type and volume. Full-arch All-on-4 solutions range from SGD 20,000–40,000 per arch.

### Is dental implant treatment covered by MediShield Life or Medisave in Singapore?

MediShield Life does not cover dental implants. However, Medisave can be used to offset costs for dental surgery performed under general anaesthesia or intravenous sedation, which may apply to complex implant placements or All-on-4 procedures. Standard implant placements under local anaesthesia are not claimable under Medisave.

### How long does the dental implant process take in Singapore?

The full implant process typically takes 3–6 months from extraction or implant placement to final crown fitting. After the titanium fixture is surgically placed, a healing period of 8–16 weeks (osseointegration) is required before the crown is attached. Cases requiring bone grafting prior to implant placement extend the timeline by an additional 3–4 months.

### What is the success rate of dental implants in Singapore?

Dental implants have a documented long-term success rate of 95–98% over 10 years when placed by a qualified practitioner and properly maintained. Factors that reduce success rates include smoking, uncontrolled diabetes, poor oral hygiene, and insufficient bone volume that was not adequately addressed prior to placement.

### What should I ask a dental implant clinic before booking?

Ask: Which implant brand do you use and is it internationally certified? Does the price include the fixture, abutment, and crown? Is a CBCT scan included in the treatment plan? What is your dentist's specific implant qualification? What happens if the implant fails within the warranty period? How many implants have you placed in the past 12 months?

### Are dental implants safe for older patients?

Yes. Age is not a contraindication for dental implants provided the patient has adequate bone density and is free from uncontrolled systemic disease. Patients in their 60s and 70s regularly receive implants in Singapore with high success rates. The more relevant factors are bone quality, medical history, and medication use, particularly bisphosphonates, which affect bone remodelling.

### What are red flags when choosing a dental implant clinic in Singapore?

Red flags include: prices quoted significantly below SGD 2,500 without disclosing the implant brand; no mention of CBCT scanning in the treatment plan; a dentist without a disclosed postgraduate qualification in implant dentistry; no written cost estimate provided before treatment; pressure to proceed without a proper diagnostic workup; and Google reviews that are generic or clustered within a short period suggesting artificial boosting.

### How do I know if I need bone grafting before an implant?

A CBCT scan will reveal the height and width of available bone at the implant site. Bone grafting is typically required if a tooth has been missing for more than a year, if bone was lost due to periodontal disease, or if a tooth was extracted traumatically. A credible implant dentist will present CBCT findings to you and explain whether grafting is needed before you commit to treatment.

### Can dental implants fail, and what happens if they do?

Implant failure can occur if osseointegration does not complete, typically due to infection, smoking, or insufficient bone contact. Early failure (within 3 months) is more common than late failure and is usually managed by removing the failed implant, treating the site, and re-placing after healing. Reputable clinics in Singapore include an implant replacement policy for early failure, so confirm this at the consultation stage.

### What is the difference between a dental implant and a dental bridge?

A dental implant replaces the entire tooth structure including the root, requiring no modification of adjacent teeth and preserving jawbone density. A dental bridge is supported by crowns on adjacent teeth, which requires those teeth to be shaved down. Implants are the preferred long-term solution where bone volume is adequate; bridges are appropriate when bone is insufficient for implant placement or when adjacent teeth already require crowning.
