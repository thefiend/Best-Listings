## What to Look for When Choosing an ENT Specialist in Singapore

**Accreditation and sub-specialisation.** All ENT specialists in Singapore must be registered with the Singapore Medical Council (SMC) and accredited by the Ministry of Health. Beyond general ENT credentials, look for sub-specialisation: rhinology (sinus and nasal), otology (ear and hearing), laryngology (voice and throat), head and neck surgery, or paediatric ENT. Choosing a specialist with specific training in your condition improves diagnostic accuracy and surgical outcomes.

**Google review score and volume.** A 4.8+ rating with at least 50 reviews is the reliable baseline for private ENT specialists in Singapore. Volume matters as much as average: a 5.0 from 30 reviews carries less statistical weight than a 4.9 from 200. Check that reviews mention specific conditions or procedures, not just generic praise, as this signals authentic patient experiences.

**Hospital affiliation.** ENT specialists in Singapore typically consult at either a private specialist centre (e.g. Mount Elizabeth, Gleneagles, Camden Medical Centre, Mount Alvernia) or a public restructured hospital (e.g. SGH, TTSH, NUH). Private clinics offer shorter waiting times and more flexible appointment scheduling. Public hospitals offer subsidised rates under the Medisave/MediShield framework. Confirm whether the specialist's facility accepts your insurance plan before booking.

**Consultation thoroughness.** A proper ENT consultation should include at minimum a nasal endoscopy (flexible or rigid scope), otoscopy for ear examination, and throat assessment. Be cautious of consultations completed in under 10 minutes without any instrumentation. Patient reviews that describe detailed explanations and use of diagnostic scopes during the first visit are a strong positive signal.

**Transparency on treatment options.** Reputable ENT specialists present conservative options before recommending surgery. If a specialist moves immediately to a surgical recommendation without discussing medical or non-surgical management, seek a second opinion. The best specialists on this list are consistently praised by patients for presenting all options and respecting patient decision-making.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does an ENT specialist cost in Singapore?

A private ENT specialist consultation in Singapore typically costs SGD 150 to SGD 350 for the initial consultation, which may include diagnostic scoping. Follow-up consultations are generally SGD 80 to SGD 200. Diagnostic procedures such as audiometry hearing tests add SGD 50 to SGD 150. Surgical procedures, including FESS for sinusitis or tonsillectomy, range from SGD 5,000 to SGD 15,000 including hospitalisation at a private hospital. Medisave can be used for approved surgical procedures.

### What conditions does an ENT specialist treat in Singapore?

ENT specialists treat conditions affecting the ear (hearing loss, ear infections, tinnitus, perforated eardrum, vertigo), nose (sinusitis, nasal polyps, allergic rhinitis, deviated septum, nosebleeds), and throat (tonsillitis, adenoid problems, voice disorders, laryngitis, dysphagia). Many Singapore ENT specialists also treat snoring and obstructive sleep apnea, thyroid and neck lumps, and head and neck cancers.

### Do I need a referral to see an ENT specialist in Singapore?

No referral is required to see a private ENT specialist in Singapore. You can book directly with any of the clinics on this list. A referral letter from your GP can be useful as it provides the specialist with background medical history, but it is not mandatory. If you are seeking subsidised care at a public restructured hospital ENT department (e.g. Singapore General Hospital), a referral from a polyclinic is required and will significantly reduce waiting time.

### How do I know if I need an ENT specialist or if a GP is sufficient?

See a GP first for mild, short-duration ear, nose, or throat symptoms — a cold-related sore throat, minor ear pain from flying, or a brief episode of blocked nose. See an ENT specialist if symptoms persist beyond 3 weeks, recur frequently, cause significant hearing loss, involve a lump in the neck, affect swallowing or breathing, or if a GP has prescribed multiple rounds of antibiotics without resolution.

### Are ENT specialists in Singapore regulated?

Yes. All ENT specialists in Singapore must hold full registration with the Singapore Medical Council (SMC) and be accredited as specialists in Otorhinolaryngology by the Ministry of Health. Most private ENT specialists have trained in Singapore's public hospitals before entering private practice and hold postgraduate fellowship qualifications such as FRCS (ORL-HNS) from the Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh or equivalent.

### How far in advance should I book an ENT specialist in Singapore?

For non-urgent consultations at popular private clinics, expect to wait 1 to 3 weeks for an appointment. For urgent concerns such as sudden hearing loss or severe nosebleeds, call the clinic directly as most ENT specialists in Singapore will accommodate urgent cases within 24 to 48 hours. Sudden sensorineural hearing loss in particular should be treated as a medical emergency, ideally seen within 72 hours of onset.

### What is the difference between an ENT specialist and a general practitioner for ear and nose conditions?

A GP has general medical training and can manage common ENT presentations such as otitis media, tonsillitis, and allergic rhinitis with medication. An ENT specialist has completed five or more years of postgraduate surgical and medical training specific to ear, nose, and throat conditions and has access to specialised diagnostic equipment including nasal endoscopes, audiometers, tympanometers, and laryngoscopes. For recurrent, complex, or surgical cases, only an ENT specialist has the training and tools to fully investigate and manage the condition.

### Can an ENT specialist treat my child in Singapore?

Yes. Several ENT specialists on this list offer paediatric ENT services, most notably Dr Leslie Koh at The ENT Practice (Mt. Alvernia). Paediatric ENT conditions include recurrent otitis media (ear infections), glue ear requiring grommet insertion, enlarged tonsils and adenoids, snoring and sleep apnea in children, hearing loss affecting speech development, and nasal obstruction. When choosing a paediatric ENT specialist, look specifically for reviews mentioning children or families, as consultation style with young patients differs from adult practice.

### What red flags should I watch for when choosing an ENT specialist?

Avoid any clinic that cannot provide a clear address or operates without a fixed registered location. Be cautious of consultations that conclude without any instrumentation or physical examination. Avoid practices that push surgical recommendations at the first consultation without trial of non-surgical options. Very low review counts (under 20) at a practice that has been operating for several years warrant caution. Unanswered phones or inability to book within 2 to 3 weeks for a non-urgent issue may indicate understaffed or overstretched practices.

### Does Medisave cover ENT treatment in Singapore?

Medisave can be used for approved ENT surgical procedures performed in Singapore hospitals and day surgery centres, including tonsillectomy, adenoidectomy, septoplasty, turbinate reduction, functional endoscopic sinus surgery, myringoplasty, and cochlear implantation. Outpatient consultations and most diagnostic procedures are not covered by Medisave but may be claimable under integrated shield plans or other private health insurance. Always confirm with your insurer and the clinic prior to booking.
