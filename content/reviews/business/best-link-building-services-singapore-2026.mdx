## What to Look for When Hiring a Link Building Service

**1. Transparent source reporting.** Any reputable link building service should provide a monthly report naming every placed link: the URL where the link appears, the referring domain, the domain rating, the anchor text used, and whether the placement is dofollow or nofollow. Agencies that deliver a domain authority graph without showing you the individual placements are not giving you the information needed to evaluate quality.

**2. White-hat methodology with no PBN usage.** Ask directly: does the agency use private blog networks, link exchanges, or automated link placement tools? Google's spam updates have increasingly targeted these tactics, and a single manual penalty from a PBN-contaminated link profile can take 12 to 24 months to recover from. Legitimate agencies will answer this question clearly and point to their editorial outreach process.

**3. Topical relevance, not just DR.** A link from a DR50 site in an unrelated vertical contributes less ranking signal than a link from a DR30 site with genuine topical alignment to your industry. Ask how the agency selects publisher targets: the answer should reference industry relevance, audience overlap with your target market, and organic traffic verification on the referring domain.

**4. Realistic timeline expectations.** Link building compounds over time: expect six to twelve weeks before meaningful domain authority movement is visible in tools like Ahrefs or Semrush, and three to six months before Google's algorithm fully processes new referring domains into ranking signals. Agencies promising dramatic ranking improvements in under four weeks from link building alone are either exaggerating or using tactics that will not hold.

**5. Integration with your on-page SEO.** Links to a page that is poorly optimised, has thin content, or sits behind crawl blockers will underperform. The strongest link building campaigns are coordinated with on-page optimisation so that acquired links reinforce pages that are already technically sound and content-ready to rank.

### What is the difference between white-hat and black-hat link building?

White-hat link building acquires links through editorial merit: creating content other sites want to reference, contributing guest articles to relevant publications, earning citations through genuine business quality, and outreach to relevant sites that results in voluntary editorial inclusion. Black-hat link building uses shortcuts that violate Google's webmaster guidelines: private blog networks (PBNs), automated link building tools, reciprocal link schemes, and paid link placements without editorial review. White-hat links are durable and compound in value over time. Black-hat links risk Google manual penalties that can remove a domain from search results entirely and take 12 to 24 months to recover from even after the offending links are removed.

### How long does it take for link building to improve Google rankings?

Google processes new referring domains and link equity changes on a delay. Typically, new links begin influencing domain authority scores in tools like Ahrefs within two to four weeks of placement. The impact on Google's actual ranking algorithm takes longer: expect three to six months for consistent ranking movement on competitive keywords, assuming on-page SEO is already correctly configured. For new domains with very low starting authority (DR below 10), initial domain authority gains tend to be faster but ranking movement on competitive keywords still requires sustained link acquisition over six to twelve months. Link building should be planned as a 12-month programme minimum for meaningful, compounding results.

### Which pages should link building campaigns target?

Link building should prioritise pages that are closest to conversion: service pages, product category pages, and high-intent informational pages that support the buyer journey for your core keywords. Homepage links build domain-level authority but do not channel ranking signal to the specific pages competing for commercial keywords. A well-structured campaign distributes links across homepage, key service or category pages, and supporting content pages in proportions aligned with the site's organic traffic goals. Internal linking architecture should be audited before the campaign begins to ensure link equity from new referring domains flows correctly to the target pages.

### Is link building still important for SEO in 2026?

Yes. Despite Google's ongoing algorithm development, backlinks from editorially relevant, high-authority domains remain one of the strongest ranking signals for competitive keywords in 2026. Google's John Mueller has confirmed that links are still used in ranking. What has changed is the tolerance for low-quality link acquisition: Google's spam updates have progressively devalued low-authority, irrelevant, and PBN-sourced links while increasing the weight assigned to topically relevant editorial placements from sites with genuine organic audiences. Link building in 2026 requires higher quality and more strategic targeting than it did five years ago, but the fundamental mechanism remains central to organic search competitiveness.

### What is domain authority and how does it affect rankings?

Domain authority (DA or DR depending on the tool, Moz vs Ahrefs) is a third-party metric estimating a domain's overall link equity relative to all other domains on the internet, on a logarithmic scale of 1 to 100. Google does not use domain authority scores directly: they are proxies for the underlying PageRank and link signals Google processes. A domain with DR30 will typically find it easier to rank for competitive mid-tail keywords than a domain at DR10, all other factors being equal. Each 10-point improvement in domain authority requires progressively more referring domain volume and quality due to the logarithmic scale. Moving from DR10 to DR30 typically requires 50 to 150 quality referring domains: moving from DR30 to DR50 may require 300 to 600.

### Can I build links myself, or should I hire an agency?

Self-managed link building is feasible for businesses with time and an existing content programme. However, editorial outreach at scale: identifying relevant publishers, building relationships, pitching and placing guest contributions: is time-intensive and produces diminishing returns without an existing publisher network. For businesses targeting competitive commercial keywords, agency-managed link building is typically more efficient: the cost of staff time for DIY outreach at scale usually exceeds the agency retainer cost once productivity is factored in.
