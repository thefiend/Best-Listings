The best fertility clinics in Singapore combine specialist-led IVF and reproductive medicine expertise, evidence-based treatment protocols, transparent success rate reporting, and compassionate patient support through what is often an emotionally taxing process. Singapore's fertility medicine sector operates under Ministry of Health licensing frameworks that govern the practice of assisted reproduction, and clinics must hold specific MOH licences for procedures such as IVF, IUI, and egg freezing. With IVF cycle costs ranging from SGD 15,000 to over SGD 25,000 and success rates varying meaningfully across providers, choosing the right clinic is both a medical and financial decision of significant consequence. We evaluated Singapore's leading fertility and women's health specialists using verified Google Maps patient review data, doctor credentials, and clinic specialisation to rank the 10 best fertility clinics in 2026.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Alpha IVF Centre & Alpha Women's Specialists, 169 Google reviews at 4.9 stars, a dedicated fertility centre at Royal Square Medical with a strong track record in IVF and reproductive medicine
- **Best for patient volume and reviews:** Sincere Medical Specialist Center, 587 Google reviews at 4.9 stars, the highest-reviewed fertility-linked women's health clinic in Singapore
- **Best for comprehensive women's care:** The Prenatal Consultants, 935 Google reviews at 4.9 stars, Singapore's most reviewed obstetrics and gynaecology group serving fertility, pregnancy, and postnatal needs
- IVF cycle costs in Singapore: subsidised IVF at public hospitals from SGD 6,000–10,000; private IVF from SGD 15,000–25,000 per cycle; egg freezing from SGD 10,000–15,000 per cycle
- MOH requires all clinics offering IVF and assisted reproductive technology to hold specific ART licences; always verify your clinic's current licensing status
- Key questions to ask: clinic-specific success rates by age group, protocol personalisation approach, embryo laboratory accreditation, and support available between cycles

## How We Ranked These Fertility Clinics

We ranked fertility and women's health clinics using Google Maps patient review data as the primary signal, requiring a minimum of 4.8 stars and a minimum of 15 verified reviews. We weighted review volume alongside rating as a measure of outcome consistency. Secondary criteria included: MOH ART licence status (for clinics offering IVF), doctor specialisation (reproductive medicine, obstetrics and gynaecology, or related subspecialties), clinic focus (dedicated fertility centres ranked higher than general medical practices offering fertility as a peripheral service), breadth of services offered (IVF, IUI, egg freezing, fertility workup, gynaecological care), and the quality of patient experience as described in verified reviews — with particular attention to comments about communication, empathy, and management of failed cycles. Clinics without current MOH ART licensing or with unresolved regulatory issues were excluded.

## What to Look for When Choosing a Fertility Clinic

**MOH licensing for ART procedures.** Any clinic offering IVF, IUI, or egg freezing in Singapore must hold a current MOH Assisted Reproduction Technology (ART) licence. Verify the licence status directly with MOH before committing to treatment. General O&G clinics that are not ART-licensed may offer initial fertility workups and referral, but cannot perform IVF or other ART procedures on-site.

**Clinic-specific success rates by age group.** Ask for the clinic's IVF live birth rates broken down by female patient age group (under 35, 35–37, 38–40, over 40). Aggregated success rates that do not account for patient age are misleading. Singapore's MOH requires licensed ART clinics to report their outcomes — compare these numbers rather than relying solely on marketing claims.

**Protocol personalisation and embryology standards.** The best fertility clinics adjust stimulation protocols based on each patient's antral follicle count, AMH level, and response to previous cycles. Ask about the laboratory's embryo culture conditions, blastocyst culture rates, and vitrification success rates. These technical details are meaningful predictors of outcome.

**Support between cycles.** Failed cycles are emotionally devastating. A clinic's approach to post-failure counselling, protocol review, and support through the decision to continue or stop treatment matters enormously to patient wellbeing. Ask about what happens after a failed cycle before you start.

**Cost transparency.** IVF in Singapore's private sector involves significant out-of-pocket expenditure even with Medisave and co-funding for eligible patients. Ensure you receive a clear, itemised cost estimate covering stimulation medication, egg retrieval, embryo transfer, and cryostorage before committing to a clinic. Add-ons such as PGT and assisted hatching should be listed separately with clear indications of when they are clinically justified.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does IVF cost in Singapore in 2026?

Private IVF in Singapore typically costs SGD 15,000 to 25,000 per fresh cycle, including stimulation medication, egg retrieval, embryo culture, and a single frozen embryo transfer. Medisave can be used for eligible IVF procedures (up to SGD 15,000 per cycle), and Singapore citizens may qualify for government co-funding of up to SGD 7,700 per IVF cycle (up to three co-funded cycles). Egg freezing (social fertility preservation) typically costs SGD 10,000 to 15,000 per cycle, and ongoing cryostorage is an additional annual cost.

### What is the difference between IVF and IUI?

IUI (intrauterine insemination) is a simpler procedure where sperm is placed directly into the uterus around the time of ovulation, often combined with mild ovarian stimulation. IVF involves full ovarian stimulation, surgical egg retrieval, laboratory fertilisation, embryo culture, and uterine transfer. IVF has higher success rates per cycle than IUI, particularly for patients with blocked fallopian tubes, low ovarian reserve, or significant male factor infertility, but is more complex and expensive. Your fertility specialist will recommend the appropriate starting point based on your specific diagnosis.

### At what age should I start fertility treatment?

Female fertility declines with age, with the most significant reduction occurring after age 37. Couples under 35 who have been trying for 12 months without success are generally advised to seek fertility evaluation. For women aged 35 or above, evaluation is recommended after 6 months of unsuccessful trying. Single women or couples considering future family planning should be aware that egg freezing success rates are significantly higher when eggs are frozen before age 35.

### What is egg freezing and is it worth it?

Egg freezing (oocyte cryopreservation) involves stimulating the ovaries to produce multiple eggs, retrieving them, and vitrifying (fast-freezing) them for future use. Success depends heavily on the patient's age at freezing and the number of mature eggs retrieved. A woman who freezes 15 mature eggs before age 35 has approximately a 70–80% cumulative live birth chance from those eggs. The value of egg freezing is greatest when done early — it provides meaningful future options but is not a guarantee of a future pregnancy.

### Can Singapore men access male fertility assessment and treatment?

Yes. Male fertility assessment — including semen analysis, hormonal evaluation, and clinical assessment — is available through urologists, andrology specialists, and specialist men's health clinics such as Sire Singapore. Male factor infertility (low sperm count, poor motility, abnormal morphology) is a primary or contributing cause in approximately half of all fertility cases. Both partners should be assessed simultaneously rather than beginning female fertility treatment before male assessment is complete.

### Is fertility treatment covered by insurance in Singapore?

Most Singapore health insurance policies do not cover fertility treatment, including IVF, as it is typically classified as an elective procedure. Medisave and government co-funding for eligible IVF procedures provide partial subsidy for Singapore citizens, but private treatment costs remain substantial. Check your specific policy for any fertility-related benefits, and confirm with your insurer before committing to treatment.

### How many IVF cycles should I attempt?

Most fertility specialists recommend at least 3 IVF cycles before concluding that IVF is unlikely to succeed, as success rates accumulate across cycles. Cumulative live birth rates after three fresh IVF cycles for women under 38 exceed 50% at experienced clinics. The decision to continue beyond three cycles depends on factors including age, embryo quality, the specific diagnosis, and the patient's emotional and financial capacity. Your doctor should review and adjust the protocol between cycles based on how you responded.

### What happens if IVF doesn't work?

If IVF does not result in a live birth after two or three cycles with optimal protocols, your specialist should conduct a thorough review of potential contributing factors — including uterine receptivity, embryo quality, and immunological factors. Interventions such as ERA (endometrial receptivity array), preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidies (PGT-A), or immune suppression protocols may be recommended. In some cases, using donor eggs or donor sperm, or exploring gestational surrogacy (only available for medical necessity in Singapore under strict MOH regulations), may be appropriate paths to explore.
