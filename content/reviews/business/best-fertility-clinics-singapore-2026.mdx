## What to Look for When Hiring a Fertility Clinic in Singapore

**Specialist Credentials in Reproductive Medicine.** Not all O&G doctors have subspecialty training in fertility and IVF. When selecting a fertility clinic, ask specifically whether the treating doctor has formal subspecialty training in reproductive endocrinology and infertility, holds FRCOG or equivalent qualification, and has hands-on IVF laboratory and clinical experience. A general O&G specialist managing fertility patients alongside a busy obstetric practice will typically provide less specialised fertility care than a dedicated reproductive medicine specialist.

**Laboratory Standards and Technology.** The IVF laboratory is where fertilisation, embryo culture, and embryo freezing occur, making it the most technically critical component of the IVF process. Ask prospective clinics about their laboratory accreditation, embryologist qualifications, incubator technology (standard vs time-lapse), freezing method (vitrification vs slow-freeze), and embryo survival rates after thawing. Clinics that cannot answer these questions in detail or that are reluctant to discuss laboratory standards should be approached with caution.

**Transparency on Success Rates.** IVF success rates in Singapore are reported by the MOH and differ significantly by age group and diagnosis. Be wary of clinics that quote headline success rates without clarifying whether these reflect live birth rate per transfer, per cycle started, or per egg retrieval, and without stratifying by patient age. The most meaningful metric is live birth rate per cycle started, stratified by patient age. Ask your prospective clinic how their results compare to the national figures published by MOH.

**Integrated Male Factor Assessment.** Fertility is a couple issue, and clinics that assess the female partner in isolation without conducting or coordinating a comprehensive male factor workup from the outset are providing incomplete care. Semen analysis, hormonal profiling, and appropriate male factor management should be part of any fertility workup from the first consultation. Clinics that focus exclusively on the female partner without a structured male assessment protocol may miss treatable male factor conditions that affect outcomes.

**Counselling and Emotional Support.** Fertility treatment is emotionally demanding, and the best clinics integrate psychological support and patient counselling into the treatment pathway. Ask whether the clinic has a counsellor available to fertility patients, how they handle the communication of difficult news (poor fertilisation, failed implantation, chromosomally abnormal embryos), and how accessible the doctor is during the treatment cycle for patient questions and concerns.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does IVF cost in Singapore?

IVF treatment at a private fertility clinic in Singapore typically costs between SGD 10,000 and SGD 20,000 per cycle, including monitoring, egg retrieval, laboratory fees, and embryo transfer. Costs vary by clinic, the specific protocol used, and whether additional services such as PGT-A or surgical sperm retrieval are required. Medication costs (stimulation injections) add approximately SGD 2,000–5,000 per cycle. Medisave can be used to offset IVF costs up to SGD 6,000 per cycle for eligible couples under MOH-approved conditions. At restructured hospitals (KKH, NUH), subsidised IVF is available for eligible Singapore citizens at significantly lower out-of-pocket costs.

### What is the difference between IVF and IUI?

IUI (intrauterine insemination) involves placing processed sperm directly into the uterus around the time of ovulation, either in a natural cycle or after mild ovarian stimulation. It is a low-intervention procedure suitable for couples with unexplained infertility, mild male factor, or cervical factor infertility. IVF (in vitro fertilisation) involves stimulating the ovaries to produce multiple eggs, retrieving those eggs under sedation, fertilising them with sperm in a laboratory, and transferring one or more embryos into the uterus. IVF is more involved and expensive than IUI but has significantly higher success rates and is the appropriate treatment for moderate-to-severe infertility diagnoses.

### Does Singapore Medisave cover IVF?

Yes. CPF Medisave can be used to offset the costs of IVF and certain other assisted reproductive technology procedures in Singapore, subject to MOH eligibility conditions. As of 2026, eligible couples can use up to SGD 6,000 from Medisave per IVF cycle. To qualify, couples must be legally married Singapore citizens or permanent residents under age 40 (for the female partner) and must not have exceeded their lifetime IVF Medisave limit. Co-funded IVF is also available at KKH and NUH for eligible Singapore citizens under the Government Co-Funding Scheme, which covers a portion of IVF costs at restructured hospitals.

### How do I choose a fertility clinic in Singapore?

When choosing a fertility clinic in Singapore, prioritise: specialist credentials in reproductive medicine (not just general O&G), IVF laboratory standards and technology, transparency about success rates stratified by age, integrated male and female factor assessment from the first consultation, and honest patient communication about realistic outcomes. Read Google reviews carefully for patterns in how the clinic handles difficult news and treatment failures, as this reveals more about clinical character than reviews from straightforward successful cases. Consider whether the clinic's volume and structure align with your preference for personal attention vs institutional resources.

### What is egg freezing and who should consider it?

Egg freezing (oocyte cryopreservation) involves stimulating the ovaries to produce multiple eggs, retrieving those eggs, and vitrifying (fast-freezing) them for future use. It is recommended for women who wish to preserve their fertility for social reasons (career, partnership, life circumstances) before age-related egg quality decline becomes significant, typically recommended before age 35 for best outcomes. It is also recommended for women facing cancer treatment that may affect fertility, those with a family history of early menopause, and those with conditions such as endometriosis that may affect future ovarian reserve. Frozen eggs can be stored indefinitely and used in an IVF cycle when the patient is ready to attempt conception.

### What documents do I need for a fertility consultation in Singapore?

For a first fertility consultation in Singapore, bring: identity documents (NRIC or passport) for both partners; any previous fertility investigation results (semen analyses, hormone blood tests, ultrasound reports); records of any previous fertility treatments, including medication names and cycle outcomes; a list of current medications and supplements for both partners; menstrual cycle tracking records if available; and any previous surgical reports relevant to reproductive organs (e.g., laparoscopy, myomectomy). Bringing comprehensive documentation allows the specialist to conduct a more targeted assessment and avoids repeating tests unnecessarily.

### How long does IVF treatment take in Singapore?

A typical IVF cycle from the start of ovarian stimulation to embryo transfer takes approximately 4–6 weeks. Ovarian stimulation injections begin on Day 2–3 of the menstrual cycle and continue for 10–14 days, followed by egg retrieval under sedation, laboratory fertilisation and embryo culture for 5–6 days to blastocyst stage, and then either a fresh embryo transfer (Day 5 after retrieval) or a frozen embryo transfer in a subsequent cycle. Including the initial workup consultations and pre-cycle investigations, the full process from first consultation to embryo transfer typically takes 6–10 weeks. Results (pregnancy blood test) are available approximately 10–12 days after embryo transfer.

### What is the IVF success rate in Singapore?

IVF success rates in Singapore vary significantly by patient age. According to MOH published data, live birth rates per embryo transfer in Singapore's licensed ART centres are approximately 40–50% for patients under 35, 30–40% for patients aged 35–37, 20–30% for patients aged 38–40, and declining further for older patients. Success rates also depend on egg/embryo quality, endometrial receptivity, and the presence of genetic abnormalities (which PGT-A can help identify). Cumulative success rates across multiple frozen embryo transfers from a single egg retrieval are higher than single-transfer rates. Ask your chosen clinic for their age-stratified outcomes data when making treatment decisions.

### Are fertility treatments covered by insurance in Singapore?

Most standard health insurance policies in Singapore do not cover IVF and assisted reproductive technology treatments, as these are typically classified as elective procedures. Some group corporate insurance policies include fertility benefits, and an increasing number of integrated shield plans offer riders or add-ons covering fertility investigation and treatment up to specific limits. Medisave (CPF) provides partial coverage for IVF costs for eligible married couples under MOH guidelines. Before commencing fertility treatment, check your insurer's specific policy terms and confirm with your HR department whether your employer's group policy includes any fertility benefits.
