## What to Look for When Choosing a Cardiologist

**Subspecialty alignment.** Cardiology is a broad specialty. A cardiologist who specialises in electrophysiology and arrhythmia management is not the same choice as one whose practice centres on interventional procedures like angioplasty. Before your first appointment, clarify what condition or concern you are being seen for and check whether the cardiologist's stated subspecialty matches that need. Most clinic websites list the conditions and procedures they focus on.

**Hospital affiliation and procedural access.** For patients who may require investigation or intervention beyond an outpatient consultation, the hospital your cardiologist is affiliated with matters. A cardiologist based at Gleneagles or Mount Elizabeth Hospital has immediate access to catheterisation laboratory facilities, 24-hour cardiac monitoring, and inpatient cardiac care. For purely preventive screening or ongoing management of a known stable condition, hospital affiliation is less critical.

**Google review recency and volume.** A 5.0-star average across 200+ reviews is more meaningful than a 5.0-star average across 12 reviews. Always check both the average and the total count, and look at whether recent reviews (within the last 6 months) reflect the same standard as the historical average. A drop in recent review quality often signals a change in staff or processes.

**Consultation communication quality.** Cardiac diagnoses are often complex and require patient understanding to manage effectively. A cardiologist who communicates clearly, answers questions directly, and explains the reasoning behind recommendations will produce better long-term outcomes than one who delivers findings without context. Patient reviews are a reliable signal here: look for language like "explained clearly", "took time to answer my questions", and "I understood my results for the first time".

**Transparency on investigations and costs.** Reputable cardiology clinics outline the likely investigation pathway and associated costs at or before the consultation. If a clinic cannot give you a clear picture of what tests will be ordered and what they cost before you consent, that is a flag worth noting. The best cardiologists in Singapore are able to explain why each investigation is clinically indicated.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a cardiologist consultation cost in Singapore?

A private cardiologist consultation in Singapore typically costs SGD 150–350 for the initial visit. Follow-up consultations range from SGD 80–200. Cardiac investigations are charged separately: an echocardiogram costs approximately SGD 350–600, a stress treadmill test SGD 200–400, a 24-hour Holter monitor SGD 250–450, and a CT coronary angiogram SGD 1,200–2,200. Total cost for a comprehensive cardiac workup at a private clinic in Singapore commonly falls between SGD 1,500 and SGD 4,000 depending on the investigation panel.

### Do I need a GP referral to see a cardiologist in Singapore?

No referral is required to see a private cardiologist in Singapore. You can book directly with any clinic on this list. However, having a GP referral letter that summarises your symptoms, past medical history, and any prior investigations is strongly recommended as it gives the cardiologist useful clinical context and can reduce duplicate testing. If you are using insurance or Medisave, check your insurer's referral requirements, as some plans require GP referral for specialist consultation claims.

### What cardiac tests should I expect at a first cardiology consultation in Singapore?

At a first consultation, most cardiologists will take a detailed clinical history, conduct a physical examination, and order a resting ECG. Depending on your presenting complaint and risk profile, they may also request an echocardiogram, a treadmill stress test, a 24-hour Holter monitor, fasting blood tests (including lipid panel, glucose, and cardiac markers), and in some cases a CT coronary angiogram or coronary calcium score. Not all tests will be ordered at every consultation; the specific panel depends on your symptoms and individual risk factors.

### How do I know if I need to see a cardiologist or if my GP can manage my condition?

Your GP can manage many common cardiovascular conditions including hypertension, high cholesterol, and mild palpitations that are well-characterised and stable. You should see a cardiologist if: you experience chest pain, unexplained shortness of breath, or syncope (fainting); your ECG is abnormal; a stress test returns borderline or abnormal results; you have a family history of premature heart disease and want a formal risk assessment; or your GP recommends specialist review for any reason. When in doubt, a single specialist consultation for assessment is low-risk and often highly reassuring.

### What is the difference between a general cardiologist and an interventional cardiologist in Singapore?

A general cardiologist manages the full range of cardiac conditions through medical therapy, lifestyle advice, and monitoring. An interventional cardiologist has additional subspecialty training to perform catheter-based procedures inside blood vessels and the heart: coronary angioplasty, stent placement, structural heart interventions such as TAVI, and closure of septal defects. If your condition may require a procedure, you will typically be referred to or seen by an interventional cardiologist. Many cardiologists in Singapore hold dual qualifications in both general and interventional cardiology.

### Are cardiologists in Singapore regulated?

Yes. All cardiologists practising in Singapore must be registered with the Singapore Medical Council (SMC) and hold a valid practising certificate. Cardiology is a recognised specialist discipline under the SMC's specialist register. Patients can verify any cardiologist's registration status through the SMC website. All clinics on this list operate under fully registered specialists. Private cardiologists practising in JCI-accredited hospitals (including Gleneagles and Mount Elizabeth) are additionally subject to hospital credentialing requirements.

### Can Medisave be used for cardiologist consultations and cardiac procedures in Singapore?

Medisave can be used for certain approved outpatient procedures and inpatient cardiac treatments, but generally cannot be used for outpatient specialist consultations alone. It can be used for hospitalisation arising from cardiac events or procedures, as well as for specific approved day procedures such as coronary angioplasty and pacemaker implantation. Speak directly with your chosen clinic's billing team or your CPF board to clarify what is claimable for your specific circumstances. Many private insurance plans with Integrated Shield policies cover private cardiologist consultations and investigations subject to deductibles and co-insurance.

### What are the red flags when choosing a cardiologist in Singapore?

Avoid clinics that cannot clearly explain the clinical rationale for investigations they are recommending. Other red flags include cardiologists who do not allow time for questions during consultation; clinics with very few or very old patient reviews; facilities that cannot give a clear pre-consultation cost estimate; and any provider who cannot be verified on the Singapore Medical Council specialist register. In cardiac care specifically, be wary of any cardiologist who recommends invasive procedures after a single outpatient visit without a supporting investigation pathway, or who discourages you from seeking a second opinion.

For non-urgent cardiac screening or risk assessment, booking 1–3 weeks ahead is typically sufficient for the private cardiologists on this list. For urgent concerns such as new-onset chest pain, unexplained palpitations, or a recent abnormal test result, most private cardiology clinics in Singapore are able to accommodate same-week or next-day appointments. If you are experiencing acute chest pain, call 995 or attend the nearest emergency department immediately rather than waiting for a clinic appointment.

Mount Elizabeth Orchard (Orchard Road), Gleneagles Hospital and Medical Centre (Napier Road), and Mount Elizabeth Novena Hospital (Novena) are the three main private hospital clusters in Singapore with the highest density of cardiologists.
