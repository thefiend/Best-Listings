Finding the best vinyl flooring in Singapore is harder than it looks, because almost every renovation shop now claims to "do vinyl", yet very few specialise in it. This guide is for HDB owners, condo residents, and landlords who want SPC, LVT, or vinyl plank flooring supplied and installed properly, usually as an overlay on existing tiles so they can skip hacking and downtime. We wrote it because a bad vinyl job looks fine on day one and shows lifted edges, hollow planks, and door-frame gaps six months later. BestThingReview.com reviewed 48 flooring businesses on Google Maps, removed renovation firms, wallpaper galleries, and polishing specialists, and ranked the remaining vinyl specialists by Google rating and review volume, then checked wear layer specs, SPC versus LVT range, and warranty terms. If you need timber, parquet, or a broader mix of materials, our guide to the <a href="/business/best-flooring-contractors-singapore">best flooring contractors in Singapore</a> covers those.

## How We Ranked These Vinyl Flooring Specialists

We started with 48 flooring-related businesses from Google Maps and applied hard thresholds before any scoring: a minimum Google rating of 4.0 stars, a minimum of 10 verified reviews, and a verifiable Singapore business address. We then excluded businesses whose primary trade is not vinyl flooring supply and installation, including general renovation and interior design firms, wallpaper and blinds galleries, floor polishing specialists, anti-slip coating providers, and furniture retailers. Businesses whose vinyl offering is secondary to outdoor decking or timber were also removed, as were laminate-led shops, since laminate is not waterproof in the way vinyl is. The remaining specialists were ranked by Google rating weighted against review volume, so a 5.0-star rating across 700+ reviews outranks a 5.0-star rating across 20. Finally, we checked each business's published product range for wear layer and thickness specs, SPC versus LVT options, stated warranties, and overlay capability on existing tiles. Ratings and review counts are taken directly from Google Maps at the time of writing. BestThingReview.com does not accept payment for placement on this list.

## What to Look for When Hiring a Vinyl Flooring Specialist

**Wear layer, not overall thickness.** Sales pitches often lead with total thickness, such as 5mm or 6.5mm, but the wear layer is what determines how long the floor resists scratches and scuffs. A 0.3mm wear layer is adequate for bedrooms with light use; 0.5mm or more is the safer benchmark for living rooms, homes with pets, and rentals. Ask for the figure in writing on the quotation.

**SPC or LVT for your specific floor.** Rigid SPC tolerates minor subfloor unevenness and resists denting, so it suits older resale flats. Flexible LVT is quieter underfoot and slimmer, but it shows imperfections in the subfloor more readily. A good specialist will inspect your existing tiles and recommend levelling where needed rather than laying over hollow or chipped tiles.

**Subfloor preparation included in the quote.** The most common cause of failed vinyl overlays in Singapore is skipping preparation. Confirm whether the quote covers removing old skirting, filling grout lines, self-levelling uneven areas, door trimming, and new skirting. These items can add S$300 to S$1,500 to a 4-room HDB job if they appear as extras later.

**Two separate warranties.** Vinyl warranties come in two parts: the manufacturer's product warranty (commonly 10 to 25 years for residential use) and the installer's workmanship warranty (commonly one year). Lifting, gapping, and hollow sounds usually fall under workmanship, so make sure that term is stated clearly.

**Review volume and recency.** A 4.8-star rating across several hundred reviews is a far stronger signal than 5.0 stars across 20. Read recent reviews for how the team handled defects after handover.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does vinyl flooring cost per square foot in Singapore?

Vinyl flooring in Singapore typically costs S$3 to S$8 per square foot supplied and installed in 2026. Entry-level glue-down LVT starts around S$3 to S$4.50 per square foot, mid-range click LVT and standard SPC fall between S$4.50 and S$7, and premium SPC, thick 6.5mm boards, or herringbone layouts can reach S$9 to S$12. For a 4-room HDB flat with about 700 to 900 square feet of flooring area, expect a total of roughly S$2,500 to S$5,500 including skirting. Self-levelling, if needed, typically adds S$1.50 to S$3 per square foot.

### What is the difference between SPC, LVT and laminate flooring?

SPC (stone polymer composite) has a rigid core made from limestone powder and PVC, making it dimensionally stable, dent-resistant, and fully waterproof. LVT (luxury vinyl tile) is a flexible PVC board, available as glue-down or click-lock, that is quieter and slimmer but more sensitive to uneven subfloors. Laminate has a high-density fibreboard core that swells if water seeps into the joints, which is why vinyl is preferred for Singapore kitchens and homes that are mopped often.

### Can vinyl flooring be laid over existing tiles in an HDB flat?

Yes. Overlaying vinyl on existing HDB floor tiles is the most common way vinyl is installed in Singapore, because it avoids hacking, noise, and debris. The existing tiles must be firmly bonded with no hollow spots, and deep grout lines or uneven areas should be filled or self-levelled first. Expect the floor height to rise by roughly 2mm to 7mm depending on the product, which may require trimming the bottom of doors.

### Do I need an HDB permit to install vinyl flooring?

Laying vinyl flooring as an overlay on top of existing floor finishes generally does not require an HDB renovation permit, whereas hacking or removing existing floor tiles does. Rules change from time to time, so check current HDB guidelines and use a contractor from HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors if any hacking is involved. Condo owners should apply to their MCST for renovation approval and check deposit requirements before work starts.

### Is vinyl flooring waterproof?

SPC and most modern LVT boards are waterproof, meaning the planks themselves will not swell or warp when exposed to water. However, the floor as an installed system is water-resistant rather than watertight: water that pools for long periods can seep through the joints to the subfloor. Vinyl suits kitchens, living rooms, and bedrooms, but not shower areas or wet bathrooms.

### How long does vinyl flooring last in Singapore?

Good quality vinyl flooring typically lasts 10 to 20 years in a Singapore home, and premium boards with a 0.5mm or thicker wear layer can exceed that with proper care. Lifespan depends mainly on wear layer thickness, subfloor preparation, and maintenance: use felt pads under furniture and avoid harsh solvent cleaners.

### What thickness and wear layer should I choose for vinyl flooring?

For most Singapore homes, choose a board with a total thickness of 4mm to 6.5mm and a wear layer of at least 0.3mm, ideally 0.5mm. Glue-down LVT is usually 2mm to 3mm thick and works well where door clearance is tight. Click LVT and SPC boards are usually 4mm to 7mm and often include an attached 1mm to 1.5mm IXPE underlay for acoustic comfort.
### How long does vinyl flooring installation take?

A typical 3-room or 4-room HDB vinyl overlay takes one to two working days, and many specialists on this list complete standard flats in a single day. A 5-room flat or executive unit usually takes two days. Add one extra day if self-levelling is required, since the compound must cure before boards can be laid.
### Is vinyl flooring good for homes with pets?

Yes, vinyl is one of the most pet-friendly flooring options in Singapore. It is waterproof, quieter underfoot than tiles, and a 0.5mm wear layer with a matte or textured finish hides minor scratches well.

### Can vinyl flooring be installed in a new BTO flat?

Yes. Vinyl is a popular choice for BTO flats because it can be laid directly over the floor tiles provided by HDB, avoiding the cost and mess of hacking in a brand-new home. Schedule the installation after carpentry and painting so other trades do not damage the new floor.
