Singapore has one of the highest myopia rates in the world — over 80% of young adults are myopic, a figure that places the country among the most affected populations globally. That public health reality, combined with an ageing population increasingly presenting with cataracts, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, and macular degeneration, makes access to a qualified eye specialist not a luxury but a clinical necessity for a large proportion of Singapore residents. The distinction between visiting an optometrist, an optician, and a fully qualified ophthalmologist is critical and frequently misunderstood — and getting it wrong can cost you more than a correct prescription.

We evaluated more than 25 eye clinics, specialist centres, and public hospitals across Singapore, assessing MOH specialist registration status, subspecialty depth, diagnostic and surgical technology, treatment range, Google ratings, and published patient outcomes where available. These are the 10 best eye specialists and eye clinics in Singapore for 2026.

## How We Ranked These Eye Specialists

Our methodology for ranking the best eye specialists in Singapore 2026 applied the following criteria. MOH specialist registration: only clinics whose ophthalmologists hold SMC specialist registration in ophthalmology were considered — this is the legal and clinical baseline for eye specialist practice in Singapore. Subspecialty depth: eye care encompasses a wide range of distinct subspecialties including cornea and refractive surgery, cataract surgery, glaucoma, retina and vitreous, oculoplastics, and paediatric ophthalmology. Clinics with demonstrable subspecialty expertise in specific areas were assessed accordingly. Diagnostic and surgical technology: the presence of gold-standard diagnostic equipment — optical coherence tomography (OCT), corneal topography, visual field analysers, femtosecond laser platforms — was assessed as an indicator of clinical capability. Treatment range: the breadth of conditions treatable within the clinic was evaluated. Google ratings and patient experience signals were used as secondary indicators of care quality and patient communication. Medisave and subsidy accessibility was noted for government institutions.

### 1. Singapore National Eye Centre (SNEC), Best Overall

<img src="/images/places/best-eye-specialist-singapore/singapore-national-eye-centre-snec.jpg" alt="Singapore National Eye Centre (SNEC)" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

The Singapore National Eye Centre on Outram Road is Singapore's only dedicated national eye hospital and the country's most comprehensive centre for the diagnosis and treatment of eye diseases. With over 40 subspecialty-trained ophthalmologists on staff — covering cornea, cataract, glaucoma, retina, oculoplastics, neuro-ophthalmology, paediatric ophthalmology, and low vision rehabilitation — SNEC is the institution that other eye clinics refer their most complex cases to. It holds a Google rating of approximately 4.0 stars, a figure that reflects the realities of high patient volume and public hospital operational constraints rather than clinical quality, which by any measure is among the highest in Asia.

SNEC's diagnostic infrastructure is unmatched in Singapore: the centre operates multiple OCT platforms, corneal topographers, fundus fluorescein angiography units, visual field analysers, and cutting-edge surgical equipment for vitreoretinal, glaucoma, and cataract procedures. Clinical programmes include a nationally significant myopia control programme, one of the largest diabetic retinopathy screening and treatment services in the region, and a glaucoma specialist service that sees among the highest volume of glaucoma patients in Southeast Asia. For patients with complex, multi-system eye disease, or rare conditions that private clinics are unlikely to have encountered, SNEC is the correct first call.

For eligible Singapore citizens and permanent residents, SNEC provides subsidised specialist care under the Ministry of Health framework — a financially meaningful advantage given that private ophthalmology consultations in Singapore typically cost SGD 150–300 per visit. Cataract surgery, glaucoma treatment, and medical management of retinal conditions are Medisave-claimable at SNEC. The primary practical limitation is appointment lead time: non-urgent referrals can carry waiting periods of several weeks, which is why private clinic alternatives remain relevant for conditions requiring faster access.

## LASIK vs ICL vs LASEK — Which Eye Surgery Is Right for You?

Refractive surgery in Singapore is no longer a single choice. Three primary surgical options are available, each with different candidacy criteria, recovery timelines, and suitability profiles. Understanding the differences before your pre-operative assessment allows you to have a more informed conversation with your surgeon.

**LASIK (Laser-Assisted In Situ Keratomileusis)** is the most commonly performed refractive surgery in Singapore and worldwide. The surgeon creates a thin corneal flap using a femtosecond laser, applies an excimer laser to reshape the underlying corneal tissue to correct the refractive error, and then replaces the flap. Recovery is fast — most patients achieve functional vision within 24 hours — and the procedure is suitable for myopia up to approximately -10.00 dioptres, hyperopia, and astigmatism. LASIK requires adequate corneal thickness (typically minimum 500 microns) and regular corneal topography with no signs of keratoconus or pellucid marginal degeneration. Patients with thin corneas, high myopia, or irregular topography are not suitable candidates.

**ICL (Implantable Collamer Lens)** is the preferred option for patients who are unsuitable for LASIK due to thin corneas, high myopia (-6.00 or above), or corneal irregularity. A phakic intraocular lens is implanted in front of the natural crystalline lens without removing any corneal tissue. ICL preserves the corneal architecture entirely and is reversible, unlike laser procedures. Visual outcomes are excellent, often superior to LASIK for high myopia. The procedure requires adequate anterior chamber depth and appropriate pupil size. Cost is higher than LASIK — typically SGD 4,000–6,000 per eye at private specialist centres.

**LASEK/PRK (Laser Epithelial Keratomileusis / Photorefractive Keratectomy)** are surface ablation techniques that reshape the cornea without creating a flap. Recovery is slower than LASIK (three to five days of blurred vision and discomfort post-operatively, with full visual recovery taking two to four weeks), but the procedures are appropriate for patients with thinner corneas who are unsuitable for LASIK flap creation, for patients in contact sports where a flap represents a risk, and for certain corneal profile considerations. Outcomes at one year are equivalent to LASIK; the trade-off is a longer, more uncomfortable recovery.

Your pre-operative assessment with an experienced refractive surgeon will determine which procedure, if any, is appropriate for your specific corneal profile, refractive error, and lifestyle requirements. Never proceed with a surgeon who recommends LASIK without performing a full pre-operative assessment.

## Optometrist vs Ophthalmologist vs Optician — Who Should You See?

The three titles most commonly encountered in eye care in Singapore represent very different levels of training and clinical authority. Understanding the distinction protects you from receiving the wrong level of care for your condition.

**An optician** is trained to manufacture, fit, and dispense corrective eyewear — spectacles and contact lenses — based on prescriptions written by optometrists or ophthalmologists. Opticians do not diagnose eye disease, perform clinical eye examinations, or provide medical treatment. If you visit a spectacle shop and have your eyes "tested" by a staff member, you are typically interacting with an optician or a dispensing optician conducting a refraction for the purpose of selling you glasses, not a clinical eye examination.

**An optometrist** holds a degree in optometry (typically a four-year bachelor's programme) and is trained to conduct comprehensive eye examinations including visual acuity testing, refraction, and basic anterior and posterior segment assessment. In Singapore, optometrists can diagnose refractive errors and certain external eye conditions, and they may detect signs of systemic disease such as hypertensive retinopathy or diabetic changes on the retina. However, optometrists are not medical doctors, cannot prescribe pharmaceutical treatments, and cannot perform surgical or laser procedures. When an optometrist detects signs of glaucoma, cataract, macular disease, or other pathology, the appropriate action is a referral to an ophthalmologist.

**An ophthalmologist** is a fully qualified medical doctor (MBBS or equivalent) who has completed a specialist residency in ophthalmology, holds Singapore Medical Council (SMC) specialist registration in ophthalmology, and may hold additional subspecialty fellowship qualifications. Ophthalmologists diagnose and treat the full spectrum of eye diseases — medically, with laser treatments, and surgically. Only an ophthalmologist can prescribe pharmaceutical eye medications, perform LASIK and refractive surgery, operate on cataracts, treat glaucoma, or perform retinal surgery.

The practical rule: for spectacles and contact lens prescriptions, an optometrist is appropriate. For any symptom suggestive of eye disease — blurred vision not correctable with glasses, floaters, flashes, eye pain, redness, or visual field loss — see an ophthalmologist directly, or ask your GP for a referral.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does LASIK cost in Singapore?**
LASIK surgery in Singapore typically costs between SGD 1,800 and SGD 4,500 per eye at private specialist clinics, depending on the laser platform (standard excimer versus custom wavefront-guided), the inclusion or exclusion of the femtosecond laser for flap creation (bladeless LASIK costs more than microkeratome LASIK), and the clinic's pricing structure. Shinagawa and similar value-oriented providers start from approximately SGD 1,800 per eye; premium platform options at Clearvision and hospital-based eye centres range from SGD 3,500–4,500 per eye. Prices are typically quoted per eye and a bilateral (both eyes) treatment is the standard. LASIK is not Medisave-claimable in Singapore as it is classified as an elective procedure. Always request a full itemised cost breakdown before proceeding.

**What is myopia control treatment?**
Myopia control refers to interventions that slow the rate of myopia progression in children and teenagers, reducing the final degree of short-sightedness they develop and — importantly — reducing the risk of serious eye complications (retinal detachment, glaucoma, macular degeneration) that are associated with high myopia in later life. The main evidence-based myopia control options available in Singapore are: orthokeratology (Ortho-K) contact lenses worn overnight to temporarily flatten the cornea and slow axial elongation; low-dose atropine eye drops (0.01%–0.1% concentration) prescribed nightly, which are the most widely used and well-studied intervention in Singapore; and myopia-correcting spectacle lenses with peripheral defocus design (such as DIMS or MiYOSMART lenses). SNEC runs one of the most active myopia control and research programmes in the region and is an excellent resource for children with rapidly progressing myopia.

**How do I know if I'm suitable for LASIK?**
LASIK suitability requires a comprehensive pre-operative assessment that typically includes: corneal topography (to assess corneal shape and rule out keratoconus or irregular astigmatism), pachymetry (corneal thickness measurement — a minimum residual stromal bed of 250 microns after ablation is required for safety), pupillometry (pupil size measurement in dim light conditions, relevant for night vision quality after LASIK), wavefront analysis, a dilated fundus examination, and a stability check (refractive prescription must have been stable for at least 12 months). Broad contraindications for LASIK include: corneal thickness below the safe threshold for the required correction, irregular corneal topography suggesting keratoconus or early ectatic disease, unstable prescription, autoimmune conditions affecting wound healing, dry eye disease of moderate or severe grade, and certain systemic medications. Patients unsuitable for LASIK may be suitable for surface ablation (LASEK/PRK) or ICL, which your surgeon can discuss following the assessment.

**How much does cataract surgery cost in Singapore?**
Cataract surgery with a standard monofocal intraocular lens (IOL) is covered by Medisave under the Small Incision Cataract Surgery (SICS) schedule, with Medisave withdrawal limits of SGD 1,500–2,450 per eye depending on the type of facility (restructured hospital, private hospital, or day surgery centre). At public hospitals (SNEC, TTSH), subsidised rates reduce the out-of-pocket cost significantly for eligible patients. At private hospitals, total costs including surgeon fee, hospital facility fee, anaesthesia, and standard IOL typically range from SGD 4,000–8,000 per eye before Medisave deduction. Premium IOL upgrades — multifocal, toric, or EDOF lenses — add SGD 1,000–3,000 to the procedure cost and are not Medisave-claimable. All pricing is indicative and should be confirmed with your specific provider based on your clinical situation.

**Is SNEC or private eye clinic better?**
The correct choice depends on your specific clinical need, financial situation, and tolerance for waiting times. SNEC is unambiguously superior for: rare or complex eye diseases requiring subspecialty depth not available in private practice; clinical trial eligibility; and cost-sensitive patients who qualify for government subsidies. Private eye clinics are better for: faster appointment access; patients requiring LASIK or elective refractive surgery (which SNEC does not provide); patients who value continuity of care with the same ophthalmologist; and patients with chronic conditions requiring frequent monitoring who want the flexibility and consistency of a private practice relationship. For routine cataract surgery, glaucoma management, and retinal disease, the clinical quality difference between a well-regarded private specialist and SNEC is less significant than is commonly assumed — the key is that your treating doctor holds SMC specialist registration in ophthalmology and has genuine subspecialty experience in the condition being treated.
