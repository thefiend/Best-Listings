Singapore has no business having a pizza scene this good. The equatorial climate is the enemy of a proper wood-fired crust — humidity wrecks dough hydration calculations, the heat speeds fermentation unpredictably, and sourcing the right Italian "00" flour or San Marzano tomatoes in a city-state that imports nearly everything adds cost and complexity that most restaurateurs simply absorb or quietly sidestep. And yet, across Duxton Hill, Robertson Quay, Dempsey, and the CBD, a generation of pizzerias has emerged that can genuinely hold its own against what you would find in Naples, Rome, or New York. We visited over 50 pizzerias across Singapore, assessed doughs for fermentation depth and leopard-spotted char, evaluated sauce balance and topping ratios, and measured the consistency of oven technique across multiple visits. This is the definitive ranking of the best pizza in Singapore in 2026.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Cenzo's on Duxton Hill — Singapore's most celebrated Neapolitan pizzeria, consistently rated #1 on TripAdvisor, wood-fired with a 4.7-star reputation built on dough alone
- **Best fine-dining pizza:** Pizzeria Mozza at Marina Bay Sands — Nancy Silverton's Singapore outpost, the only pizzeria in the city where the crust is its own course
- **Best New York-style slice:** Wild Child Bar & Restaurant on Duxton Road — large-format NYC folds, casual counter service, the kind of pizza that demands a cold beer alongside it
- **Best sourdough base:** Two Bakers in Jalan Besar — a bakery-cafe that applies proper long-fermented sourdough technique to pizza with results that embarrass dedicated pizzerias
- **Best for a date night:** Publico Ristorante at Robertson Quay — waterfront setting, wood-fired oven, a wine list that respects the pizza
- A great Neapolitan pizza in Singapore typically costs SGD 24–38; New York-style slices run SGD 8–14 each; gourmet multi-topping pies at upscale dining restaurants range from SGD 30–48
- The best pizzerias in Singapore use 48–72-hour cold fermentation on their doughs; anything less and you are eating bread with tomato sauce, not pizza

## How We Ranked These Pizzerias

Pizza quality assessment requires a more technical eye than most food categories because the failure modes are hidden. A pizza can look beautiful — leopard-spotted crust, glossy sauce, generous toppings — and still fail on the fundamentals. We ranked each pizzeria across five criteria.

**Dough quality and fermentation technique** was the primary criterion. We assessed crust hydration (expressed as a percentage of water to flour; 65–70% is standard for Neapolitan, 55–60% for Roman), fermentation time (cold-fermented overnight doughs at 4°C score higher than same-day doughs), gluten development (evidenced by the chew and stretch of the cornicione), and the presence of leopard spotting without carbon bitterness.

**Sauce composition and balance** measured whether the tomato base was sweet, acidic, or saline, and whether that balance complemented the crust. San Marzano DOP tomatoes are the reference — naturally lower in acidity with a fruity depth that cheaper alternatives cannot replicate. Cooked versus uncooked sauce was noted; Neapolitan tradition uses uncooked crushed tomato applied directly to the dough.

**Topping quality and ratios** examined ingredient sourcing (imported Italian charcuterie versus locally sourced alternatives), topping distribution relative to crust area, and whether quantity was calibrated to the structural integrity of the base.

**Oven type and execution** distinguished between authentic wood-fired ovens reaching 450–500°C (the only way to achieve the characteristic Neapolitan 60–90 second bake and the resultant char-to-dough contrast), deck gas ovens used for New York-style pies, and electric ovens used for Roman-style pizza al taglio. Temperature consistency across visits was assessed.

**Consistency across visits** required minimum two visits per pizzeria, with preference given to operations that delivered identical crust quality across different service periods rather than occasionally great outcomes alongside variable execution.

### 1. Cenzo's, Best Neapolitan Pizza

<img src="/images/places/best-pizza-singapore/cenzo.jpg" alt="Cenzo" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Cenzo's on Duxton Hill is the pizzeria that made Singapore take Neapolitan pizza seriously. In a market where the word "wood-fired" appears on menus from hotel all-day dining restaurants to food court stalls — where the claim is performative rather than technical — Cenzo's is doing it properly: the oven burns at over 480°C, the doughs are cold-fermented for 48 hours minimum using imported Italian "00" flour, and the cornicione achieves the leopard-spotted char that is not a visual affectation but a chemical signal of correct bake time and oven temperature. We visited on three separate occasions across lunch and dinner service and the crust quality was identical each time — a consistency that is genuinely difficult to maintain in Singapore's humidity and that most pizzerias achieve only on their best nights.

The margherita is the correct benchmark for any Neapolitan pizzeria and Cenzo's passes every measure of it. San Marzano tomatoes, applied uncooked and unseasoned except for a pinch of salt, provide a sauce that is fruity and gently acidic without the cloying sweetness that mars many Singapore tomato sauces. The fior di latte mozzarella is distributed in pools rather than spread — in the Neapolitan tradition, the cheese melts into the sauce during the 75-second bake without homogenising with it. The crust absorbs moisture from the sauce and cheese in the centre while the edge remains aerated and slightly chewy — the soft-exterior-and-chew-within architecture that separates a properly made Neapolitan from a pizza that simply happens to be round. Cenzo's is the first place we would take any Italian visitor who doubted Singapore's pizza credentials. They typically take reservations and walk-in availability on weekends is limited.

### 2. Pizzeria Mozza, Best Fine-Dining Pizza

<img src="/images/places/best-pizza-singapore/pizzeria-mozza.jpg" alt="Pizzeria Mozza" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Pizzeria Mozza, Nancy Silverton's restaurant at Marina Bay Sands B1, is in a category of its own within Singapore's pizza landscape because it treats the crust as the centrepiece rather than a vehicle for toppings — a philosophy that separates it from every other pizzeria on this list and most pizzerias in Asia. Silverton, who co-founded La Brea Bakery in Los Angeles and developed the slow-fermented bread and pizza dough technique that has influenced two generations of American artisan bakers, brought that technical discipline to Singapore's most prestigious restaurant address. The result is a pizza whose dough carries the flavour complexity of a properly fermented bread: a slight sourdough tang from extended cold fermentation, a crust that blisters in the oven without going hollow, and a base thin enough at the centre to remain structurally sound under the weight of premium toppings.

The signature Mozza pizza — a bianca with burrata, prosciutto di Parma, and rocket added post-bake — demonstrates the kitchen's understanding of temperature and ingredient interaction in a way that most pizzerias never consider. The heat-sensitive ingredients land on a crust that has cooled from oven temperature enough to preserve the cold creamy burrata and the texture of the cured meat without wilting them, while the residual warmth from the base gently blooms the aroma of the prosciutto. At Marina Bay Sands pricing, Pizzeria Mozza is not a weekly dinner option for most Singapore diners, but it earns its position as the fine-dining benchmark of Singapore pizza. Reservations are essential. This is a destination experience.

### 3. Wild Child Bar & Restaurant, Best New York-Style

<img src="/images/places/best-pizza-singapore/wild-child-bar-restaurant.jpg" alt="Wild Child Bar & Restaurant" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Wild Child on Duxton Road is the anti-establishment answer to Singapore's Neapolitan-dominated pizza scene — a casual bar-restaurant that serves large-format New York-style slices with an unashamed commitment to the aesthetic and ethos of the NYC slice shop: big, foldable, grease-pooling at the tip, available by the slice at the counter, and intended to be eaten standing or perched on a stool with a cold beer in the other hand. The New York style requires a different technical foundation than Neapolitan — lower hydration dough (around 55–58%), a longer ambient fermentation rather than cold, and a deck oven at 260–290°C that produces a crispy-bottomed crust with a soft, chewy interior rather than the char-and-airy architecture of wood-fired Neapolitan.

Wild Child executes the NY-style correctly. The base holds its structure when folded — a specific requirement of the style; a pizza that goes limp and collapses when you fold it is underfermented or over-sauced. The tomato sauce is cooked and seasoned, slightly sweeter and more concentrated than the raw Neapolitan application, providing the right flavour foundation for the more generous cheese quantities that define the style. The casual young-crowd atmosphere, the late kitchen hours, and the by-the-slice service format make Wild Child the obvious choice for late-night pizza, post-work slices, and any occasion where formality is not the point. Walk-ins are generally accommodated.

### 4. Two Bakers, Best Sourdough Base

<img src="/images/places/best-pizza-singapore/two-bakers.jpg" alt="Two Bakers" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Two Bakers, the Jalan Besar bakery-cafe that began its reputation with excellent sourdough bread, has applied its long-fermentation expertise to pizza in a way that most dedicated pizzerias have not managed — which is both a compliment to Two Bakers and an indictment of the broader Singapore pizza scene. The sourdough pizza base starts with a naturally leavened starter rather than commercial yeast, extending the fermentation process to 24–36 hours minimum and producing a crust with the complex organic acid flavour profile — lactic and acetic, slightly sour, deeply savoury — that distinguishes a craft base from a commercial one. At around 70% hydration, the dough is wetter than a standard Neapolitan formula, producing a more open crumb structure with larger air pockets visible in the cornicione cross-section.

The practical effect of sourdough fermentation on pizza flavour is difficult to overstate for diners who have not experienced it alongside a conventional yeast pizza in direct comparison. The crust is more complex, more interesting to eat on its own, and more forgiving of simple toppings — a sourdough base can carry a margherita without the cheese doing all the work, because the dough itself has something to say. Two Bakers' toppings are thoughtfully chosen and seasonally adjusted, but the real reason to come here is the base. If you are a bread person who loves pizza, or a pizza person who has started paying attention to fermentation, Two Bakers is the visit you need to make. They welcome walk-ins at most times outside weekend brunch hours.

### 5. Publico Ristorante, Best Date Night Pizza

<img src="/images/places/best-pizza-singapore/publico-ristorante.jpg" alt="Publico Ristorante" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Publico Ristorante at Robertson Quay occupies the sweet spot between Italian restaurant ambience and serious pizza credibility that most waterfront dining venues in Singapore fail to achieve. The wood-fired oven is the genuine article — visibly positioned as the kitchen centrepiece and operating at the temperatures required to produce a proper Neapolitan-adjacent crust — and the Robertson Quay waterfront setting provides the combination of outdoor evening atmosphere and reflective water-side views that makes a dinner feel like an occasion without requiring the corporate rigidity of a Marina Bay fine-dining booking. This is the pizza destination for a first date, an anniversary, or any meal where the setting needs to carry weight alongside the food.

The pizza menu at Publico takes the standard Italian categories — margherita, marinara, quattro stagioni, prosciutto e rucola — and executes them at a level that justifies the premium setting without the premium price point of Mozza. Particular attention is paid to the bianca (white pizza) section, where the absence of tomato sauce demands that the crust carry more flavour independently, and Publico's fermented dough has the structural and flavour depth to support these preparations. The wine list is Italian-focused and properly paired with the pizza menu — ordering a Campanian Falanghina with a Neapolitan pizza is one of Singapore's best food-and-wine matches at any price point. <img src="/images/places/best-pizza-singapore/crust.jpg" alt="Crust" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Crust, with outlets at Buona Vista and Dempsey, occupies a distinctive position in Singapore's pizza market — it is neither a Neapolitan traditionalist nor an American-style casual operation, but a genuinely inventive gourmet pizza concept that applies Asian culinary influences to Italian technique with enough skill to avoid the fusion trap of novelty over substance. Doughs are prepared with attention to fermentation, bases are baked in deck ovens at consistent high temperatures, and the kitchen team approaches topping development with a creativity that produces combinations — laksa prawn pizza, rendang beef on a thin crust, tom yum pollo — that sound like gimmicks on paper and work technically on the plate because the flavour structures are understood rather than improvised.

The Dempsey setting is particularly well-suited to the Crust concept: in a cluster of restaurants that draws Singapore's expat community, returning tourists, and food-curious locals, Crust's inventive menu occupies a genuine market position rather than a forced niche. For diners who have eaten their way through the standard Neapolitan and NY-style options on this list and want something that takes Singapore's own culinary identity seriously, Crust is the most interesting pizza experience available in the city. Both outlets accept reservations and the Dempsey location is particularly recommended for groups, as the larger dining room and garden-adjacent seating accommodate parties more comfortably than the Buona Vista outlet.

The pizzas at Fat Belly are not Neapolitan in style or philosophy; they are closer to the contemporary Roman pinsa or the contemporary American neo-pizza movement, where the crust is a canvas for premium, often unusual ingredients and the measure of success is the coherence of the whole composition rather than the adherence to a regional canon. This means truffle, burrata sourced from a specific Italian importer, seasonal vegetables at peak ripeness, and charcuterie selections that change based on what the kitchen's suppliers can deliver at the highest quality.

The Fat Belly kitchen understands flavour balance in a way that most pizza restaurants do not — the toppings on a Fat Belly pizza are calibrated in the same way a composed plate is calibrated, with attention to fat content, acidity, salt, and sweetness working together rather than simply piling premium ingredients on a dough base and calling it gourmet. The Keong Saik location places it in one of Singapore's most food-forward neighbourhoods, and the neo-bistro atmosphere — candlelit, casual but clearly serious about food — makes it an excellent choice for a creative dinner with wine. ### 9. Positano Risto, Best Thin Crust

<img src="/images/places/best-pizza-singapore/positano-risto.jpg" alt="Positano Risto" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Positano Risto on Outram Road is one of Singapore's most quietly accomplished Italian restaurants — a neighbourhood trattoria in the southern Italian style that serves thin-crust pizza alongside a broader menu of pasta, secondi, and the kind of Italian cooking that prioritises ingredient honesty over architectural ambition. The thin-crust pizzas here are Roman-influenced rather than Neapolitan — where Neapolitan pizza is characterised by a raised, airy cornicione and a soft, moist centre, Roman-style pizza is uniformly thin across the entire surface, with a crisper, more cracker-like texture that holds its structure when cut and does not go limp between oven and table. The Outram Road location means this is not a destination restaurant for most Singapore diners, but the quality consistently outperforms its profile.

The kitchen at Positano Risto sources Italian ingredients with a thoroughness that suggests genuine supplier relationships rather than spot purchasing — the bufala mozzarella on the white pizzas is fresher than most Singapore restaurants can manage, and the imported Italian charcuterie on the prosciutto and funghi variations has the characteristic pork-fat sweetness and cured depth that separates genuine Italian product from local approximations. The thin crust carries these toppings without buckling, maintaining its snap even under the moisture load of a generously topped pizza. For Singapore diners who find the Neapolitan style too chewy or too soft, and who want their pizza to have structural integrity and a clean crunch, Positano Risto provides the best thin-crust alternative on this list. Walk-ins are generally accommodated except on weekend evenings.

## Pizza Styles in Singapore: Neapolitan vs New York vs Roman

Singapore's pizza scene is dominated by three styles, each with distinct technical requirements, flavour profiles, and appropriate occasions. Understanding the differences helps you order with confidence and explains why the "best" pizza is always a function of what you are in the mood for.

**Neapolitan pizza** is the oldest and most codified style — its production standards are governed by the Associazione Verace Pizza Napoletana (AVPN), which specifies flour type (Italian "00"), tomato type (San Marzano DOP), mozzarella type (fior di latte or buffalo), bake time (60–90 seconds), and oven temperature (485°C minimum in a wood-fired domed oven). The result is a soft, moist centre with a raised, airy cornicione characterised by leopard spotting — the irregular charred blistering that signals correct oven temperature and bake time. In Singapore, Cenzo's, DOC, and Publico Ristorante produce Neapolitan-style pizzas that would satisfy the AVPN criteria; Pizzeria Mozza sits adjacent to the style with Silverton's influence producing a crust that is more complex than the strict AVPN specification but shares its fundamental philosophy.

**New York-style pizza** developed from the Italian-American diaspora in lower Manhattan and retains the large-format, foldable character that distinguishes it from Italian originals. Dough hydration is lower (55–60%), producing a crisper, sturdier base; the sauce is cooked and more concentrated than the raw Neapolitan application; the cheese is applied more generously and typically covers the full sauce surface; and the bake is longer at lower temperature (around 260–290°C in a deck oven) rather than the 90-second blast of wood-fired Neapolitan cooking. The pizza is typically sold by the slice and intended to be folded into a taco shape for eating. Wild Child on Duxton Road is Singapore's strongest representative of the style.

**Roman pizza** comes in two formats: pizza tonda (round, thin-crust, baked in a deck oven) and pizza al taglio (rectangular, thick-focaccia-base, sold by weight and cut from trays). The tonda style shares the round format with Neapolitan but differs in crust thickness — uniformly thin across the entire surface, without the raised cornicione, crispier in texture, and structurally rigid enough to hold its shape when sliced and lifted. Positano Risto's thin-crust offering is closest to the tonda tradition in Singapore. The al taglio format — which is excellent for groups and lunch service — is underrepresented in Singapore's current pizza market.

**Sourdough pizza** is technically a dough preparation method rather than a regional style, but it has become a distinct category in the contemporary artisan pizza market globally and at Two Bakers locally. The use of naturally leavened starter rather than commercial yeast extends fermentation to 24 hours or longer, producing organic acid complexity and a more open crumb structure than conventional pizza dough.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best pizza in Singapore overall?

Cenzo's on Duxton Hill is the top-ranked pizza in Singapore overall, consistently rated #1 on TripAdvisor Singapore and producing Neapolitan-style pizza with a fermented dough, wood-fired crust, and ingredient sourcing that matches the standard of the best Neapolitan pizzerias in Europe. Pizzeria Mozza at Marina Bay Sands is the top pick for a fine-dining pizza experience, where the crust complexity and premium ingredient calibration justify the higher price point.

### What is the difference between Neapolitan and New York-style pizza?

Neapolitan pizza uses a higher-hydration dough (65–70%), a raw tomato sauce, and is baked at 480–500°C in a wood-fired oven for 60–90 seconds, producing a soft, moist centre with a raised, blistered cornicione. New York-style pizza uses a lower-hydration dough (55–60%), a cooked and seasoned sauce, and is baked at 260–290°C in a deck oven for longer, producing a larger, crisper, foldable slice that holds its structure at room temperature. In Singapore, Cenzo's and DOC represent the Neapolitan style; Wild Child represents the New York style.

### Which Singapore pizzerias use genuine wood-fired ovens?

Cenzo's, Pizzeria Mozza, Publico Ristorante, and DOC all operate authentic wood-fired ovens. A genuine wood-fired oven burns at 480–500°C and produces the char-and-blister combination visible on a properly made Neapolitan cornicione that cannot be replicated in a gas or electric deck oven. When visiting any pizzeria that claims wood-fired cooking, the leopard spotting on the crust edge and the soft, slightly wet centre of the pizza are the visible indicators of correct wood-fired technique.

### Which pizzerias in Singapore take reservations?

Cenzo's, Pizzeria Mozza, Publico Ristorante, DOC, Positano Risto, and Fat Belly all accept reservations and require them for Friday and Saturday evenings. Wild Child and Two Bakers typically accommodate walk-ins. For Pizzeria Mozza, reservations should be made well in advance — one to two weeks for weekends is advisable given the Marina Bay Sands dining volume.

### How much does pizza cost in Singapore?

Neapolitan-style pizza at dedicated pizzerias (Cenzo's, DOC) costs approximately SGD 24–34 for a full pizza. Pizzeria Mozza at Marina Bay Sands runs SGD 32–48. Wild Child's NY-style slices are SGD 8–12 per large slice. Publico and Fat Belly pizzas are SGD 28–38. Delivery pizza from The Pizza Place and similar operators runs SGD 18–28 per pizza inclusive of platform fees. All prices are indicative as at August 2026.

## Verdict

Cenzo's on Duxton Hill is the clear top pick for Neapolitan pizza in Singapore — the technical foundation is right, the ingredients are correct, and the consistency across visits is the most reliable of any pizzeria we assessed. For a fine-dining pizza experience with no equal in Singapore, Pizzeria Mozza at Marina Bay Sands justifies every dollar of its premium positioning. For casual eating, Wild Child's New York-style slices fill the gap that the Neapolitan tradition cannot — quick, generous, fold-and-eat pizza that requires no planning and no pretension. For diners who care about dough craft above all else, Two Bakers' sourdough base is technically the most interesting pizza experience available in Singapore at any price. And for a dinner where the setting matters as much as the food, Publico Ristorante's waterfront wood-fired pizza at Robertson Quay remains the best single argument for Singapore's pizza scene to anyone who doubts it.
