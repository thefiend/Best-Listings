## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does Mitsubishi aircon repair cost in Singapore?

Common Mitsubishi aircon repair costs in Singapore range from SGD 80–150 for a gas top-up, SGD 80–120 for a capacitor replacement, SGD 150–250 for a fan motor replacement, SGD 200–450 for a PCB board replacement, and from SGD 600 for compressor repair or overhaul. Diagnostic fees for a technician callout are typically SGD 50–80, often waived if you proceed with the repair. Prices vary depending on the Mitsubishi model, refrigerant type (R22, R410A, R32), and whether GST is included in the quoted figure. Always request an itemised written quote before work begins.

### What are common Mitsubishi aircon fault codes and what do they mean?

Common Mitsubishi Electric fault codes include: **E6** (communication fault between indoor and outdoor unit, often caused by a loose or damaged wiring connection); **P8** (high-pressure protection trip, commonly caused by a blocked condenser coil, low refrigerant, or faulty high-pressure sensor); **U4** (outdoor unit fault, covering a range of issues from thermistor faults to inverter module failures); **E9** (indoor fan motor fault); **P6** (freeze protection, triggered by restricted airflow over the indoor coil or low refrigerant). Fault codes displayed on Mitsubishi Starmex systems use the same base set but may include multi-split-specific codes relating to inter-unit communication. Never approve parts replacement based on a fault code alone without a technician confirming the root cause.

### What is the difference between Mitsubishi Electric and Mitsubishi Starmex?

Mitsubishi Starmex is the residential split and multi-split system range marketed specifically in Singapore under the Mitsubishi Electric brand. The Starmex range covers standard split units (single indoor and outdoor unit), the Starmex system 2, 3, 4, and 5 multi-split systems (multiple indoor units connected to a single outdoor unit), and the higher-end Kirigamine and Zensho series. All Starmex units are manufactured by Mitsubishi Electric and use the same inverter technology and fault code framework. When booking repair, confirming whether you have a standard Starmex split, a multi-split system, or a commercial Mitsubishi Electric unit helps the technician arrive with the correct tools and parts.

### Will Mitsubishi aircon repairs void my warranty?

Mitsubishi Electric Singapore's standard residential warranty covers parts and labour for 1 year from installation, with a 5-year compressor warranty for registered units. Work involving the refrigerant circuit must be performed by NEA-registered companies using approved refrigerant types to comply with Singapore regulations and preserve warranty coverage. Non-authorised repairs, particularly gas top-ups using incorrect refrigerant or PCB replacements using non-genuine parts, can void the warranty for the affected components. If your unit is within warranty, contact the authorised Mitsubishi Electric service agent listed on your warranty card before booking an independent repair company.

### How do I know if my Mitsubishi aircon needs repair or just servicing?

Your Mitsubishi unit needs repair rather than servicing if: it displays a fault code or error light that does not clear after a power cycle; it is blowing warm air despite a recent chemical wash or general service; it trips the circuit breaker repeatedly; it makes unusual noises such as grinding, rattling, or high-pitched squealing; or it starts normally but shuts down within 30 minutes. These symptoms indicate a component fault rather than a maintenance issue. If the unit cools adequately but is less efficient than expected, smells musty, or has a blocked drain, standard servicing (general service or chemical wash) is the appropriate first step.

### How long does a Mitsubishi aircon repair take?

Most common Mitsubishi aircon repairs take 1–2 hours on-site. Capacitor replacement, gas top-up, fan motor replacement, and leak sealing are typically completed in a single visit once parts are confirmed available. PCB board replacement can extend to 2–3 hours if the technician needs to source the board from their supplier and return. Compressor repair or replacement on a Mitsubishi Starmex multi-split system is a half-day job. Diagnostic assessment alone takes 15–30 minutes and should precede any parts quotation.

### Can I repair a Mitsubishi aircon myself?

Refrigerant handling is regulated in Singapore under the Environmental Protection and Management Act. Only NEA-registered personnel are permitted to handle, recover, or recharge refrigerant gases in air conditioning systems. DIY gas top-ups are illegal. Electrical repairs such as capacitor replacement are technically possible for those with electrical knowledge but carry safety risks with live components in a confined space. Cleaning the air filter and external casing is safe for homeowners to do themselves. All other Mitsubishi aircon repair work should be handled by a qualified technician.

### How do I find a Mitsubishi authorised service agent in Singapore?

Mitsubishi Electric Singapore maintains a list of authorised service agents on their official website. Authorised agents have received factory training on Mitsubishi Electric products and are able to perform warranty-covered repairs. For residential Starmex units, the authorised service centre contact details are typically printed on the installation certificate and warranty card provided at the time of installation. If these documents are not available, contact Mitsubishi Electric Singapore directly via their customer service hotline to confirm current authorised agents in your area.

### What refrigerant type does my Mitsubishi aircon use?

Mitsubishi Electric Starmex units installed in Singapore from 2010 onwards predominantly use R410A refrigerant. Newer models introduced from 2020 use R32, which has a lower global warming potential. Older units installed before 2010 may use R22 (now being phased out under the Montreal Protocol). The refrigerant type is printed on the nameplate of the outdoor unit. Using the wrong refrigerant type is dangerous, can damage the compressor, and is non-compliant with Singapore regulations. Always confirm the refrigerant type with the technician before any gas top-up is performed.
