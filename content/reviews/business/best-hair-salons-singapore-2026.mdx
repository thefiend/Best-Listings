Singapore's hair salon industry is one of the most competitive and consumer-driven service markets in the country. Walk through Orchard Road on any weekend and the density of hair salons: Korean concept boutiques, Japanese minimalist studios, mid-range local chains, and premium private ateliers: reflects a population that takes hair care seriously and has abundant choice in how and where it invests. The market has expanded significantly over the past decade, driven partly by the influence of Korean and Japanese beauty culture, which introduced to mainstream Singaporean consumers new colouring techniques, scalp treatment protocols, and styling approaches that local and Western-trained salons had not historically offered.

The salon landscape broadly divides into four categories. Korean hair salons, represented strongly on Orchard Road and in the Bugis corridor, are characterised by natural-light colouring, hair skin care philosophy, and highly Instagram-conscious styling. Japanese hair salons bring precision cutting technique, structured perming expertise, and an emphasis on healthy hair texture above decorative effect. Local independent salons: many of them built by individual master stylists with loyal client followings: tend to offer a more personal experience with a style philosophy that reflects the stylist's own training and aesthetic. Premium private salons and atelier-style studios occupy the top of the market, offering bespoke consultations, exclusive products, and an elevated service environment distinct from high-street competitors.

Services in the Singapore hair salon market cover the full spectrum: precision haircuts from wash-and-cut to technical dry-cut and scissor-over-comb; colouring services including balayage, highlights, ombré, Korean-style natural dyeing, bleach-and-tone, and full colour correction; treatments covering Japanese or Korean hair rebonding, digital perming, hair botox, scalp treatments, and keratin smoothing; and styling services for special occasions including weddings and formal events. The price range across these services varies enormously: a basic haircut at a neighbourhood salon runs SGD 20–40, while a full colour correction at a premium Orchard Road studio can run SGD 500 and above.

When evaluating which hair salon in Singapore is right for your needs, the key factors to assess are: stylist skill level and specialisation (a colourist who excels at blonde correction may not be the right choice for a technical rebond); service range (not every salon offers the full spectrum, and some are specialists); the salon's portfolio (look for photos of actual client results, not stock imagery); booking experience and responsiveness; and value for money relative to the service level delivered. Google review count and rating remain the most reliable public indicators of consistent quality: a salon with hundreds of real reviews is far more verifiable than one with ten five-star entries.

For this ranking of the best hair salons in Singapore 2026, we sorted by Google review count as the primary factor (reflecting real booking volume and breadth of client feedback) and Google rating as the secondary tiebreaker. We then assessed each salon's documented service range, specialisation, stylist calibre as evidenced by client reviews, and the quality of the booking experience.

## How We Ranked These Hair Salons

Our methodology for ranking the best hair salon singapore 2026 applied the following criteria in order of weight. Google review count (primary): review volume reflects real booking frequency across a broad client base and is the most reliable indicator of market-proven, consistent performance: a salon with thousands of reviews has been trusted with a vastly larger and more diverse sample of clients than one with a handful. Google rating (secondary tiebreaker): all salons on this list hold a minimum 4.8 rating, reflecting a quality floor above which we then distinguished by review volume. Stylist skill and specialisation: assessed through patterns in client reviews, noting consistent mentions of specific services done exceptionally well versus single-service specialists. Service range: salons offering the full spectrum of cut, colour, treatment, and styling services ranked above narrow specialists for general recommendations. Atmosphere and environment: client feedback on salon cleanliness, ambience, consultation quality, and overall service experience was assessed. Booking experience: responsiveness, ease of appointment management, and transparent pricing practices were evaluated. Value for money: assessed relative to the service level and location tier: a neighbourhood salon and an Orchard Road studio are not priced comparably, but each must deliver value commensurate with its positioning. All data reflects conditions as of August 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a haircut cost in Singapore in 2026?**
Haircut pricing in Singapore covers a wide range depending on salon tier, stylist seniority, and location. A basic wash-and-cut at a neighbourhood or heartland salon runs SGD 20–45 for women and SGD 15–35 for men. Mid-tier salons in suburban malls and boutique independent studios typically charge SGD 50–100 for women's cuts and SGD 40–70 for men's. Premium Orchard Road salons and private ateliers charge SGD 100–200 and above for a women's cut and style, with senior or principal stylists commanding SGD 150–300+. Colouring adds significantly to the total cost: a single-process colour starts around SGD 80–120, balayage or highlights range from SGD 150–350, and complex colour corrections can exceed SGD 400 at premium salons. Treatment services: rebonding, digital perming, hair botox, scalp treatments: range from SGD 80 for basic conditioning to SGD 400+ for Japanese rebonding at premium studios. Always confirm the full price before the appointment begins.

**What is the difference between a Japanese, Korean, and local hair salon in Singapore?**
Japanese hair salons are distinguished by their precision cutting technique, which emphasises the structural weight and fall of the hair as it grows out, not just at the point of cutting. Japanese stylists are typically trained in scissor technique for extended periods before working independently, and Japanese salons are known for structured perming, digital wave techniques, and scalp treatment protocols rooted in the Japanese approach to hair health. Korean hair salons bring a colouring-first philosophy that treats colour selection as a skin-tone matching exercise and an emphasis on hair skin care as an integrated part of every service. Korean salons introduced techniques like natural-finish balayage, hair botox, and hair care-infused colour processes to the Singapore market and have significantly shifted consumer expectations. Local Singapore salons span the full range from hawker-centre neighbourhood shops charging SGD 15 for a trim to independent master-stylist studios with international training backgrounds. The best local salons often combine technical training from Japan or Korea with a deep understanding of the textures and styling needs specific to Singaporean and Asian hair.

**How often should I get a haircut or hair treatment in Singapore?**
For maintaining a haircut shape: whether a short men's cut, a precision bob, or a layered style: most stylists recommend a trim every six to eight weeks to preserve the structure before it grows out significantly. For longer styles being grown out, ten to twelve weeks between trims is workable with the understanding that ends may need a maintenance tidy. For colour-treated hair, root touch-ups or colour refresh appointments typically run six to eight weeks depending on the colour technique: full block colours show regrowth faster, while balayage and ombré techniques are designed to grow out naturally and may go twelve to sixteen weeks between full colour services. For chemical treatments: rebonding or digital perming: most salons in Singapore recommend a treatment interval of six to twelve months, as over-processing damages hair protein structure. Scalp treatments can be done monthly as a maintenance protocol; intensive treatments such as hair botox or keratin smoothing typically last three to five months depending on washing frequency and heat exposure. In Singapore's humid climate, the longevity of styling and smoothing treatments is often shorter than in temperate climates due to ambient moisture.

**What should I tell my hairstylist to get a good result?**
Clear communication before the scissors come out is the single most important factor in a successful salon visit. Bring reference images: multiple, if possible: showing both the style you want and styles you have had that you liked or disliked. Be specific about your lifestyle: how much time you spend styling at home, whether you use heat tools daily or barely at all, whether you swim regularly (chlorine affects colour dramatically), and whether your priority is low-maintenance or high-precision styling. Tell your stylist honestly about your hair history: previous chemical treatments, bleaching episodes, and the products you currently use all affect what is achievable in a single session. If cost is a concern, say so at the consultation stage: a skilled stylist will restructure the service plan to fit your budget rather than proceeding with a full service and presenting a surprising total at checkout. Finally, if you are unhappy with the result, say so before you leave: most reputable salons will offer to correct an issue on the day or at a follow-up appointment rather than leaving a dissatisfied client to write a negative review.
