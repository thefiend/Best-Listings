A landing page is not a condensed website or a styled homepage variant. It is a single-goal conversion asset, engineered to turn one specific type of visitor, arriving from one specific source, into one specific conversion event: an enquiry, a booked appointment, or a purchase. We ranked each provider on: verified Google Maps rating (minimum 4.4 stars required), review count and recency as a proxy for sustained client throughput, portfolio evidence of conversion-focused output rather than generic website delivery, technical performance capability evidenced by PageSpeed and Core Web Vitals benchmarks, inclusion of copywriting or conversion strategy in the service offering, tracking setup competence, and pricing transparency. It is a dedicated, campaign-specific page with a single call to action, no navigation to other pages on the site, persuasive copy matched precisely to the ad or search term that brought the visitor, and conversion tracking that proves whether the page is working. Providers that demonstrated a genuine understanding of this architecture scored markedly higher than those treating landing pages as simple small website projects.

## What to Look for When Hiring a Landing Page Design Service

**Single-goal architecture.** The agency must understand that a landing page has one call to action. If they propose adding navigation menus, multiple offers, or links to other pages, they are treating it as a small website, not a landing page. Every distraction reduces the conversion rate. The best providers will push back on client requests that add complexity without adding conversion value.

**Conversion copywriting included.** Most agencies in Singapore design pages and expect the client to supply the copy. This is backwards: the copy is the primary conversion driver, not the design. The design frames and presents the copy. If an agency does not include copywriting in their landing page service, ask specifically how the copy is produced and who writes it. Vague answers about "content included" that resolve to a template with placeholder text are a warning sign.

**Tracking setup and verification.** GA4 conversion tracking and Google Ads conversion tracking must be set up, tested, and verified before the page goes live. Ask specifically: will you set up and test GA4 event tracking and Google Ads conversion tracking as part of the project? If the answer is no, the client is flying blind on campaign performance from day one.

**PageSpeed and mobile performance.** Ask for a PageSpeed Insights score on a recent live project. Scores below 70 on mobile indicate performance problems that will increase bounce rates and reduce Google Ads Quality Scores, raising the cost per click for every campaign the page supports.

**Post-launch support and iteration.** A landing page is a hypothesis: the best way to improve conversion rates over time is to test variations. Ask whether the page is built in a way that supports modifications and A/B testing. Agencies that treat page delivery as the end of the engagement are not the right partner for businesses that want to improve campaign performance continuously.

### What is the difference between a landing page and a website homepage?

A landing page is a dedicated, campaign-specific page with a single call to action, no navigation to other parts of a website, and copy matched exactly to the search term or ad that brought the visitor. A homepage serves multiple audiences with multiple goals: it introduces the brand, provides navigation to all services, and handles first-time visitors who know nothing about the business. Sending paid traffic to a homepage almost always produces lower conversion rates than sending it to a dedicated landing page, because the homepage was not designed for the specific visitor intent that the campaign is targeting. Every paid campaign should have its own dedicated landing page.

### How long does it take to build a landing page in Singapore?

A straightforward conversion landing page with copywriting, design, development, and tracking setup typically takes seven to fourteen business days with a competent agency. Rush delivery of three to five days is possible with some providers for an additional fee. The main variable is how quickly the client can review and approve content: agencies waiting weeks for client approvals on copy and design cannot hit seven-day timelines. For campaign launches with a fixed date, brief the landing page at least three weeks in advance to allow for revisions without timeline pressure.

### Does my landing page need to be on a separate domain or my main website?

Landing pages for Google Ads campaigns work most effectively as subfolders of your main domain (e.g. yourdomain.com/campaign-name) because this approach inherits the domain's existing authority and trust signals, which can improve Quality Scores and reduce cost per click. Separate domains require building authority from scratch and are generally less efficient for Google Ads performance. Subdomains are treated by Google more like separate sites than subfolders. For most Singapore businesses, a subfolder on the main domain is the correct technical choice, and a good agency will build the page accordingly.

### What is a good conversion rate for a landing page in Singapore?

Conversion rates vary significantly by industry, offer, and traffic source. For Google Ads campaigns in Singapore across service-based industries, a well-designed landing page typically converts at four to eight percent. Pages converting below two percent usually have a structural problem: either the traffic audience does not match the page's offer, the copy is too generic, or the call to action is not visible on mobile. Pages converting above ten percent typically have a very specific, high-intent audience and a very low-friction offer such as a free consultation or audit. Always track conversion rate by traffic source: organic, paid search, and social traffic often convert at very different rates on the same page.

### Should I use a landing page builder like Unbounce or a custom-built page?

Landing page builders like Unbounce, Instapage, or Leadpages are appropriate for businesses that need to launch and test landing pages quickly without developer involvement, particularly for A/B testing at scale. Custom-built landing pages on your own domain are better for SEO, load faster on average, and do not carry ongoing SaaS subscription costs. For most Singapore SMEs running Google Ads, a custom-built page on their own domain is the more cost-effective long-term solution, provided the agency builds the page in a way that allows copy and design to be modified without requiring a full redevelopment.

### How do I know if my landing page is converting well?

Set up GA4 event tracking with a specific conversion event (form submission, phone number click, WhatsApp button click) and monitor the conversion rate weekly by traffic source. Connect your Google Ads account to GA4 so you can see cost per conversion by campaign and by keyword. Compare your cost per lead from landing page traffic against the cost per lead from homepage traffic: the improvement should be measurable within the first two to four weeks of a campaign. If you cannot see this data, the tracking is not set up correctly and needs to be fixed before any meaningful optimisation decisions can be made.

### What should be on a landing page for a Singapore service business?

A landing page for a Singapore service business should include: a headline that matches the search term or ad the visitor arrived from; a sub-headline that explains the primary benefit in one sentence; three to five bullet points addressing the most common objections or questions; a visible call to action (phone number, WhatsApp button, or form) above the fold on mobile; social proof (Google review rating, number of reviews, client logos, or a verified testimonial); a secondary call to action repeated at the bottom of the page; and on mobile, a sticky click-to-call bar at the bottom of the screen. Navigation menus, links to other pages, and multiple competing offers should be removed entirely.

### How often should I update my landing page?

Landing pages should be reviewed and updated whenever campaign performance data indicates a declining conversion rate, when a new offer or pricing structure is introduced, or when competitive landscape changes affect the relevance of the existing trust signals and value proposition. For active paid media campaigns, a quarterly review of the landing page against current conversion data is a reasonable minimum cadence. High-spend campaigns with significant cost-per-lead implications justify more frequent review cycles, potentially monthly.
