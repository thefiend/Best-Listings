## What to Look for When Choosing a Gynaecologist in Singapore

**Credentials and subspecialty training.** All private gynaecologists in Singapore must be registered with the Singapore Medical Council (SMC) and hold postgraduate O&G qualifications. Beyond that, look for Fellowship of the RCOG or equivalent. If you have a specific condition such as endometriosis, fibroids, or a high-risk pregnancy, verify that the specialist has subspecialty training or a documented focus in that area, not just general O&G experience.

**Google review volume and recency.** A 4.8+ rating with 50+ reviews is a reliable baseline for private gynaecologists in Singapore. More important than the average is recency: check that reviews have been posted in the past three months. A specialist with many reviews but nothing recent may have reduced their practice or changed their patient care model.

**Hospital affiliation.** For women who plan to deliver privately, the hospital matters as much as the specialist. Confirm that your chosen gynaecologist delivers at a hospital you are comfortable with. Mt Elizabeth, Mt Alvernia, Gleneagles, Thomson Medical Centre, and Parkway East are the primary private delivery hospitals in Singapore, and each has a different environment, pricing tier, and delivery suite standard.

**Consultation style.** A gynaecologist who takes time to explain diagnoses and treatment options is especially important for sensitive conditions like fertility challenges, endometriosis, or abnormal smear results. Send a brief enquiry via the clinic's contact form or call reception to gauge responsiveness before booking. Clinics that reply within 24 hours to initial queries typically reflect well-organised patient communication overall.

**Pricing transparency.** Consultation fees at private gynaecologists in Singapore range from SGD 150–350 for a first visit. Some clinics publish fee ranges; others require a call. Be cautious of clinics that are entirely opaque on fees, particularly for procedures like colposcopy, sonohysterography, or laparoscopy, where costs can reach SGD 3,000–15,000. Always ask for an itemised estimate before agreeing to any procedure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a gynaecologist consultation cost in Singapore?
A first consultation at a private gynaecologist in Singapore typically costs SGD 150–350, excluding investigations such as ultrasound scans (SGD 100–250) or blood tests. Subsequent follow-up visits are usually SGD 80–150. Subsidised rates are available at KKH Women's and Children's Hospital and SGH for Singapore Citizens and PRs with a GP referral.

### How do I choose the right gynaecologist in Singapore?
Start with your specific need. For routine check-ups and Pap smears, any reputable O&G clinic will suffice. For obstetrics, choose a specialist who delivers at a hospital you trust. For conditions like endometriosis, fibroids, or fertility issues, prioritise a specialist with documented subspecialty focus in that area. Check Google reviews for recency and specificity, and call the clinic to assess responsiveness.

### What is the difference between an obstetrician and a gynaecologist?
An obstetrician manages pregnancy, labour, and delivery. A gynaecologist manages the non-pregnant female reproductive system, covering conditions such as PCOS, fibroids, endometriosis, menopause, and abnormal bleeding. In Singapore, most private specialists are O&G-trained, meaning they are qualified in both disciplines, but individual practitioners often develop a stronger focus in one area over time.

### Are gynaecologists in Singapore regulated?
Yes. All doctors in Singapore, including gynaecologists, must be registered with the Singapore Medical Council (SMC). Private specialists practising at private hospitals must also hold hospital privileges granted by that specific hospital's credentialling committee. O&G specialists are additionally expected to hold the MRCOG or FRCOG from the Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists, or an equivalent qualification recognised by SMC.

### How far in advance should I book a private gynaecologist in Singapore?
For routine check-ups, two to four weeks in advance is generally sufficient. For antenatal care, book as early as possible, ideally in the first trimester (before 12 weeks). For surgical consultations or second opinions on complex conditions, top specialists can be booked out four to eight weeks ahead. Call the clinic as soon as you identify your need rather than waiting.

### What is the difference between a polyclinic and a private gynaecologist?
Polyclinics offer subsidised women's health services including Pap smears, breast examinations, and family planning for Singapore Citizens and PRs, but do not have O&G specialists on-site. For specialist-level care, you need a referral to a public hospital's O&G department or you can self-refer to a private gynaecologist. Private gynaecologists offer shorter waiting times, direct access without referral, and more time per consultation.

### What are red flags when choosing a gynaecologist in Singapore?
No SMC registration number on the clinic website. No hospital affiliation listed for a surgeon offering procedures. Reviews that are generic and lack clinical detail. Pressure to proceed with elective procedures at the first consultation without offering alternatives. Reluctance to provide a written quote for planned procedures. Clinics with very few reviews claiming to be "Singapore's top" specialists without verifiable credentials.

### Can I switch gynaecologists in Singapore?
Yes, there is no restriction on changing your gynaecologist at any point. Request a copy of your medical records from your current clinic, which they are legally required to provide. If you are mid-pregnancy, inform your new specialist at the first appointment so they can review your full obstetric history before taking over your care.

### Is Medisave claimable for gynaecologist visits in Singapore?
Medisave can be used for approved day surgery procedures performed by registered specialists at accredited facilities, including laparoscopy and hysteroscopy. Outpatient consultations are generally not Medisave-claimable unless they are part of a specific approved chronic disease management programme. Check with your clinic and CPF Board for the latest claimable procedure list before your appointment.

### What should I bring to my first gynaecologist appointment?
Bring your NRIC or passport, previous medical reports or scan results if any, a list of current medications, your menstrual cycle calendar if relevant, and any questions you want answered written down in advance. For an initial obstetric visit, also bring documentation of a positive pregnancy test and the date of your last menstrual period.
