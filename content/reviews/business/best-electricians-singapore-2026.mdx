Singapore's electrical services market is large, technically regulated, and consequential — a faulty wiring installation or an incorrectly wired distribution board (DB box) is not merely an inconvenience but a genuine fire and safety risk. The Energy Market Authority (EMA) governs all electrical work in Singapore under the Electricity Act, and the regulatory framework draws a clear distinction between work that any competent tradesperson can perform and work that legally requires a Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW). Understanding this distinction is the first step in choosing the right electrician singapore for your needs.

A Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW) is a certified professional licensed by EMA to carry out and supervise electrical installation works in Singapore. LEW certification is mandatory for any electrical work that connects to SP Group's power supply — this includes rewiring, DB box replacement or upgrades, installation of new power points and circuit breakers, consumer switch room work, and any electrical work in commercial and industrial premises. Residential work such as ceiling fan installation, light fitting replacement at existing points, and plug socket replacement at existing outlets can be carried out by a general electrical contractor, but any work touching the main distribution board or adding new circuits requires an LEW-licensed individual. When you engage an electrician singapore, confirming whether the job requires LEW certification is the correct first question.

The scope of electrical services singapore covers a wide range of residential, commercial, and industrial requirements. On the residential side, common jobs include ceiling fan installation and replacement, light fixture and LED downlight installation, additional power point installation, DB box upgrading for BTO and resale HDB flats, aircon circuit wiring, and fault diagnosis for tripping circuit breakers. For new BTO flat owners, a DB box upgrade is often one of the first major electrical jobs needed — the standard BTO power supply may not accommodate all appliances without an upgraded consumer unit. Emergency electrician singapore calls typically involve total power failure, burning smells from electrical fittings, repeatedly tripping circuit breakers, or sparking from outlets — situations that require same-day or immediate response rather than scheduled booking.

Pricing for electrical services singapore varies by job type, complexity, and urgency. Ceiling fan installation typically costs SGD 50–120 depending on existing wiring; DB box upgrading runs SGD 400–900 depending on the consumer unit specification; adding a new power point costs SGD 80–200 depending on trawling requirements; emergency call-out fees add SGD 80–200 on top of job costs for after-hours response. The rule for choosing an electrician singapore: verify their credentials match the job type, confirm they provide a written quotation before commencing work, and check that any LEW-required work is performed and signed off by a properly licensed individual.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does an electrician cost in Singapore 2026?**
Electrician pricing in Singapore varies significantly by job type, urgency, and time of call-out. For scheduled residential work: ceiling fan installation costs SGD 50–120 depending on whether new wiring is needed; replacing a ceiling fan motor is SGD 80–150; installing a new power point runs SGD 100–250 depending on trawling requirements; LED downlight installation per point is SGD 40–100 with wiring; a DB box consumer unit upgrade runs SGD 400–900 depending on the unit specification and number of circuits. For emergency electrician singapore call-outs after hours, weekends, and public holidays, expect a surcharge of SGD 80–200 on top of job costs. Wiring fault tracing typically costs SGD 80–200 for the diagnostic work alone, with repairs charged separately. Always request a written quotation before work begins — a reputable electrician singapore will provide this as standard practice.

**Do I need an EMA-licensed electrician in Singapore?**
Yes, for most electrical work beyond basic plug replacements. The Energy Market Authority (EMA) requires that a Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW) carry out and supervise any electrical installation work connected to the power supply in Singapore. This includes DB box (consumer unit) installation or modification, installation of new electrical circuits, rewiring, and most commercial electrical work. Residential cosmetic electrical work — replacing a like-for-like ceiling fan at an existing wiring point, or swapping a light bulb — does not require an LEW. However, installing a new ceiling fan where no existing wiring point exists, adding new power points, upgrading a DB box, or wiring a new aircon circuit all require a licensed electrician singapore operating under LEW certification. Always confirm whether your job requires LEW sign-off and ask the electrician singapore to confirm their licensing status before work begins.

**What are common electrical emergencies in Singapore?**
The most common electrical emergencies requiring immediate electrician singapore response include: total power failure where the main switch cannot be reset; repeatedly tripping circuit breakers that cannot be isolated to a specific appliance; burning smell from a power point, switch, or electrical panel; sparking from an outlet or electrical fitting; visible scorch marks around switches or power points; flickering lights accompanied by a burning smell; and water ingress into electrical fittings after a pipe burst or ceiling leak. All of these situations present genuine fire or electrocution risk and should not be investigated by an untrained homeowner. For any electrical emergency in Singapore, the correct response is to switch off the main breaker at the DB box if it is safe to do so, avoid using the affected circuits, and contact an emergency electrician singapore with 24-hour availability. SK ELECTRICAL, LOTUS ELECTRICAL ENGINEERING, and AVV Electricals all offer around-the-clock emergency response.

**Can I do my own electrical work in Singapore?**
Very limited electrical work can be performed by a non-licensed person in Singapore. Under EMA regulations, a homeowner may replace a like-for-like plug or socket at an existing wiring point, replace a light bulb, or reset a tripped circuit breaker at the DB box. All other electrical work — installing new wiring, adding circuits, modifying the DB box, installing new power points, connecting new appliances to dedicated circuits, and any commercial electrical work — must be performed or supervised by a licensed electrician singapore holding EMA's Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW) certification. DIY electrical work beyond these narrow boundaries is illegal in Singapore and may void your home insurance, create a fire hazard, and expose you to personal liability if damage or injury results. The cost of engaging a licensed electrician singapore for most residential jobs is modest relative to the risk of unlicensed self-installation — engaging a professional is both the legally correct and practically safer approach.
