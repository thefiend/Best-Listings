Dermatology consultation and treatment costs vary based on individual assessment, diagnosis, the complexity of your skin condition, and the clinic's pricing structure. Always consult directly with a MOH-registered dermatologist for an accurate quotation and personalised treatment plan. Dermatological care involves medical evaluation that can only be properly conducted through an in-person consultation with a qualified specialist.

Singapore's tropical climate — persistent heat, humidity above 80%, and year-round UV exposure — creates an environment that is uniquely demanding on skin. Conditions that might be minor inconveniences in temperate climates become chronic management challenges in Singapore: eczema flares triggered by sweat and heat, acne worsened by excess sebum production, fungal infections thriving in moist conditions, and accelerated photoageing from cumulative UV damage. Choosing the right dermatologist matters.

The distinction between a **MOH-registered dermatology specialist** and a general practitioner offering skin treatments is significant and frequently misunderstood. A dermatologist in Singapore holds a specialist registration with the Singapore Medical Council (SMC) in dermatology — requiring at least four years of postgraduate specialist training following their basic medical degree. A GP offering "skin treatments" does not hold this qualification. For chronic skin conditions, complex diagnoses, or resistant acne and eczema, the depth of training of a qualified specialist makes a measurable clinical difference. We evaluated 30+ clinics before shortlisting these 10.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** National Skin Centre (NSC) — Singapore's only government specialist dermatology centre, with the most comprehensive range of dermatology subspecialties and subsidised rates for Singapore citizens and PRs
- **Best private practice:** Dr Ong Dermatology & Aesthetic Clinic, Orchard — a highly rated specialist private practice with 4.4 stars, covering the full range of medical and aesthetic dermatology

- Red flag: any clinic that diagnoses and prescribes treatment for chronic skin conditions without a physical examination, or that markets "dermatologist-grade" treatments without a registered specialist on site

### 1. National Skin Centre (NSC), Best Overall

<img src="/images/places/best-dermatologist-singapore/national-skin-centre-nsc.jpg" alt="National Skin Centre (NSC)" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

The National Skin Centre on Mandalay Road is Singapore's only dedicated government dermatology specialist centre and the reference institution for dermatological care in the country. NSC is a specialist outpatient clinic under the National Healthcare Group, staffed by a full faculty of SMC-registered dermatologists covering every dermatology subspecialty: adult medical dermatology, paediatric dermatology, contact dermatitis, photobiology and phototherapy, dermatological surgery, hair and nail disorders, and laser and aesthetic dermatology. No private clinic in Singapore can replicate this breadth of subspecialty expertise within a single institution.

NSC's most significant advantage for the majority of Singaporean patients is cost. Subsidised consultation fees are a fraction of private specialist rates, and treatments including narrowband UVB phototherapy — the gold-standard treatment for psoriasis, eczema, and vitiligo — are available at NSC with subsidy, whereas most private clinics do not have phototherapy equipment at all. For patients with complex chronic conditions such as severe psoriasis, bullous pemphigoid, or cutaneous lymphoma, NSC's access to the latest biologics, clinical trials, and multidisciplinary oncology teams is unmatched by any private provider.

The acknowledged limitation of NSC is waiting time. Subsidised new patient appointments regularly carry waits of four to eight weeks. For patients with urgent presentations — suspected skin cancer, acute urticaria requiring investigation, or rapidly progressing rashes — private specialist appointments will be faster. NSC does offer priority appointments for urgent cases, and their specialist emergency service handles acute inpatient dermatology. For any patient who is a Singapore citizen or PR with a non-urgent chronic skin condition, NSC is the most clinically comprehensive and cost-effective choice in the country.

## Dermatologist vs GP for Skin Conditions — When to See a Specialist

General practitioners in Singapore receive basic dermatology training as part of their medical degree and can diagnose and manage common skin conditions: mild acne, minor fungal infections, simple rashes, and routine eczema. This is appropriate and cost-effective for many patients. However, there are clear clinical situations where GP-level care is insufficient and a MOH-registered dermatology specialist is needed.

**See a dermatologist if:**
- Your skin condition has not responded to two or more courses of GP treatment
- You have moderate to severe acne, particularly with scarring or cystic nodules that require isotretinoin (a specialist-only prescription in Singapore)
- You have a chronic skin condition including psoriasis, atopic dermatitis, rosacea, or hidradenitis suppurativa that requires long-term management and potential immunosuppressive therapy
- You have a suspicious lesion — changing mole, new growth, bleeding or non-healing sore — that requires dermoscopic examination and potentially skin biopsy for histological diagnosis
- You have an occupational or contact skin problem that requires patch testing to identify specific allergens
- You have significant hair loss or a nail disorder that has not been diagnosed or is not responding to GP treatment
- You have a widespread or severe acute rash, particularly if accompanied by systemic symptoms

**GP care is appropriate if:**
- You have mild, uncomplicated eczema that responds to standard emollient and topical corticosteroid treatment
- You have a straightforward fungal skin infection (tinea corporis, tinea pedis) that has not been previously treated
- You need a simple skin lesion removed (skin tag, sebaceous cyst) that is clearly benign and does not require histological assessment
- You are seeking a first assessment and referral — GPs can assess and refer to NSC under the polyclinic referral pathway, which provides access to subsidised NSC rates for eligible patients

The cost differential between a GP consultation (SGD 30–80) and a private dermatologist consultation (SGD 150–350) is real. For straightforward presentations, GP care is appropriate. For any condition that is persistent, severe, diagnostically uncertain, or not responding to treatment, the additional cost of specialist care is consistently justified by better outcomes and avoided months or years of ineffective treatment.

## Common Skin Conditions in Singapore's Tropical Climate

Singapore's combination of year-round heat, high humidity, intense UV radiation, and the physical stress of constant transitions between hot outdoor air and heavily air-conditioned indoor environments creates a skin environment unlike temperate climates. Understanding which conditions are most prevalent helps patients seek appropriate care.

**Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema).** Singapore has among the highest prevalence rates of atopic dermatitis in Asia. The combination of heat, sweat, dust mites (which thrive in tropical humidity), and air-conditioning-driven skin barrier disruption makes eczema both more common and harder to control in Singapore than in temperate countries. Specialist management is frequently required for moderate to severe cases.

**Acne.** High humidity increases sebum production and creates an environment where acne-causing bacteria proliferate more readily. Acne in Singapore tends to be more inflammatory than in cooler climates, and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation — the dark marks that persist after acne heals — is more pronounced in Asian skin types due to higher baseline melanin density.

**Fungal Infections.** Tinea versicolor (a yeast-driven pigmentation change), tinea pedis (athlete's foot), and tinea cruris (groin ringworm) are significantly more prevalent in Singapore than in temperate climates. The warm, moist conditions that persist year-round create ideal conditions for dermatophyte and yeast proliferation.

**Melasma and Pigmentation.** Chronic UV exposure in Singapore's equatorial sun drives melasma — hormonally triggered facial pigmentation — and other forms of post-inflammatory and sun-induced hyperpigmentation. Melasma is particularly challenging to treat in Singapore because UV trigger avoidance, a key management pillar, is difficult in an environment with year-round intense sun exposure.

**Psoriasis.** While psoriasis prevalence is not higher in Singapore than globally, the tropical climate creates specific management challenges: sweating can trigger flares, topical treatments may be uncomfortable in heat, and the social visibility of plaque psoriasis in a climate where short sleeves and shorts are the norm adds to disease burden. Access to phototherapy and modern biologics through NSC or specialist private practice significantly improves quality of life for psoriasis patients.

**Contact Dermatitis.** Singapore's diverse occupational environment — food handling, healthcare, construction, and beauty sectors — creates high rates of occupational contact dermatitis. Accurate diagnosis through specialist patch testing is essential for identifying specific allergens and preventing chronic recurrence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a dermatologist cost in Singapore?**
At the National Skin Centre under subsidy, consultation fees for Singapore citizens and PRs are significantly lower than private rates — typically SGD 30–80 for a subsidised specialist consultation. Private specialist dermatologist consultations range from SGD 150–350 for an initial consultation, with follow-ups at SGD 80–200. Treatment costs (laser procedures, phototherapy, biologic injections) are charged separately. Some Medisave-accredited procedures can be partially paid using Medisave. Foreign patients pay unsubsidised rates at NSC comparable to private clinic pricing.

**Do I need a referral to see a dermatologist?**
No referral is required to book a private specialist dermatologist appointment in Singapore — you can call or book online directly at any private dermatology clinic on this list. A referral is required to access subsidised rates at the National Skin Centre: polyclinic referrals qualify patients for subsidised NSC appointments. Without a polyclinic referral, NSC appointments are available but at unsubsidised rates. GPs at private clinics and specialist outpatient clinics can also refer to NSC for subsidised rates.

**What skin conditions does a dermatologist treat?**
SMC-registered dermatologists treat the full range of skin, hair, and nail conditions including: acne (including severe/cystic acne requiring isotretinoin), eczema and atopic dermatitis, psoriasis, rosacea, seborrhoeic dermatitis, fungal infections, skin infections (bacterial and viral including warts and molluscum), urticaria (hives), contact dermatitis, skin cancer (including melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, and squamous cell carcinoma), hair loss (alopecia areata, androgenetic alopecia, scarring alopecia), nail disorders, vitiligo, and hereditary skin disorders. Aesthetic dermatologists additionally treat pigmentation, acne scars, wrinkles, and skin laxity.

**Is National Skin Centre good?**
NSC is clinically excellent — it is the reference institution for dermatology in Singapore, staffed by SMC-registered specialists covering every dermatology subspecialty, and equipped with phototherapy and advanced treatment capabilities not available at most private clinics. For patients with complex or chronic conditions, or for patients eligible for subsidy who want the highest clinical standard at accessible cost, NSC is the best option in Singapore. The primary drawback is waiting time: new subsidised patient appointments can take four to eight weeks. Patients requiring urgent care, or who prioritise shorter waiting times and a more personalised private clinic experience, should consider private specialist practices. NSC is not a trade-off in clinical quality — it is a trade-off in convenience and appointment speed.

**How do I know if my clinic is MOH-registered?**
The Singapore Medical Council maintains a public register of all SMC-registered doctors, including their specialist registration status, at the MOH website (moh.gov.sg). Search for the specific dermatologist's name — confirm they hold a specialist registration in dermatology, not merely general medical registration. A clinic marketing "dermatologist services" or "dermatology treatments" without a named SMC-registered specialist in dermatology is not a dermatology specialist practice. The SMC register check takes under five minutes and is the most important step before booking a dermatology appointment.

The National Skin Centre is Singapore's best dermatology provider overall — no private practice can match its breadth of subspecialty expertise, its phototherapy and biologic treatment access, or its subsidised rate structure for citizens and PRs. For patients who need specialist care urgently, want a private clinic experience, or are seeking aesthetic as well as medical dermatology, Dr Ong Dermatology & Aesthetic Clinic is the highest-rated private specialist option. Whatever your skin concern, the single most important step is confirming that your dermatologist holds MOH specialist registration in dermatology — the credential that separates qualified specialists from the large number of non-specialist clinics competing for the same patients.
