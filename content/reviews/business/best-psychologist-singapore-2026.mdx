## How We Ranked These Psychologists and Counsellors

Our ranking of the best psychologists and counsellors in Singapore for 2026 applied the following methodology. Google review rating: all practices were required to hold a minimum rating of 4.5 stars; higher ratings were weighted positively in ordering. Google review volume: treated as a proxy for patient throughput, outcome consistency, and the breadth of the sample attesting to the practice's quality. A minimum of 50 verified Google reviews was required for inclusion. Practitioner qualifications: practices whose team members hold postgraduate psychology or counselling degrees from recognised institutions, and where credentials are transparent on the practice website, were weighted above those with limited qualification disclosure. Condition scope: practices addressing a range of presentations including anxiety, depression, trauma, relationships, OCD, grief, and child or adolescent psychology were weighted above single-issue specialists, unless the specialist depth was exceptional. Patient communication: how clearly the practice communicates fees, session structure, the therapeutic approach used, and what clients should expect was assessed from website content and review evidence. Accessibility: location, appointment availability, and whether online or teleconsultation options are offered were considered practical quality indicators. All data reflects conditions as of August 2026.

## What to Look for When Choosing a Psychologist in Singapore

Selecting a psychologist or counsellor in Singapore requires more due diligence than most service decisions because the quality, qualifications, and therapeutic approach of practitioners vary significantly across the private market. The following criteria should guide your evaluation before committing to a practice.

**Qualifications and registration.** Psychologists in Singapore are not regulated by statute in the same manner as doctors and dentists. The Singapore Psychological Society (SPS) maintains a voluntary register of psychologists, and the Allied Health Professions Council (AHPC) regulates specific allied health roles including some psychology-adjacent positions. Before booking, ask directly whether the practitioner holds a relevant postgraduate degree (Masters or Doctorate) in psychology or counselling from a recognised institution. Practitioners who cannot clearly answer this question should be regarded with caution.

**Therapeutic modality.** Different psychological approaches are supported by different evidence bases for different conditions. CBT has the strongest evidence base for anxiety disorders and depression. EFT and Gottman Method have strong evidence for couples therapy. ERP is the gold-standard for OCD. Trauma-focused CBT and EMDR have strong support for PTSD. Ask what therapeutic approach the practitioner uses for your specific presenting concern, and verify that it is evidence-based rather than eclectic to the point of incoherence.

**Specialisation versus generalist scope.** A generalist counsellor can effectively support many common presentations including stress, adjustment difficulties, grief, and mild to moderate anxiety and depression. For clinically significant or complex presentations including OCD, PTSD, bipolar disorder, personality disorders, and autism spectrum conditions, specialist expertise matters and should be verified before booking.

**Fees and transparency.** Private psychology in Singapore typically costs SGD 120–250 per session. Be cautious of practices that are reluctant to discuss fees openly, or that pressure new clients into committing to a large number of sessions before any assessment has been conducted. A reputable practice will discuss fees clearly, explain what a first assessment involves, and let you make an informed decision before committing.

**Online options.** Many Singapore psychology practices now offer teleconsultation or online therapy options. For clients with scheduling constraints, mobility issues, or who travel frequently, the availability of online sessions meaningfully increases the likelihood of consistent attendance: a critical factor in therapy outcomes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a psychologist, psychiatrist, and counsellor in Singapore?

A psychiatrist is a medical doctor (MBBS) who has specialised in psychiatry. Psychiatrists can diagnose psychiatric conditions, prescribe medications, and manage cases where pharmacological treatment is indicated. A psychologist holds a postgraduate degree (Masters or Doctorate) in psychology and is trained in psychological assessment and evidence-based therapy. Psychologists cannot prescribe medications in Singapore. A counsellor typically holds a diploma or postgraduate qualification in counselling and is trained to provide therapeutic support for common emotional and relational difficulties. The distinction matters when selecting a provider: if your presentation may require medication or formal psychiatric diagnosis, a psychiatrist is the appropriate first contact. For psychological assessment, complex clinical conditions, or evidence-based therapy, a psychologist is appropriate. For general counselling support, a qualified counsellor can be highly effective and is generally the most accessible and affordable option.

### How much does seeing a psychologist cost in Singapore?

Private psychology sessions in Singapore typically cost SGD 120–250 per session for a standard 50-minute therapy session. Initial consultations, which involve a more detailed assessment of your history and presenting concerns, may be priced higher. Psychiatrist consultations in the private sector are generally SGD 200–400 for an initial appointment. Some practices, including Singapore Counselling Centre, offer fee assistance or sliding-scale options for clients who cannot afford standard private rates. Medisave cannot generally be used for private psychology or counselling appointments. Some employer-provided employee assistance programmes (EAPs) and certain integrated shield plans include mental health coverage: check with your HR department and insurer before your first appointment.

### How do I know if I need a psychologist or a GP?

A GP (general practitioner) is the appropriate first point of contact if your mental health symptoms are new, severe enough to significantly impair daily functioning, or if you want to rule out a medical cause for your symptoms. GPs can assess for medical conditions that mimic anxiety or depression, can refer you to a psychiatrist or public-sector mental health services, and can prescribe medications for short-term management. A psychologist is appropriate if you have a specific psychological concern you want to address through therapy, if a GP or psychiatrist has already assessed you and recommended therapy, or if you are managing a long-standing condition and want structured psychological support. For presentations involving both medication and therapy, coordinating care between a psychiatrist (for medication management) and a psychologist (for therapy) produces the best outcomes.

### Are online therapy sessions as effective as in-person sessions?

For most common presentations including anxiety, depression, stress, grief, and relationship difficulties, online therapy delivered via video has evidence supporting comparable effectiveness to in-person sessions. The therapeutic alliance: the quality of the relationship between therapist and client: is the strongest predictor of therapy outcomes, and this alliance can be built effectively online. Online sessions offer practical advantages for clients with busy schedules, mobility limitations, or who travel frequently. For presentations requiring physical assessment (rare in psychology), or for clients who find it significantly harder to engage authentically on video, in-person sessions may be preferable. Ask your chosen practice whether online sessions are available, as most Singapore psychology practices now offer this option.

### How long does therapy typically take?

The duration of therapy depends on the nature and complexity of the presenting concern. Brief therapy for specific, well-defined concerns can produce meaningful change in six to twelve sessions. Common presentations such as mild to moderate anxiety and depression, grief, adjustment difficulties, and specific relationship issues often resolve or reach a stable point of management within twelve to twenty sessions over three to six months. Complex or long-standing presentations: chronic depression, PTSD, personality disorders, or longstanding relational patterns: typically require longer therapy, sometimes extending to a year or more of regular sessions. OCD treatment with ERP is often most effective when delivered intensively over twelve to twenty sessions with specific homework between sessions. A qualified therapist should discuss expected duration and review milestones with you after the initial assessment.

### Is therapy confidential in Singapore?

Yes. Professional psychology and counselling practices in Singapore are bound by confidentiality obligations as a core ethical standard. Information disclosed in therapy is not shared with third parties without the client's explicit consent, with specific exceptions required by law: these include situations where the client discloses an intention to harm themselves or others imminently, or where a court order requires disclosure. Practitioners should explain the limits of confidentiality at the start of the therapeutic relationship. Corporate EAP programmes typically provide therapy on a confidential basis, with employers receiving aggregate data only rather than individual case information. If confidentiality is a concern for you, ask your chosen practice directly about their confidentiality policy and the specific circumstances under which information might be disclosed.

### Can I claim insurance for psychology sessions in Singapore?

Medisave generally cannot be used for private psychology or counselling sessions under standard MOH provisions. Some integrated shield plans include mental health outpatient coverage: check your policy documents or call your insurer to verify what is covered and whether there are network provider requirements. Many employer-provided employee assistance programmes (EAPs) include a number of free or subsidised psychology or counselling sessions per year as part of their benefit package: contact your HR department to find out what your employer provides. For clients who need financial assistance beyond insurance, practices including Singapore Counselling Centre offer fee assistance options.

### What should I expect from my first psychology session?

A first psychology or counselling session is primarily an assessment and orientation appointment rather than the beginning of deep therapeutic work. The practitioner will typically ask about what brought you to therapy, your relevant background and history, your goals for therapy, and any previous experience of psychological support. You should expect to spend most of this session providing information rather than receiving it. At the end of the session, a well-qualified practitioner will share their initial understanding of your situation and propose a therapeutic approach or next steps, giving you an informed basis for deciding whether to proceed with that therapist and that approach. It is normal and entirely appropriate to ask the therapist about their qualifications, their proposed therapeutic approach, their experience with your presenting concern, and what you should expect from the process before committing to a series of sessions.

### Are there subsidised or lower-cost psychology options in Singapore?

Yes. Singapore's public sector provides mental health services through Institute of Mental Health (IMH), Community Health Assist Scheme (CHAS) GP clinics with mental health training, and polyclinics with mental health support services: these options are substantially more affordable than private psychology but typically involve longer waiting times. Within the private sector, practices including Singapore Counselling Centre offer fee assistance for clients who cannot afford standard rates. Affordable Counselling Space (ACS) provides subsidised counselling at SGD 5 per session for individuals with demonstrated financial need. Samaritans of Singapore (SOS) provides free crisis counselling. For young people, Community Outreach teams and school-based counsellors provide free or very low-cost mental health support. If cost is a barrier, these options provide access to professional psychological support at a fraction of the private market rate.
