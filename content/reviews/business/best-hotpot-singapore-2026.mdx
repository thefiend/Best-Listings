Finding the best hotpot restaurant in Singapore is harder than it looks. With dozens of options spanning Sichuan mala, collagen white broth, tomato base, and Japanese shabu-shabu styles, the quality gap between an outstanding hotpot and a mediocre one is enormous. We evaluated over 40 hotpot restaurants across Singapore, scoring each on broth quality, ingredient freshness, variety of dipping options, service consistency, and value for the price paid. This list ranks the top 10 hotpot restaurants in Singapore for 2026, using Google Maps ratings, verified review counts, and direct dining assessments.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Good Combo Hotpot Chinatown, rated 4.9 stars across 4,710 Google reviews, with a dual-broth format and premium ingredient selection in a heritage Chinatown shophouse.
- **Most reviewed:** Coucou Hotpot at Jewel Changi Airport leads all Singapore hotpot restaurants with 8,639 Google reviews at 4.8 stars, making it the most validated dining choice on this list.
- **Best for Sichuan mala:** Xiao Long Kan Hotpot at Bugis Junction is the Singapore outpost of a globally recognised Sichuan hotpot chain, rated 4.7 stars across 1,837 reviews.
- **Expect to pay SGD 30–80 per person** for a full hotpot meal in Singapore, depending on the restaurant tier and ingredients ordered. Premium chains and CBD locations run SGD 50–100 per head.
- **Reservations are essential** at the top-rated hotpot restaurants in Singapore, particularly on weekends. Walk-ins at Good Combo and Haidilao routinely face 45–90 minute queues.
- **Red flag to avoid:** restaurants listing "unlimited" hotpot under SGD 20 per person almost always compensate by limiting freshness, ingredient quality, and broth depth.

## What to Look for When Choosing a Hotpot Restaurant

**Broth quality and depth.** The broth is the foundation of any hotpot meal. A good broth should deepen in flavour as the meal progresses, not dilute or turn bitter. Ask whether the broth is made in-house or from a commercial base, and whether unlimited broth refills are included. Restaurants that charge per broth refill are often signalling that the preparation cost is high, which is a positive indicator of quality.

**Ingredient freshness and sourcing.** Hotpot ingredients are served raw and cooked at the table, so freshness is non-negotiable. Check recent Google reviews specifically for comments on ingredient freshness and whether meat arrives pre-frozen or fresh-chilled. Premium restaurants will offer same-day sourced seafood; mid-range options will use frozen seafood that has been properly thawed. Avoid restaurants where reviewers mention off-smelling proteins or pre-thawed meat left sitting at room temperature.

**Broth variety and heat calibration.** A good hotpot restaurant offers at least two to three broth options and allows diners to split the pot. If you are dining with a group that has mixed spice tolerances, confirm before booking whether split-pot options are available and whether mala heat levels are genuinely adjustable. Some restaurants offer three mala levels but all taste identical; look for reviews that confirm the heat calibration is real.

**Reservations and queue management.** Singapore's top hotpot restaurants operate near capacity on Friday and Saturday evenings. Restaurants with phone reservations are preferable to walk-in only. If a restaurant has no reservation system and routinely generates 45–90 minute queues, factor that into your planning, especially for group dinners or occasions where timing matters.

### What is the difference between Sichuan hotpot and Cantonese hotpot?

Sichuan hotpot uses a spicy mala broth made from chilli, Sichuan peppercorn, and a suet or vegetable oil base. The defining characteristic is the numbing heat from Sichuan peppercorn. Cantonese hotpot uses a clear or lightly flavoured broth, typically made from pork bones, chicken, or dried seafood, with the emphasis on ingredient quality rather than broth intensity. Good examples of each in Singapore: Xiao Long Kan (Sichuan) and Uncle Fong (Cantonese).
