## What to Look for in a Language School

**Qualified native-speaker teachers.** The single biggest differentiator in language outcomes is teacher quality. Look for schools that disclose their teachers' credentials: native speakers with formal language instruction training or relevant certifications. Avoid schools that list no teacher information at all. A native speaker without teaching training is not automatically a good instructor.

**Exam preparation integration.** If you have a specific certification goal, such as TOPIK for Korean, JLPT for Japanese, HSK for Mandarin, or IELTS for English, choose a school that integrates exam preparation into their standard curriculum rather than offering it as an expensive add-on. Schools like SEJONG and Japanese Explorer build exam frameworks into every level.

**Class size policy.** Smaller classes produce better outcomes at beginner and intermediate levels where individual feedback matters most. Ask the school what their maximum class size is. Anything above 12 students in a speaking-focused language class significantly reduces the time each student gets to practise.

**CPE registration for private schools.** The Council for Private Education (CPE) registers and regulates private education providers in Singapore. A CPE-registered school has met minimum standards for curriculum, teacher qualifications, and financial protection for students. Check the CPE registry at moe.gov.sg before enrolling in any private language school.

**Schedule flexibility for working adults.** Most language learners in Singapore are working professionals. Check whether the school offers evening classes (after 6pm), weekend classes, and make-up sessions or recordings for missed lessons. Schools that only operate weekday daytimes are not realistic options for most employed students.

**Response time to your initial enquiry.** Send a test message before enrolling. A school that takes three days to reply to a prospective student enquiry will take three days to resolve a scheduling problem or curriculum question once you are enrolled. Under 24 hours is acceptable; under 4 hours is excellent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much do language classes cost in Singapore?

Group language classes in Singapore typically cost SGD 200–500 per term (10–12 weeks) for standard programmes. Intensive courses or smaller group classes run SGD 400–800 per term. Private lessons cost SGD 60–120 per hour depending on the language and instructor level. IELTS and TOPIK preparation courses carry a premium and typically run SGD 400–900 for a full exam prep cycle.

### Which languages can I learn in Singapore?

Singapore has strong school coverage for Korean, Japanese, Mandarin Chinese, English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, and Russian. Korean and Japanese have the widest choice of specialist schools. Mandarin schools are numerous given the local Chinese-speaking population. For European languages outside French and Spanish, options narrow but quality schools like inlingua, Lingo Languages, and School of Language International cover the most common ones.

### How long does it take to learn a language in Singapore?

For conversational competency (enough to have basic conversations and understand common phrases), expect 6–12 months of weekly classes for European languages and 12–24 months for Korean, Japanese, or Mandarin. For exam certification at mid-level (TOPIK II, JLPT N3, HSK 4, IELTS 6.5), most committed students taking 2 classes per week achieve this in 18–30 months from zero.

### What is TOPIK and do I need it for Korean classes?

TOPIK (Test of Proficiency in Korean) is the internationally recognised Korean language proficiency exam, managed by the Korean government. It is required for university admission in Korea, certain Korean work visas, and is used by employers as a credential. You do not need TOPIK to enrol in Korean classes, but enrolling in a school that structures its curriculum around TOPIK levels ensures your learning has a clear, measurable progression path.

### Are language schools in Singapore regulated?

Private language schools that offer full courses must be registered with the Council for Private Education (CPE) under the Enhanced Registration Framework (ERF). CPE-registered schools must meet minimum standards for teacher qualifications, curriculum, student protection, and fee transparency. Schools affiliated with MOE or operating as MOE-registered institutions are additionally regulated. Always verify CPE registration before enrolling.

### What is the difference between group classes and private lessons for language learning?

Group classes are more affordable (SGD 200–500 per term versus SGD 60–120 per hour for private lessons) and provide peer interaction that is valuable for speaking practice. Private lessons allow curriculum customisation and faster progression through material that you already partially know. The optimal approach for most learners is group classes supplemented by private lessons during exam preparation phases or when addressing specific weak areas.

### Which is the best Korean language school in Singapore?

SEJONG Korean Language School is the top-rated Korean language school in Singapore, with a 5.0-star rating across 1,320 Google reviews. ONLYOU Korean Language School is an excellent alternative, particularly for learners motivated by Korean culture and entertainment. Both offer TOPIK preparation. SEJONG's larger cohort and CBD location make it more accessible for working professionals.

### Can I learn a language online from a Singapore school?

Several schools on this list, including Edupedia and inlingua, offer hybrid and online class options. Online learning suits learners with irregular schedules but requires more self-discipline and typically produces slower speaking improvement due to reduced spontaneous interaction. If your primary goal is conversational fluency, in-person group classes consistently outperform fully online alternatives.

### What should I ask a language school before enrolling?

Ask: What are your teachers' qualifications and native language? What is the maximum class size? How is curriculum structured across levels? Are there make-up options for missed classes? What exam preparation is included? What is the refund policy if I need to withdraw? How long has the school been operating at this address? Schools that answer all of these clearly and promptly are the ones worth enrolling with.
