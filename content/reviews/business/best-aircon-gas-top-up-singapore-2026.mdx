Finding the best aircon gas top-up service in Singapore matters more than most homeowners realise. Refrigerant is the lifeblood of any air conditioning system: when gas levels drop, cooling efficiency deteriorates, electricity consumption rises, and continued operation under low-refrigerant conditions can cause permanent compressor damage. Singapore's year-round tropical heat means units run continuously, and slow refrigerant leaks go unnoticed until the unit stops cooling altogether. We evaluated over 40 aircon gas top-up providers in Singapore, assessing refrigerant handling certifications, leak detection capability, R32 and R410A gas expertise, pricing transparency, and verified Google review scores. Only companies with a minimum 4.5-star Google rating, a verifiable Singapore business address, and proper refrigerant-handling credentials were shortlisted for this 2026 ranking.

## Key Takeaways

- **Best overall for aircon gas top-up Singapore:** SJR Air-Conditioned, with a perfect 5.0-star Google rating, full refrigerant top-up service covering R32 and R410A, and mandatory leak detection before every gas refill
- **Most reviewed:** SoCool Pte Ltd with 10,904 Google reviews at 4.9 stars, the highest verified review volume of any aircon gas top-up provider assessed
- **Best budget option:** Affordable Aircon Services Pte Ltd, with gas top-up from SGD 60 per unit and 2,219 Google reviews at 4.8 stars
- Aircon gas top-up without leak detection first is a waste of money: refrigerant does not deplete on its own in a sealed system, so low gas always indicates a leak that must be found and fixed before topping up
- R32 refrigerant (used in most post-2015 units) and R410A (older systems) require different certified handling procedures; always confirm your technician is qualified to handle the specific refrigerant type in your system
- Typical gas top-up pricing in Singapore: SGD 60–150 per unit for R410A; SGD 80–180 per unit for R32, depending on leak severity and gas quantity required
- A unit that needs repeated gas top-ups every 6–12 months almost certainly has a slow refrigerant leak that requires professional repair, not just another top-up

## What to Look for When Hiring an Aircon Gas Top-Up Service

**Leak detection before top-up.** This is the single most important criterion. Refrigerant in a properly sealed aircon system does not deplete on its own: if your unit is low on gas, there is a leak. A company that tops up the gas without first finding and fixing the leak is wasting your money and potentially causing further damage. Every reputable gas top-up provider in Singapore should inspect for leaks before adding any refrigerant. If a company skips this step, find a different provider.

**Refrigerant type certification.** R32 and R410A require different handling procedures, different recovery equipment, and different technician certifications. Most Singapore residential units installed after 2015 use R32; older units use R410A or R22 (now largely phased out). Confirm that the company you hire is certified to handle the specific refrigerant type in your system before booking. A technician without proper R32 certification handling an R32 system is both unsafe and non-compliant with Singapore regulations.

**Pricing transparency.** Legitimate gas top-up providers in Singapore display price ranges on their website or provide a written quote before the technician arrives. Be wary of companies that quote only verbally on-site after the technician has already opened the unit: the price tends to escalate at that point. Ask for a written quote that covers labour, gas quantity (typically priced per pound or per kg), and any repair work identified.

**Review recency and volume.** A 4.8-star rating across 50 reviews is less meaningful than the same rating across 2,000 reviews. More important than the average is recency: check that reviews have been posted in the last three months. A company with a strong historical rating but no recent reviews may have changed ownership or declined in service quality.

**Post-service warranty.** Reputable gas top-up companies offer a warranty on their work: typically 30–90 days during which they will return at no charge if the unit loses refrigerant again. This warranty signals that the company has confidence in the quality of their leak repair and is not simply refilling and walking away.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does an aircon gas top-up cost in Singapore in 2026?

Gas top-up pricing in Singapore for 2026 ranges from SGD 60–150 per unit for R410A refrigerant and SGD 80–180 per unit for R32, depending on the volume of gas required and whether leak repair work is also needed. Prices vary by company, unit type, and gas quantity consumed. Always request an itemised written quote covering gas cost, labour, and any repair identified before the technician starts work. Confirm whether GST is included in the quoted figure.

### What is the difference between R32 and R410A refrigerant?

R32 is the current standard refrigerant for most residential aircon systems installed in Singapore from 2015 onwards. It has a lower global warming potential than R410A and operates at higher pressure, requiring different certified handling equipment and procedures. R410A was the dominant residential refrigerant before 2015 and remains in use in older units. R22, an older refrigerant, has been largely phased out in Singapore. Always confirm which refrigerant your unit uses before booking: it is typically printed on the specification label on the indoor unit.

### Why does my aircon keep losing gas?

Refrigerant loss always indicates a leak in the sealed refrigerant circuit. Common causes include: small cracks in the copper evaporator or condenser coils (often caused by vibration or corrosion); loose or faulty flare fittings where refrigerant pipes connect to the indoor and outdoor units; a damaged Schrader valve; or physical damage to the refrigerant lines. A gas top-up without leak repair is a temporary fix: the refrigerant will slowly escape again through the same leak path. The correct approach is to locate the leak, repair it, and then recharge the circuit.

### Can I top up my aircon gas myself in Singapore?

No. Refrigerant handling in Singapore requires certification under the NEA regulatory framework. Purchasing, handling, or disposing of refrigerant without the appropriate licence is illegal. All gas top-up work must be carried out by a licensed technician. Attempting a DIY top-up also risks personal injury from high-pressure gas, incorrect refrigerant type, and voiding the manufacturer warranty on your unit.

### How long does an aircon gas top-up take?

A standard gas top-up including leak check takes 30–60 minutes per unit. If a leak is identified and requires repair, add 30–60 minutes depending on the location and severity of the leak. Units requiring full gas recovery, circuit flushing, and recharge take 90–120 minutes. Most residential gas top-up visits for a single unit are completed within one hour.

### How do I know if my aircon needs a gas top-up?

Common symptoms of low refrigerant in Singapore aircon units: the unit blows air but fails to reach the set temperature; the indoor unit produces unusual hissing or bubbling sounds from the refrigerant circuit; ice forms on the indoor unit coil or the outdoor unit refrigerant pipe; the outdoor unit runs continuously without the room cooling down; or the unit has been serviced recently but still performs poorly. If you observe any combination of these symptoms, request a gas check and refrigerant pressure test rather than a standard general service.

### What happens if I ignore low refrigerant for too long?

Operating an aircon unit under low refrigerant conditions causes the compressor to work harder to compensate for reduced cooling capacity. Sustained operation in this state accelerates compressor wear and can lead to premature compressor failure: the most expensive single repair in any aircon system, often costing SGD 500–1,200 or more depending on brand and unit type. Addressing low refrigerant early, with proper leak detection and repair, is significantly cheaper than waiting until the compressor fails.

### Are aircon gas top-up technicians licensed in Singapore?

Yes. Singapore's National Environment Agency (NEA) requires technicians handling fluorinated greenhouse gases (F-gases) including R32 and R410A to hold the relevant certification. The BCA also regulates contractors performing certain types of aircon and mechanical ventilation work. Always confirm that the company you hire employs NEA-certified refrigerant technicians. Reputable companies will state their certifications on their website or provide documentation on request.

### How often should I need a gas top-up?

A properly sealed aircon system in good condition should not require a gas top-up more than once every 5–10 years, if at all. If your unit has needed a top-up within the last 12–18 months, or if you have had multiple top-ups in successive years, there is an unresolved leak in the refrigerant circuit that requires proper investigation and repair rather than another temporary refill.
