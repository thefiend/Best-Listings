Singapore's interior design industry serves one of the world's most demanding renovation markets. With nearly nine in ten residents living in HDB flats — whether newly balloted BTO units, mature resale flats, or executive maisonettes — the local appetite for professionally designed interiors is sustained by a continuous pipeline of new flat handovers, upgrading activity, and the lifecycle renovation needs of an ageing private housing stock. Add condominiums from entry-level leasehold units to freehold penthouses, and the landed segment of terrace houses, semi-detached properties, and bungalows, and the sheer variety of project types in Singapore makes interior design singapore a remarkably specialised discipline that no single template can cover.

A typical home renovation in Singapore moves through several distinct stages regardless of property type. Hacking — demolishing non-structural walls, removing existing tiles, and stripping outdated fittings — comes first, and in HDB properties requires an HDB renovation permit before work can begin. Structural works are strictly regulated: load-bearing walls cannot be hacked, wet areas must meet waterproofing standards, and electrical works above a certain scope must be certified by a Licensed Electrical Worker. Carpentry follows, covering built-in wardrobes, kitchen cabinets, TV feature walls, and custom storage solutions that define the functional character of Singapore homes where space optimisation is not a luxury but a necessity. Tiling, painting, lighting installation, and final furnishing complete the sequence.

The distinction between an interior designer (ID) and a general renovation contractor matters significantly when choosing who to engage for home renovation singapore. An ID typically provides concept development, space planning, material and colour specification, and project coordination — managing multiple sub-contractors including tilers, carpenters, electricians, and painters — while a contractor executes specific trades without the design oversight layer. For complex renovations involving layout changes, custom carpentry, and a coherent aesthetic vision, an ID firm adds value that justifies the fee premium. For straightforward repaints or single-trade works, a direct contractor engagement may suffice. The firms on this list are ID firms offering full-service interior design singapore — concept to key collection.

When evaluating an interior designer singapore, the fundamentals to verify are: CaseTrust accreditation (the industry-standard consumer protection mark), a clear written quotation that itemises all works and materials, a renovation timeline with milestone payments rather than full upfront payment, and a portfolio of completed projects in the same property type as yours. Google reviews from verified past clients are the most transparent indicator of how an ID firm actually performs once the contract is signed — which is why this ranking is ordered by review count as the primary signal of real-world, at-scale client satisfaction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does interior design cost in Singapore in 2026?**\
Interior design costs in Singapore vary significantly by property type, renovation scope, and design firm tier. For a standard 4-room HDB BTO flat starting from bare shell, a mid-market interior design singapore package typically runs between SGD 40,000 and SGD 70,000, covering carpentry, tiling, electrical, painting, and design fees. Resale HDB renovations that include hacking of existing finishes can cost more — typically SGD 50,000 to SGD 90,000 for a 4-room flat — due to the demolition work involved. Condominium renovations for a 2-bedroom unit start from approximately SGD 50,000 and can exceed SGD 120,000 for premium finishes and extensive built-in carpentry. Landed property renovations are priced on scope and can range from SGD 150,000 to well above SGD 500,000 for full rebuilds. Always obtain itemised quotations from at least three firms to establish a meaningful cost baseline for your specific project scope, and ensure the quotation covers all materials, labour, and sub-contractor works without excluded items that will appear as variation orders mid-renovation.

**What is the difference between an interior designer and a contractor in Singapore?**\
An interior designer (ID) in Singapore provides end-to-end service: space planning, concept development, material and finish specification, 3D visualisation, and project management across all renovation trades. They act as the single point of coordination between the homeowner and multiple sub-contractors — carpenters, tilers, electricians, painters, and plumbers — ensuring the design is executed cohesively and the project timeline is managed across all workstreams. A renovation contractor focuses on executing specific trades or a broader scope of works without the design development and coordination service layer. For simple renovations — repainting, changing fixtures, or replacing flooring — a direct contractor engagement is cost-effective. For complex renovations involving layout reconfiguration, extensive custom carpentry, and a deliberate aesthetic vision, an interior designer singapore adds value that pays for itself through better design outcomes, fewer coordination failures, and reduced risk of expensive mistakes.

**How long does a BTO renovation take in Singapore?**\
A full BTO renovation in Singapore typically takes between 8 and 16 weeks from the start of works, depending on scope and the availability of sub-contractors. The timeline begins after HDB renovation permit approval — which homeowners must obtain before any works begin, and which typically takes 3 to 5 business days for standard applications — and runs through hacking, tiling, carpentry fabrication and installation, electrical works, painting, and final installation of fixtures and fittings. Delays most commonly occur at the carpentry stage (if the manufacturer's production schedule is tight), during inspections if any works require amendment, and at the finishing stage if materials are out of stock or back-ordered. Homeowners should plan for a minimum 10-week renovation timeline for a 4-room HDB BTO and build in a buffer for the defect inspection and rectification period before moving in, particularly if renovation works are scheduled concurrently with other major commitments.

**Do I need HDB approval for renovation in Singapore?**\
Yes. HDB homeowners must apply for a renovation permit through the HDB e-Services portal before commencing any renovation works. The permit is required for all renovation works in HDB flats and must be obtained by a registered renovation contractor or interior design firm on the homeowner's behalf. Specific works categories require prior HDB approval: structural hacking (with strict restrictions on load-bearing elements), wet area waterproofing works, changes to floor finishes, electrical main switchboard upgrades, and any works affecting the building facade or common areas. HDB also regulates renovation hours: weekdays and Saturdays between 9 AM and 6 PM, with noisy works (hacking, drilling, mechanical equipment) restricted to 9 AM to 5 PM on weekdays. No renovation works are permitted on Sundays and public holidays. Reputable interior design singapore firms are fully familiar with these requirements and will manage the permit application and scheduling compliance as part of their service.
