Singapore's primary care system is structured around a deliberate tiering of access. At the foundation sit the 20-plus polyclinics operated by the National University Polyclinic and SingHealth Polyclinics, providing subsidised general practice and chronic disease management to Singapore citizens and permanent residents. Above that sits a dense network of private general practice clinics: estimated at over 1,800 island-wide: ranging from solo GP practices in HDB heartland estates to multi-outlet private healthcare groups such as Raffles Medical and Parkway Shenton. Choosing the right GP clinic singapore depends on what you need: a routine consultation for a fever or cough is a different requirement from ongoing chronic disease management for diabetes or hypertension, or pre-employment health screening for a new employer.

The CHAS (Community Health Assist Scheme) is the mechanism through which the Singapore government extends polyclinic-equivalent subsidies to private GP clinics, enabling eligible Singapore citizens to access private GP care at significantly reduced out-of-pocket costs. CHAS Blue cardholders (lower-income households) and CHAS Orange cardholders (middle-income households) receive tiered subsidies at accredited private GP clinics, covering both acute consultation fees and a defined list of chronic disease conditions under the Chronic Disease Management Programme (CDMP). Pioneering GP Group and Healthway Medical operate some of the largest networks of CHAS-accredited private clinics. For Singapore citizens managing chronic conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, high cholesterol, and asthma, selecting a CHAS-accredited GP clinic can reduce the annual out-of-pocket cost of medical care materially.

Medisave withdrawal for GP visits is permitted under the Medisave for Outpatient Treatment scheme, which allows use of Medisave for consultations at MOH-approved GP clinics for selected chronic conditions. The withdrawal limit per patient per year and the qualifying conditions are defined by the Ministry of Health and updated periodically. Patients with chronic disease should confirm with their chosen GP clinic whether it is accredited for Medisave outpatient treatment before their first visit.

The practical calculus between polyclinic and private GP for any given patient comes down to three variables: subsidy eligibility, appointment urgency, and continuity of care preferences. For working adults who need a same-day appointment for an acute illness, any nearby private GP clinic will almost always be more practical than a polyclinic queue. For patients with complex chronic conditions who benefit from seeing the same doctor consistently, a private family clinic with a committed GP is often superior to polyclinic care where patients may see a different doctor at every visit.

## How We Ranked These GP Clinics

Our methodology for ranking the best GP clinic singapore 2026 applied the following criteria. Singapore Medical Council registration: all clinics must be operated by SMC-registered medical practitioners: this is the non-negotiable legal baseline for GP practice in Singapore, and all clinics on this list meet it. CHAS accreditation: clinics accredited under the Community Health Assist Scheme were noted and weighted, as CHAS status is a significant financial consideration for the large proportion of Singapore patients who are eligible for subsidies. Medisave accreditation for outpatient chronic disease treatment was evaluated as a separate criterion. Clinic operating hours, including evening and weekend availability: Singapore's working population depends heavily on GP clinics with non-standard hours given that daytime consultations require time off work. Google rating: a minimum of 4.0 stars was applied as a quality floor, with higher ratings rewarded in ordering. Service breadth: clinics offering vaccinations, health screening, chronic disease management, minor surgical procedures, and health certificates alongside standard acute consultations were weighted above single-service practices. Network scale and accessibility: multi-outlet groups were assessed for island-wide coverage quality. All data reflects conditions as of August 2026.

## Polyclinic vs Private GP: When to Use Each

Singapore's polyclinic system, operated by SingHealth Polyclinics and the National University Polyclinic (NUP), provides heavily subsidised primary care to Singapore citizens and permanent residents across 20-plus locations. Polyclinic fees for Singapore citizens start from approximately SGD 15–40 for a standard GP consultation after subsidy, compared to SGD 25–80 at a private CHAS-accredited clinic and SGD 40–100 at a non-CHAS private GP clinic. The polyclinic cost advantage is most pronounced for patients with multiple chronic conditions who attend for regular CDMP follow-up: the total annual outpatient cost at a polyclinic can be significantly lower than at a private GP even after CHAS subsidies are applied.

The cases where private GP is clearly preferable are: acute illness requiring same-day or next-day attendance (polyclinic walk-in queues can involve waits of two to four hours; private GP clinics typically see you within twenty to forty minutes of arrival or at your booked appointment time); conditions requiring a specific specialist referral where you want to control which specialist you see (polyclinic referrals go to restructured hospital specialist outpatient clinics, which do not allow you to choose your specialist; private GP referrals can be directed to a specific private specialist of your choice); conditions where continuity with a specific doctor is clinically important (large polyclinics cycle patients across multiple doctors, whereas a private GP clinic or family practice enables consistent patient-doctor pairing); and situations where the patient is covered by employer health insurance that makes the cost difference irrelevant.

Polyclinics are clearly preferable when: the patient is cost-sensitive and not in a hurry; the patient requires a referral to a restructured hospital specialist outpatient clinic, which is effectively only accessible via polyclinic referral for subsidised rates; the patient is a new resident or has complex administrative needs that benefit from the full social and medical services integrated within polyclinics (pharmacist consultation, dietitian referral, medical social worker access); or the clinical need is straightforward and waiting time is acceptable.

## Understanding CHAS, Medisave, and HealthAssist for GP Visits

The Community Health Assist Scheme (CHAS) is administered by the Agency for Integrated Care (AIC) on behalf of the Ministry of Health. CHAS provides fee subsidies at accredited private GP and dental clinics to eligible Singapore citizens, with three tiers: CHAS Blue (lower-income households earning below SGD 1,800 per month per capita), CHAS Orange (middle-income households earning between SGD 1,800–2,600 per month per capita), and MG/Pioneer Generation (Merdeka Generation and Pioneer Generation cardholders who are automatically enrolled). Subsidy amounts differ by tier and condition type: acute conditions attract a lower subsidy than chronic disease conditions under the CDMP. To use CHAS, you must present your physical CHAS card at a registered CHAS clinic; as of 2024, CHAS cards are being progressively integrated into the Singpass digital wallet.

Medisave for outpatient treatment allows account holders to withdraw from their CPF Medisave account to pay for consultation fees and approved outpatient medications at MOH-registered clinics for a defined list of chronic conditions. The Medisave500 and Medisave700 (for those aged 60 and above) limits specify the annual maximum withdrawal. Not every GP clinic is accredited for Medisave outpatient withdrawal: the clinic must have applied for and received MOH accreditation separately from its basic licensing. Always confirm Medisave accreditation with your clinic before relying on it at the point of payment.

HealthAssist is the online government portal at eservices.sg through which citizens can check their CHAS eligibility and card tier, find CHAS-accredited clinics near their address, and review their CHAS subsidy utilisation. Using HealthAssist before selecting a GP clinic is the most reliable way to confirm that the specific clinic you intend to visit is currently CHAS-accredited for the type of care you need: acute or CDMP chronic: as accreditation status can change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a GP consultation cost in Singapore?**
A standard GP consultation at a private clinic in Singapore without CHAS subsidy typically costs SGD 25–80 for an acute illness consultation, covering the consultation fee plus any medication dispensed. The actual out-of-pocket amount depends on the clinic's tier (heartland community clinic vs. premium private group), the complexity of the consultation, the number and type of medications prescribed, and any additional services such as wound dressing, injections, or swab tests. For patients with CHAS Blue cards, private GP consultations at CHAS-accredited clinics can cost as little as SGD 5–15 after subsidy for acute consultations. For patients with employer corporate health insurance, many major private GP groups: including Raffles Medical, Parkway Shenton, and Fullerton Health: are on the panel of most Singapore corporate health plans, meaning out-of-pocket costs may be minimal or nil. Polyclinic consultation fees for Singapore citizens are approximately SGD 15–40 per visit after means-tested subsidy. Always ask the clinic reception for a fee estimate before your consultation if cost is a concern.

**What is CHAS and am I eligible?**
CHAS stands for Community Health Assist Scheme. It is a Singapore government programme that provides fee subsidies for primary care at accredited private GP and dental clinics to Singapore citizens who meet the income threshold criteria. CHAS eligibility is based on per-capita monthly household income: CHAS Blue applies to households with per-capita income below SGD 1,800 per month; CHAS Orange applies to households with per-capita income between SGD 1,800–2,600 per month. All Merdeka Generation (born 1950–1959) and Pioneer Generation (born 1949 or earlier) Singapore citizens are automatically enrolled in CHAS regardless of income level. CHAS cards are issued by the Agency for Integrated Care and can be applied for online at the AIC website or through your nearest polyclinic or Community Centre. To check if you are currently eligible or to apply, visit careline.sg or the HealthAssist portal at eservices.sg. If you received a CHAS card several years ago, verify that your tier is still current as household income changes may affect eligibility.

**Can I use Medisave for GP visits?**
Medisave can be used for GP visits at MOH-accredited outpatient treatment facilities, but only for a defined list of chronic conditions under the Medisave for Outpatient Treatment scheme. The qualifying conditions include diabetes mellitus, hypertension, high cholesterol (hyperlipidaemia), stroke, asthma, COPD, schizophrenia, major depression, bipolar disorder, and dementia: conditions that require ongoing regular medical management. The annual Medisave withdrawal limit for outpatient treatment is SGD 500 per account holder, rising to SGD 700 for patients aged 60 and above under the Flexi-Medisave scheme (which also applies to healthcare for family members). Routine acute GP visits for illnesses such as flu, fever, or coughs are not Medisave-claimable. To use Medisave at a GP clinic, the clinic must be specifically accredited for Medisave outpatient treatment: not all private GP clinics have this accreditation. Confirm with your chosen clinic before the visit.

**When should I go to A&E instead of a GP?**
A&E (Accident and Emergency) attendance is appropriate when you have symptoms or a medical situation that may require immediate investigation, specialist assessment, or emergency treatment that a GP clinic is not equipped to provide. Go to A&E for: chest pain or tightness that may indicate a cardiac event; sudden onset severe headache unlike any you have had before; signs of stroke (facial drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty: use the FAST acronym); difficulty breathing or sudden shortness of breath; significant trauma including road traffic accidents, falls from height, or injuries causing suspected fractures; loss of consciousness or altered mental state; severe abdominal pain of sudden onset; significant burns; suspected poisoning or overdose; and any condition where you are genuinely unsure whether you are safe to wait. If in doubt, call 995 (Singapore Civil Defence Force ambulance) or go to the nearest restructured hospital A&E. A GP clinic is appropriate for conditions that are uncomfortable and require medical attention but are not immediately life-threatening: fever, sore throat, upper respiratory infection, UTI, skin rash, minor wounds, and the vast majority of everyday illnesses.

**What is the difference between a GP and a specialist?**
A General Practitioner (GP) is the first point of contact in the healthcare system, trained to assess, diagnose, and manage a broad range of medical conditions across all organ systems and all age groups. GPs registered with the Singapore Medical Council and practising family medicine may hold additional postgraduate qualifications including the Membership of the Royal College of General Practitioners (MRCGP) or the Graduate Diploma in Family Medicine (GDFM) from the NUS Yong Soo Lin School of Medicine. A specialist is a doctor who has completed additional post-MBBS training in a specific medical or surgical discipline: cardiology, gastroenterology, orthopaedics, neurology, and so on: and is registered with the Singapore Medical Council as a specialist in that field. Specialists are accessed by referral from a GP, either to a restructured hospital specialist outpatient clinic (for subsidised care) or to a private specialist clinic (for self-pay or insured care). The correct sequencing is: see a GP first for most medical concerns; the GP will manage what is within primary care scope and refer to the appropriate specialist if the condition warrants it. Self-referring directly to a specialist without a GP assessment can lead to attending the wrong specialty, delays in receiving the correct diagnosis, and higher costs without clinical benefit.
