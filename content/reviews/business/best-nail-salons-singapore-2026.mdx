Singapore's nail salon industry has matured from a commodity service into a genuinely specialised beauty segment. Where once the market was dominated by generic walk-in parlours offering basic manicures at HDB-mall prices, it now spans a wide spectrum: from Japanese-trained nail technicians executing intricate 3D nail art and minimalist Omotenashi-style service to Korean-influenced salons specialising in gel overlays, ombre gradients, and hand-painted designs that regularly appear on social media. The best nail salons in Singapore today are not just grooming venues; they are creative studios where skilled technicians apply artistry equivalent to fine craft work onto a 1.5 cm canvas.

The services available across Singapore nail salons cover significant ground. Gel manicures: using UV-cured gel polish for a high-gloss finish that lasts two to three weeks without chipping: remain the most-booked service. Acrylic nails, built using a liquid-and-powder system, offer structural extension and durability for clients who want length beyond what their natural nails can achieve. Dip powder systems, popular for their gentler application process and natural finish, suit clients prioritising nail health over maximum durability. Nail art ranges from simple stamped patterns and chrome powder effects to hand-painted florals, abstract designs, and 3D embellishments using gel sculpture: a specialism where Japanese-trained technicians in Singapore hold a visible edge. Pedicure services have similarly evolved beyond basic callus removal into full spa treatments with scrubs, masks, and extended foot and lower-leg massage.

Hygiene is the non-negotiable baseline in this industry. Singapore nail salons are regulated under the Environmental Public Health Act, which requires beauty establishments to maintain sanitation standards for tools, surfaces, and nail dust containment. All metal implements: nippers, cuticle pushers, nail files: should be either single-use or autoclave-sterilised between clients. Reputable salons use hospital-grade UV sterilisers or autoclaves visibly; clients should not hesitate to ask. Proper ventilation is equally important: nail dust and acrylic fumes are occupational health hazards, and well-run salons invest in filtered extraction systems. Any salon that reuses disposable tools or cannot clearly explain its sterilisation process should be avoided regardless of price.

When choosing a nail salon in Singapore, the key differentiators are technician skill level (particularly for nail art and extensions), the quality of products used (branded gel systems such as OPI, CND Shellac, or Presto versus unbranded alternatives), the booking process (walk-in versus appointment-only and how this affects wait times), and the breadth of the service menu. Japanese nail salons tend to emphasise meticulous preparation, cuticle work, and hand massage in their process; Korean nail salons typically emphasise colour selection, design trends, and faster throughput. Neither style is superior: the right choice depends on what the client values most.

For this 2026 ranking of the best nail salon singapore options, we evaluated salons using Google review count as the primary ranking factor, with Google rating as the secondary tiebreaker. We then assessed service breadth, nail art capability, hygiene standards, booking accessibility, and value for money based on verified client reviews.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a manicure cost in Singapore in 2026?**
A basic regular polish manicure in Singapore costs SGD 20–35 at most salons. Gel manicures: the most popular service: range from SGD 35–55 for a single-colour gel with standard preparation, to SGD 55–80 for gel with nail art, chrome powder, or other design elements. Acrylic full sets for nail extensions typically start at SGD 80–120 depending on length and complexity. Hard gel extensions are priced similarly to acrylics. Dip powder manicures range from SGD 40–70. Gel pedicures range from SGD 45–80, with spa pedicure packages that include scrub, mask, and extended massage reaching SGD 80–120. Nail art add-ons are typically priced per design element at SGD 5–30 depending on complexity. Prices in Orchard Road and CBD salons are generally 15–25% higher than equivalent services at neighbourhood and heartland salons for the same service quality tier.

**What is the difference between gel, acrylic, and dip powder nails?**
Gel nails use a UV-cured gel polish applied directly to the natural nail or over a gel base, producing a glossy, flexible finish that lasts two to three weeks. Gel is the gentlest option for nail health when applied and removed correctly using a gel remover soak rather than filing. Acrylic nails are built from a combination of liquid monomer and powder polymer that hardens through a chemical reaction, allowing nail technicians to create extensions beyond the natural nail tip. Acrylics are the most structurally durable extension system and are the standard choice for significant length. Dip powder (SNS or similar systems) applies a coloured acrylic powder bonded with adhesive resin, producing a matte-finish nail that is thicker and more chip-resistant than gel polish but more flexible than traditional acrylic. Dip powder is popular with clients who want durability without UV lamp exposure. In Singapore, gel manicure is the most commonly booked service; acrylics are preferred for nail extensions; and dip powder appeals to clients prioritising nail health and longevity.

**How long do gel nails last in Singapore's climate?**
In Singapore's hot and humid climate, well-applied gel nails last two to three weeks before lifting, chipping, or growing out visibly. Factors that affect longevity include the quality of the gel product used, the thoroughness of the nail preparation (dehydration and priming are critical), and the client's lifestyle: frequent hand-washing, swimming, and exposure to harsh chemicals all shorten gel lifespan. Premium gel systems such as CND Shellac, OPI Gel Color, and Presto (a Japanese brand popular in Singapore) generally outperform unbranded alternatives in adhesion and chip resistance. To extend gel lifespan, apply cuticle oil daily to maintain flexibility and avoid using fingernails as tools. Most salons in Singapore recommend a three-week infill or removal cycle to maintain nail health and prevent moisture trapping under lifting edges.

**What nail services should I ask for on my first visit to a Singapore nail salon?**
On a first visit, a gel manicure with a classic single-colour finish is the best way to assess a nail salon's core technical standard: preparation quality, gel application precision, curing process, and final finish reveal everything about the technician's skill level without the complexity of nail art. Before the appointment, note whether the technician opens a sealed sterilised pouch for their metal tools: this is the hygiene baseline. Good cuticle work: gently pushed back and cleaned without cutting unless specifically requested: is a marker of Japanese-trained technique. If you want nail art, describe the design clearly or bring a reference image; a skilled technician will confirm whether they can match the reference or suggest an adaptation before starting. For a first pedicure visit, request a spa pedicure rather than a basic pedicure to properly assess the salon's foot care thoroughness. Ask about removal procedures for gel at the end of the service: a salon that recommends soaking in acetone wrap rather than filing off the gel is protecting your nail health correctly.
