## How We Ranked These Tile Shops

We started with 50 Google Maps listings returned for tile shops, tile suppliers, and tile showrooms in Singapore, then applied four filters. First, every business had to sell or install floor and wall tiles as a core activity: furniture retailers, carpet and rug dealers, general renovation and interior design firms, floor polishing services, laminate-only suppliers, countertop-only surface brands, and unrelated listings (including a Lava Floor play attraction, toy shops, and department stores) were excluded. Second, each business needed a minimum verified Google rating of 4.0 stars and at least 20 reviews, so that the rating reflects a genuine spread of customer experience. Third, where a brand appears several times (Hafary, Lian Seng Hin, White Horse Ceramic, and Soon Bee Huat all have more than one outlet), we listed the brand once using its strongest branch and mention the others in the write-up. Hafary's Stone Gallery listing in Sungei Kadut fell below our rating threshold. Finally, we ranked the retail showrooms by a combination of rating and review volume, and placed one grouting and tile repair specialist at the end of the list as a service pick for homeowners fixing existing tiles rather than buying new ones. BestThingReview.com does not accept payment for placement in this ranking.

## What to Look for When Buying Tiles

**Material and water absorption.** Porcelain (including homogeneous, or full-body, tiles) absorbs less than 0.5% water and is dense enough for floors, wet areas, and heavy traffic. Ceramic tiles absorb more water and are softer, which makes them easier to cut and cheaper, but better suited to walls than to busy floors. Ask the showroom for the water absorption figure on the product sheet rather than relying on the label.

**Slip resistance for wet areas.** Bathroom and balcony floors need a textured or matt tile with a proven slip rating. Look for R10 or above on the DIN 51130 scale for bathroom floors, and R11 for outdoor or frequently wet areas.

**Batch, shade, and calibre.** Tiles are produced in batches, and each batch can vary slightly in colour (shade) and size (calibre). Buy all of your tiles from the same batch, check the batch code on every box before installation, and keep spare boxes for future repairs.

**Size versus your space.** Large-format tiles of 600 x 1200 mm and above reduce grout lines and make rooms feel bigger, but they demand a very flat substrate, skilled installers, and careful transport through HDB lifts and corridors. Smaller tiles are more forgiving on uneven floors and offer better grip in showers.

**Total installed cost.** The price per square foot on the sample is only part of the cost. Ask your contractor for a written quotation covering tiles, wastage, screeding or levelling, waterproofing, grout type, and labour,.

### How much do tiles cost per square foot in Singapore?

Mass-market ceramic and porcelain tiles in Singapore typically cost SGD 2 to SGD 6 per square foot for the tile alone, while imported Italian and Spanish collections, large-format slabs, and specialist finishes commonly range from SGD 6 to SGD 15 or more per square foot. Installation labour is charged separately and usually adds SGD 3 to SGD 8 per square foot depending on tile size, layout pattern, and whether hacking, screeding, or waterproofing is required.

### What is the difference between porcelain, ceramic, and homogeneous tiles?

Ceramic tiles are made from a lighter clay body fired at lower temperatures, so they are more porous, easier to cut, and generally cheaper, which makes them a common choice for walls. Porcelain tiles are made from finer clay fired at higher temperatures, giving a denser tile with water absorption below 0.5% that suits floors and wet areas. Homogeneous tiles are a type of porcelain where the colour runs through the full body of the tile, so chips are less visible.

### What are the best tiles for HDB floors and bathrooms?

For HDB living and dining areas, a matt or lightly textured porcelain or homogeneous tile in 600 x 600 mm or 600 x 1200 mm is the most popular choice, balancing durability, easy cleaning, and cost. For HDB bathroom floors, choose a smaller anti-slip porcelain tile rated R10 or higher, because smaller tiles create more grout lines for grip and follow the floor fall to the drain more easily. Note that HDB guidelines restrict hacking of bathroom wall and floor tiles in new flats within the first three years after key collection, so check the current rules before planning a bathroom retile.

### What do anti-slip R ratings mean?

R ratings come from the German DIN 51130 ramp test and run from R9 (lowest slip resistance) to R13 (highest). R9 tiles suit dry indoor areas such as bedrooms and living rooms, R10 is the usual minimum for bathroom and kitchen floors, R11 suits balconies, outdoor paths, and pool surrounds, and R12 to R13 are used in commercial kitchens and industrial settings. Ask the showroom for the test certificate on any tile intended for a wet area.

### Are large-format tiles a good idea in Singapore homes?

Large-format tiles, typically 600 x 1200 mm and above, work well in open-plan living areas because fewer grout lines make the space feel larger and cleaner. They require a very flat subfloor, levelling clips, full mortar coverage, and experienced installers, otherwise uneven edges (lippage) and hollow spots become visible. Confirm that very large slabs will fit through HDB lifts before ordering.

### Should I overlay or hack my existing tiles?

Overlaying, which means laying new tiles directly over the existing ones, is usually faster, cheaper, quieter, and less messy than hacking, and it works well when the existing tiles are firmly bonded and level. Hacking is the better option when existing tiles are hollow, cracked, uneven, or when the waterproofing underneath has failed. Overlay raises the floor height, which can affect door clearances and the step between rooms, so thinner tiles of 9 to 10 mm are often used.

### How many extra tiles should I buy?

Buy at least 10% more tiles than the measured area for standard straight layouts, and 15% or more for diagonal, herringbone, or large-format layouts where more tiles are cut. Keeping one or two spare boxes after installation is strongly recommended, because the same batch may be impossible to source in a few years if a tile cracks or needs replacing.

### Is epoxy grout worth it compared to cement grout?

Epoxy grout is worth it for bathrooms, kitchen backsplashes, and any area exposed to water, oil, or frequent cleaning. Unlike cement grout, it is non-porous, which means it resists staining, mould, and water seepage and does not need sealing. It costs more and needs an experienced installer, but prevents the black grout lines common in older bathrooms. Existing cement grout can also be removed and replaced with epoxy by a specialist such as Grout Specialist.

Start with showrooms that have a rating of 4.5 stars or higher across at least 50 reviews, then shortlist two or three based on your style and budget. Ask each showroom for price per square foot, water absorption, slip rating, batch availability, and lead time on every tile you like.
