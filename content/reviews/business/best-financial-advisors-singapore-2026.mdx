Financial advisors in Singapore operate under one of Asia's most rigorous regulatory frameworks, governed by the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) under the Financial Advisers Act. We evaluated the leading financial advisory firms in Singapore by assessing Google ratings, review volume, MAS licensing status, breadth of advisory services, transparency of fee structures, and their ability to serve clients across different life stages and wealth levels. This list covers firms providing wealth management, insurance planning, retirement planning, investment advisory, CPF advisory, and comprehensive financial planning services across Singapore.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Alpha Wealth Financial Advisers Pte. Ltd., 222 Google reviews at 4.9 stars, MAS-licensed independent financial adviser with a strong track record across wealth, insurance, and retirement planning
- **Best for review volume:** Unicorn Financial Solutions, 1,213 Google reviews at 5.0 stars, the highest review count of any financial advisory firm on this list

- MAS requires all financial advisers in Singapore to hold a valid Financial Adviser's Licence: always verify a firm's MAS licensing status on the MAS Financial Institutions Directory before engaging
- Financial advisory fees in Singapore range from commission-based structures (zero upfront cost, adviser earns from product placement) to fee-based models charging SGD 150–500 per hour or SGD 2,000–10,000 for a comprehensive financial plan
- Independent financial advisers (IFAs) can recommend products from multiple providers, whereas tied advisers are restricted to their employer's product range: IFAs generally offer more objective advice

## How We Ranked These Financial Advisors

We ranked each financial advisory firm using Google Maps rating and review volume as primary signals of client satisfaction, verified each firm's MAS licensing status via the Financial Institutions Directory, and assessed the breadth of advisory services offered. Firms were evaluated on whether they offer independent advice across multiple product providers or are restricted to a single insurer or fund house, their transparency around fee and commission structures, documented client outcomes, and their experience serving both local Singaporeans and expatriate clients. Firms with any adverse MAS regulatory actions, complaints upheld by the Financial Industry Disputes Resolution Centre (FIDReC), or review patterns suggesting mis-selling were excluded regardless of brand prominence. Minimum threshold for inclusion was 20 verified Google reviews at a rating of 4.7 or above.

## What to Look for When Hiring a Financial Advisor in Singapore

**MAS licensing verification.** Before engaging any financial adviser in Singapore, verify their licence on the MAS Financial Institutions Directory at eservices.mas.gov.sg. A valid Financial Adviser's Licence is a legal requirement: advisers who are unlicensed cannot legally provide regulated financial advisory services in Singapore. This check takes two minutes and is the single most important due diligence step.

**Independent vs tied adviser.** Independent financial advisers (IFAs) are licensed to recommend financial products from multiple providers, whereas tied advisers represent a single company and can only recommend their employer's products. An IFA can compare life insurance policies from multiple insurers and recommend the one that best fits your needs; a tied adviser can only offer their own insurer's range. For most clients, an IFA provides more objective, comprehensive advice.

**Fee transparency.** Understand exactly how your adviser is compensated before accepting advice. Commission-based advisers earn fees from the product provider when you purchase: there is no direct cost to you, but advisers may be influenced by commission structures. Fee-based advisers charge you directly for advice (typically SGD 150–500 per hour or a fixed planning fee), eliminating product-placement incentives. Ask for a clear written disclosure of compensation before the advisory process begins.

**Professional qualifications.** MAS requires financial advisers to hold a minimum standard of qualifications, but the CFP (Certified Financial Planner) and ChFC (Chartered Financial Consultant) designations represent substantially higher levels of education, examination, and ongoing professional development. When choosing between similarly reviewed advisers, a CFP or equivalent designation is a meaningful positive signal.

**Ongoing relationship vs transactional advice.** Financial planning is a continuous process: your situation changes, markets move, and insurance needs evolve. An adviser who engages with you only at product purchase is less valuable than one who conducts regular annual reviews, proactively contacts you when market conditions or regulation changes affect your plan, and maintains an updated picture of your complete financial position.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I choose a financial advisor in Singapore?

Start by identifying what you need: insurance coverage, investment management, retirement planning, or comprehensive financial planning. Verify any adviser's MAS licence at eservices.mas.gov.sg, confirm whether they are an independent financial adviser (IFA) or tied to a single insurer, and ask for a written disclosure of how they are compensated. Request references from existing clients of similar profile and schedule a no-obligation initial meeting to assess how well the adviser listens and communicates before committing.

### What is a MAS-licensed financial advisor?

A MAS-licensed financial adviser holds a Financial Adviser's Licence issued by the Monetary Authority of Singapore under the Financial Advisers Act. This licence authorises the adviser to provide regulated financial advisory services in Singapore, including advice on life insurance policies, investment products, and retirement planning. Advisers must meet MAS examination and qualification requirements, maintain adequate professional indemnity insurance, and comply with MAS conduct obligations. You can verify an adviser's licence at eservices.mas.gov.sg.

### What is the difference between fee-based and commission-based financial advisors?

Commission-based advisers earn remuneration from the financial product provider when a client purchases a product: the client pays nothing directly for the advice. Fee-based advisers charge clients directly for advice, either by the hour (typically SGD 150–500/hour) or a fixed fee for a comprehensive financial plan (typically SGD 2,000–10,000). Fee-based advice eliminates the financial incentive to recommend higher-commission products, but requires clients to pay for advice regardless of whether they implement recommendations. Many advisers in Singapore operate on a hybrid model.

### What is the CFP certification and why does it matter?

The Certified Financial Planner (CFP) designation is awarded by the Financial Planning Association of Singapore (FPAS) under global standards set by the CFP Board. It requires completion of an approved education programme covering financial planning, insurance, investment, tax, and estate planning; passing a comprehensive examination; three years of qualifying professional experience; and commitment to an ongoing ethics and continuing education requirement. CFP holders typically provide more comprehensive, integrated financial planning than advisers holding only the minimum MAS-required qualifications.

### How much do financial advisors charge in Singapore?

Commission-based advisers have no direct cost to clients: they earn from product placement. Fee-based advisers charge SGD 150–500 per hour for advisory time, or SGD 2,000–10,000 for a comprehensive written financial plan. Some IFAs charge a combination of fee and commission. Annual portfolio management fees for investment advisory typically range from 0.5% to 1.5% of assets under advice. Always obtain a written fee disclosure before engaging.

### Can foreigners and expatriates use Singapore financial advisors?

Yes. MAS-licensed financial advisers can advise both Singapore citizens, permanent residents, and foreigners holding valid Singapore employment passes. Expatriates have distinct financial planning needs: no access to CPF, cross-border tax obligations, portable investment vehicles, and internationally valid insurance coverage: and should ideally engage an adviser with documented experience in expatriate financial planning, such as the Expat Advisory Group (IPP Financial Advisers).

### What is CPF and how do financial advisors help with it?

The Central Provident Fund (CPF) is Singapore's mandatory social security savings scheme, requiring monthly contributions from Singapore citizens and permanent residents. CPF has multiple accounts: Ordinary Account (OA), Special Account (SA), MediSave Account (MA): each with distinct interest rates and withdrawal rules. Financial advisers help optimise CPF by advising on voluntary top-ups to earn higher interest, CPF Investment Scheme (CPFIS) strategies for OA and SA funds, MediShield Life supplementation, and CPF LIFE retirement income payout optimisation.

### What documents should I bring to my first financial advisor meeting?

Bring your NRIC or passport, CPF statement (printable from cpf.gov.sg), existing insurance policy schedule pages showing coverage and premiums, recent CPF Investment Scheme statements if applicable, a summary of your investment accounts and approximate values, your latest payslip or income tax notice of assessment, and a brief note on your financial goals and timeline. The more complete your financial picture, the more targeted the initial planning session will be.

### What is the difference between an IFA and a tied financial advisor?

An Independent Financial Adviser (IFA) holds an MAS Financial Adviser's Licence that authorises them to recommend financial products from any product provider: they are not employed by or tied to any single insurer or fund house. A tied adviser is employed by a specific financial institution and can only recommend their employer's products. IFAs offer broader market access and theoretically more objective advice. Most of the firms on this list are IFAs.

### When should I consider changing my financial advisor?

Consider changing your financial adviser if they fail to conduct regular annual reviews of your financial plan, you feel pressured toward products without adequate explanation of alternatives, they cannot explain how they are compensated, your adviser has left the firm and not been replaced with comparable quality, or your financial situation has evolved significantly: marriage, children, inheritance, business sale: and your existing adviser lacks the relevant planning expertise for your new circumstances.
