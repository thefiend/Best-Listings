## What to Look for When Hiring a Financial Advisor

**MAS licensing and registration.** Every financial adviser in Singapore must be licensed by the Monetary Authority of Singapore. You can verify any firm's licence and any individual adviser's Representative's Licence on the MAS Financial Institutions Directory at mas.gov.sg. Never engage an adviser who cannot point you to their MAS registration, and check whether they have any enforcement history on the same register.

**Independent vs. tied adviser.** An independent financial adviser (IFA) can recommend products from any provider in the market. A tied agent represents only one insurer or fund manager. For genuinely comprehensive and unbiased advice, an IFA is almost always the better choice unless you are specifically buying from a brand you already trust. Clarify this distinction before the first meeting.

**Fee structure and conflict of interest.** Ask how your adviser is compensated. Commission-based advisers earn from the products they recommend, which creates an inherent conflict. Fee-only advisers charge directly and earn nothing from product recommendations. Most clients benefit from understanding this structure before engaging, so they can assess the advice they receive in context.

**Adviser credentials.** Look for recognised financial planning designations: Certified Financial Planner (CFP), Chartered Financial Consultant (ChFC), and Associate Estate Planning Practitioner (AEPP) are the most meaningful. These require multi-year study and adherence to professional ethics codes. An adviser with verifiable credentials is more likely to provide technically sound advice than one without.

**Ongoing service commitment.** A financial plan is not a one-time product — it needs to be reviewed as your income, family situation, and financial markets change. Ask prospective advisers what their annual review process looks like, how they stay in contact between reviews, and whether they will still be engaged with your plan in five years. Continuity is one of the most undervalued aspects of financial advisory relationships.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between an IFA and a tied agent in Singapore?

An Independent Financial Adviser (IFA) is licensed by MAS and can recommend financial products from any provider in the market. A tied agent is employed by or contracted to a specific insurer (such as AIA, Prudential, or Great Eastern) and can only sell that company's products. IFAs provide genuinely market-wide recommendations, while tied agents are limited to their employer's product shelf. For unbiased advice, choose an IFA.

### Do I need a financial adviser if I have a CPF account?

CPF provides a foundation for retirement but is typically insufficient on its own, particularly for individuals with income above the CPF contribution ceiling or those with significant financial obligations. A financial adviser can help optimise CPF contributions (including voluntary top-ups and CPF Life selections), identify insurance gaps that CPF does not cover, and build an investment portfolio that complements CPF savings.

### How much should a good financial adviser in Singapore cost?

Fee-based advisers typically charge SGD 3,000 to SGD 15,000 for a comprehensive financial plan, depending on the complexity of your situation and the scope of the planning work. Commission-based advisers do not charge upfront fees but earn from the products they sell. The total cost of commission-based advice is often higher over time than fee-based advice due to ongoing trail commissions embedded in insurance and investment products.

### How do I verify if a financial adviser is licensed in Singapore?

Visit the MAS Financial Institutions Directory at mas.gov.sg/financial-institutions and search for the adviser's firm. Individual representative licences can be checked on the same platform under the MAS Register of Representatives. Always verify before engaging. Unlicensed financial advice is illegal in Singapore and provides no consumer protection.

### What is the best type of insurance to get in Singapore?

Most financial advisers recommend a combination of term life insurance for pure death benefit coverage (low cost, high coverage amount), critical illness insurance to protect against the financial impact of major illness, and medisave-integrated hospitalisation insurance to complement MediShield Life. Whole life and investment-linked policies are more complex and should be assessed carefully with a trusted IFA before committing.

### Should I invest in a CPF SA top-up or private investments first?

CPF SA top-ups are tax-deductible (up to SGD 8,000 per year) and the SA earns 4% guaranteed. For most employees in the 22-24% income tax bracket, the tax saving plus guaranteed 4% return on CPF SA is a compelling proposition before committing to private investments with higher risk. However, the illiquidity of CPF funds means private investments may be preferred for near-term financial goals. A qualified adviser can help structure the allocation based on your tax position, liquidity needs, and retirement timeline.

### What is the difference between a financial adviser and a financial planner?

In Singapore's regulatory context, both titles can refer to MAS-licensed practitioners offering financial advice. "Financial planner" typically implies a more comprehensive, holistic approach covering all aspects of personal finance, while "financial adviser" may refer more specifically to product recommendations. Practically, look for the CFP designation as the clearest signal of genuine comprehensive financial planning competence.

### How often should I review my financial plan?

Most advisers recommend a comprehensive review annually, with more frequent check-ins when significant life events occur: marriage, children, property purchase, career change, inheritance, or divorce. Annual reviews ensure insurance coverage remains adequate as incomes and obligations change, and that investment allocations remain appropriate as market conditions and personal risk tolerance evolve.
