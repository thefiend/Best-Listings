Singapore's plant-based dining scene has undergone a genuine transformation over the past decade. What was once a category dominated by Chinese Buddhist canteens and Indian vegetarian tiffin halls has expanded into a diverse, confident ecosystem that now includes Michelin-recommended fine dining, Japanese-French fusion, all-vegan burger chains, and raw food cafes operating across the island. The motivation to eat vegetarian or vegan in Singapore has also broadened: from religious observance and health consciousness to environmental concern and simple culinary curiosity: and the restaurants serving this demand have risen to meet it.

We reviewed more than 40 vegetarian and vegan restaurants across Singapore, evaluating each on menu creativity, taste execution, Google rating, ambience, vegan-friendliness, and value for money. The result is a list that spans every budget and occasion: from a casual bowl under SGD 15 to a Michelin-recommended tasting menu.

- **Best overall:** Original Sin in Holland Village, Singapore's most respected Mediterranean vegetarian restaurant with 4.3 stars and a menu that has anchored the city's plant-based scene for over two decades

### 1. Original Sin, Best Overall

<img src="/images/places/best-vegetarian-restaurants-singapore/original-sin.jpg" alt="Original Sin" className="w-full rounded-lg my-4 object-cover" style={{height: "300px"}} />

Original Sin, operating from Holland Village since 1997, is the foundational address for Mediterranean vegetarian dining in Singapore: a restaurant that predates the modern plant-based movement by more than fifteen years and has remained the standard against which every subsequent vegetarian restaurant opening in Singapore is measured. Its 4.3-star rating reflects sustained quality across decades rather than a recent surge of enthusiasm, and the menu is built on a genuine philosophy: Mediterranean cuisine is naturally abundant in plant-based ingredients, and removing meat from the equation does not require substitution or compromise: it requires skill, creativity, and respect for seasonal produce.

The menu spans handmade pasta, wood-fired pizza, Mediterranean salads, and main courses that rotate with produce availability: dishes like mushroom and lentil ragu, roasted aubergine cannelloni, and spinach ricotta gnocchi that would hold their own at any non-vegetarian Italian restaurant in the city. Original Sin has also been at the forefront of vegan adaptation, modifying a significant proportion of the menu to be fully vegan on request without diminishing the dish. For diners looking for the best vegetarian restaurant Singapore offers as an all-round experience: quality, creativity, ambience, and longevity: Original Sin remains the answer.

## Vegetarian vs Vegan: What's the Difference at Singapore Restaurants?

The distinction matters practically when dining in Singapore, and it is worth understanding clearly before you book.

**Vegetarian** means the food contains no meat, poultry, or seafood. Most vegetarian restaurants in Singapore: including Original Sin, Whole Earth, Elemen, and Sufood: use dairy and eggs freely in their cooking. A pasta dish at Original Sin may contain parmesan; a Sufood set meal may include a milk-based dessert. Vegetarian diners who consume dairy and eggs have no restrictions at these restaurants. Buddhist vegetarian food (as at Lotus Vegetarian) has the additional restriction of excluding pungent vegetables: garlic, onion, leeks: as a religious practice.

**Vegan** means the food contains no animal products at all: no meat, no seafood, no dairy, no eggs, no honey. Fully vegan restaurants in Singapore: Brownice, Real Food, VeganBurg, and The Kind Bowl: cook without any animal-derived ingredients, eliminating the need to check individual dishes. At mixed vegetarian restaurants like Original Sin and Joie, many dishes can be modified to be vegan on request, but the kitchen uses animal products and cross-contact is possible.

For strict vegan diners, the safest option is a fully vegan restaurant where the kitchen never handles animal products. For vegetarian diners who consume dairy and eggs, the full list above is accessible. For Buddhist vegetarian practitioners who avoid pungent vegetables, Lotus Vegetarian is the most reliable specialist option.

## Hawker Vegetarian Options in Singapore

Singapore's hawker centres are the city's most democratic dining format, and vegetarian options at hawker level have expanded significantly over the past decade. Several stalls and categories are reliably accessible:

**Indian vegetarian stalls** are the most numerous and consistent hawker vegetarian option across Singapore. Available at most hawker centres, Indian vegetarian banana leaf rice stalls serve rice with multiple vegetable curries, dhal, papadum, and pickles for SGD 4–8 per set: a nutritionally complete, flavourful meal that is fully vegetarian and often vegan. The Tekka Centre in Little India has the highest concentration of quality Indian vegetarian stalls, but hawker centres across the island carry at least one.

**Chinese vegetarian economic rice** operates similarly to Chinese economy rice but with fully vegetarian ingredients: braised tofu, stir-fried vegetables, mock meat preparations, and preserved vegetable dishes served over rice or with bee hoon for SGD 3.50–6. These stalls are identified by green signage or by the word "vegetarian" in Chinese (素食) and are found in most HDB-estate hawker centres. Quality varies significantly between stalls: the best operate with the same wok technique and seasoning depth as their non-vegetarian counterparts.

**Yong tau foo** with careful selection offers a de facto vegetarian meal at hawker level: the soup base (request plain clear broth), tofu, eggplant, and vegetable items can be selected while avoiding fish-paste-stuffed items. Confirm with the stall whether the soup base is vegetarian, as many use fish stock.

**Rojak** (fruit and vegetable salad), **soon kueh** (vegetable-stuffed dumplings), and **chee cheong fun** with vegetarian toppings are additional hawker options accessible to vegetarian diners at most centres.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best vegetarian restaurant in Singapore?

Original Sin in Holland Village is the best overall vegetarian restaurant in Singapore: a Mediterranean vegetarian kitchen that has operated at a consistent 4.3-star standard for over two decades, with genuine culinary ambition rather than a simple meat-free menu. For fine dining, Joie by Dozo at Orchard Central is Michelin Bib Gourmand-recommended and unmatched in the Japanese-French fusion vegetarian category. For Chinese Buddhist vegetarian, Lotus Vegetarian is the most rigorously traditional option. The right answer depends on the occasion, cuisine preference, and budget: this list covers the full range.

### Is Singapore vegetarian-friendly?

Singapore is one of Asia's most vegetarian-friendly cities. The population includes a significant proportion of Hindu, Buddhist, and health-conscious residents who observe vegetarian diets, and the restaurant industry has developed robust infrastructure to serve them: from dedicated vegetarian restaurants and fully vegan cafes to Indian vegetarian hawker stalls at most food centres. Most mainstream restaurants in Singapore now offer at least one vegetarian main course option, and the fully vegetarian and vegan restaurant sector has grown substantially since 2015. International vegan travellers consistently rank Singapore among the most accessible cities in Asia for plant-based dining.

### What is Chinese Buddhist vegetarian food?

Chinese Buddhist vegetarian food is a distinct culinary tradition that excludes not only meat, poultry, and seafood but also the five pungent root vegetables avoided in Buddhist practice: garlic, onion, leeks, shallots, and chives. The tradition holds that these vegetables stimulate desires and agitation, conflicting with Buddhist principles of calm and moderation. The culinary result is a cuisine that develops flavour entirely through technique, fermented ingredients, dried mushrooms, tofu preparations, and slow-cooking methods: producing deeply savoury, complex food without the aromatic base that most Chinese cooking relies on. Chinese Buddhist vegetarian restaurants like Lotus Vegetarian in Singapore are the primary commercial expression of this tradition, serving dim sum, claypot dishes, mock meat preparations, and rice and noodle dishes within these constraints.

### Are there vegan hawker options in Singapore?

Yes, though they require some navigation. Indian vegetarian banana leaf rice stalls at hawker centres are the most accessible: the rice, vegetable curries, dhal, and papadum are typically vegan (confirm that the curries are not made with ghee or dairy). Chinese vegetarian economic rice stalls serve plant-based dishes that are often vegan, though some use egg. Fruit rojak without the prawn paste (hae ko) is vegan. Yong tau foo can be assembled as a vegan meal by selecting vegetable-only items and requesting a plain vegetable broth rather than a fish-based soup. The Vegetarian Society Singapore maintains a list of hawker stalls with certified vegetarian credentials across the island.

### How do I order vegetarian at a zi char stall?

Zi char (煮炒) stalls: the Chinese kitchen-style stalls found at hawker centres and coffee shops: can almost always accommodate vegetarian requests, though the quality varies. The most reliable approach: order dishes that are inherently plant-based: stir-fried kailan with garlic, braised tofu in claypot, steamed egg with preserved vegetables, or butter milk pumpkin. Specify "no meat, no seafood" (没有肉，没有海鲜 in Mandarin, or "no meat, no prawn" in English) when ordering, and confirm whether the wok is shared with meat cooking if cross-contact is a concern. Oyster sauce: used in most zi char vegetable dishes: contains oyster extract and is not vegetarian; request "no oyster sauce" (没有蚝油) for strict vegetarian dishes. Most zi char cooks can substitute light soy sauce or sesame oil without difficulty.
