## What Causes Aircon Water Leaking in Singapore

Understanding why your aircon is leaking helps you communicate accurately with the repair technician and avoid being charged for work that is not needed.

**Blocked drain pan or condensate pipe.** This is the most common cause in Singapore, accounting for roughly 60% of residential aircon water leak calls. The drain pan collects condensation from the evaporator coil; the condensate pipe carries that water to the drainage point. Dust, mould, algae, and debris accumulate in both over time. When the pipe or pan becomes partially or fully blocked, condensation overflows the pan and drips from the unit. The fix is drain pan cleaning and pipe flushing, which takes 30–60 minutes and costs SGD 60–120 depending on the severity of the blockage.

**Frozen evaporator coils.** When airflow across the evaporator coil is restricted (from a clogged air filter or low refrigerant gas), the coil temperature drops below freezing point and ice forms on the fins and piping. When the unit cycles off or the ice load becomes too heavy, it melts rapidly and overwhelms the drain pan capacity. The visible symptom is water dripping in larger volumes than a typical drain pan overflow. The fix requires defrosting the coil, identifying and correcting the root cause (filter replacement, refrigerant top-up, or both), and cleaning the drain pan of the overflow residue. Cost: SGD 150–300 depending on whether a gas top-up is needed.

**Dirty or clogged air filter.** A filter so clogged that it restricts airflow can lead to both reduced cooling and frozen coils. Regular filter cleaning every 4–6 weeks in Singapore's dusty environment prevents this. If a water leak is traced to a dirty filter, the repair is straightforward: filter cleaning or replacement, coil inspection, and drain pan check.

**Failed or undersized condensate pump.** In aircon installations where the indoor unit is below the drainage outlet, a condensate pump pushes water upward to the drain point. A failed or worn pump allows water to back up into the drain pan and overflow. Condensate pump replacement costs SGD 150–250 including parts and installation.

**Improper installation or pipe gradient.** If the condensate pipe was installed with an incorrect gradient (uphill rather than downhill toward the drain), water collects rather than draining, eventually backing up into the drain pan. This is a less common cause in established installations but is seen in post-renovation or new installation jobs. Rerouting the condensate pipe costs SGD 300–600 depending on access difficulty.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is my aircon leaking water in Singapore?

The most common cause is a blocked drain pan or condensate pipe, where accumulated dust, mould, and debris prevent condensation from draining correctly, causing the pan to overflow. Frozen evaporator coils (caused by a dirty filter or low refrigerant) are the second most common cause, producing a larger volume of water when the ice melts. Less common causes include a failed condensate pump, an incorrectly installed drain pipe with insufficient gradient, or physical damage to the drain pan itself.

Drain pan cleaning and condensate pipe clearing: SGD 60–120. Frozen coil defrost with filter replacement: SGD 80–150. Gas top-up (if low refrigerant is contributing to frozen coils): SGD 80–150 additional. Condensate pump replacement: SGD 150–250. Condensate pipe rerouting: SGD 300–600. For most residential HDB or condo units, a single-cause repair (blocked drain pan or dirty filter) costs SGD 60–150 all-in. ### Can I fix an aircon water leak myself?

Cleaning the accessible part of the drain pan tray and the air filter is something most homeowners can do safely. However, flushing the condensate pipe, defrosting a frozen coil, or investigating the condensate drainage system beyond the visible tray requires tools, access, and diagnostic knowledge that most homeowners do not have. Attempting to clear a deeply blocked condensate pipe without the right equipment risks pushing the blockage further into the pipe or damaging the drain pan seal. For anything beyond basic filter cleaning, calling a professional is faster, safer, and typically produces a more complete fix.

### How long does aircon water leaking repair take?

A standard drain pan cleaning and pipe flush takes 30–60 minutes per unit. A frozen coil defrost requires the unit to be powered off for 30–90 minutes before the technician can complete the inspection and repair; total visit time is typically 1.5–2.5 hours. Condensate pump replacement takes 1–2 hours. Pipe rerouting is a half-day to full-day job depending on wall access. ### How do I prevent my aircon from leaking in Singapore?

Clean the air filter every 4–6 weeks: a clogged filter is both the leading cause of frozen coils and a contributing factor to mould growth in the drain pan. Schedule a professional general service every three months, which includes drain pan inspection and cleaning as standard. Set the thermostat to 24–26°C rather than extreme low settings; running the unit at 16–18°C in Singapore's humidity accelerates coil freeze risk. After any servicing, run the unit for 20 minutes and inspect the drain pan area visually for any dripping before assuming the repair is complete.

### Is a leaking aircon dangerous?

A leaking aircon is not electrically dangerous in most cases, as the condensate water is low-voltage byproduct separate from the electrical components. However, if water is dripping near the electrical socket, the isolator switch, or the indoor unit's wiring, power off the unit at the isolator switch immediately and do not restore power until a technician has inspected the unit. Water saturation of ceiling boards, cornices, and plaster walls causes structural weakening and mould growth within 24–48 hours in Singapore's humidity: the property damage risk is the more pressing concern in most residential leak cases.

### Will the aircon water leaking repair company clean up the water damage?

Aircon repair companies fix the source of the leak; they do not typically provide water damage remediation services such as drying out ceiling boards, repainting stained walls, or treating mould. If water damage is significant, a separate water damage or mould remediation contractor is required. Confirm the repair technician's scope before booking to avoid misunderstanding.

### How do I know if my aircon water leak is from a blocked drain pan or frozen coils?

A drain pan blockage typically produces a steady, low-volume drip from the unit's base or front panel, often present whenever the aircon is running. A frozen coil leak produces a larger volume of water intermittently, often most visible when the unit switches off or is turned off after a long running period (as the accumulated ice melts). If you see frost or ice visible on the piping near the indoor unit, or if the unit blows air but cools poorly, frozen coils are the likely cause. If the unit cools normally but drips steadily, a blocked drain pan or pipe is more likely. Either way, a qualified technician should confirm the diagnosis before any repair is undertaken.

### Should I turn off my aircon if it is leaking water?

Yes. If your aircon is actively dripping water, power it off at the isolator switch or breaker, not just the remote. Continuing to run a unit with a blocked drain pan accelerates overflow and worsens water damage to ceilings and walls. Place a towel or container under the drip point to contain damage while you arrange a repair. Do not restart the unit until the repair is complete and the technician has confirmed the drainage is clear.
