## What to Look for When Hiring a Funeral Service

**Transparent pricing.** Request a written breakdown before committing. Reputable funeral companies will itemise every cost, including casket, floral arrangements, tent and table rental, MC services, and cremation or burial fees. Avoid providers who give only a lump-sum quote.

**24/7 availability.** Deaths do not occur on business hours. The best funeral companies maintain a 24-hour hotline and can deploy a team within one to two hours of your call. This is especially important for hospital or nursing home collections.

**Experience with your rites.** Buddhist, Taoist, Christian, Catholic, and Muslim funerals all have specific ceremonial requirements. Confirm that the company has direct experience with your family's religious tradition before engaging.

**Repatriation capability.** If your loved one passed away overseas, or if the family wishes to repatriate the remains to another country, verify that the funeral company is experienced in handling international documentation, air freight coordination, and local customs formalities.

**Post-funeral administrative support.** The practical tasks that follow a death, including cancelling NRIC, processing CPF nominations, and handling estate matters, can be overwhelming. Some funeral companies offer guidance or referrals for these steps. Ask upfront what post-funeral support is included.

**Physical showroom.** The ability to view caskets, urns, and other items in person, rather than making these decisions from a website, can matter to families who want to feel more in control during a difficult process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a funeral in Singapore cost?

Funeral costs in Singapore vary widely depending on the rites, duration, and add-ons selected. A basic package for a one-day wake with cremation typically starts from around $1,500 to $2,500. A full three-day Buddhist or Taoist wake with elaborate floral arrangements, a large tent, catering, and traditional music can cost $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Request an itemised quote from at least two providers before deciding.

### How quickly can a funeral company respond after a death?

Most reputable funeral companies in Singapore can deploy a team within one to two hours of a call. If the death occurs in a hospital, the hospital will typically advise you on next steps and some have partnerships with specific funeral providers. However, you are not obligated to use the hospital-recommended provider. You may contact any licensed funeral company directly.

### What are the main types of funerals in Singapore?

Singapore's most common funeral types are Buddhist, Taoist, Christian, Catholic, and Muslim. Buddhist and Taoist funerals typically involve a multi-day wake with chanting sessions, incense burning, and paper offerings. Christian and Catholic services are usually simpler in ceremony and may involve a church service. Muslim funerals are typically conducted within 24 hours and are handled by mosque-appointed coordinators. The funeral company you choose should have experience with your specific rites.

### What documents are needed to arrange a funeral in Singapore?

You will typically need the deceased's NRIC, the death certificate issued by the hospital or doctor, and the Certificate for Burial or Cremation issued by the Ministry of Health. The funeral company will guide you through obtaining these documents if they have not yet been issued. For non-Singaporean residents, additional documentation from the deceased's home country may be required.

### Is burial or cremation more common in Singapore?

Cremation is significantly more common in Singapore, accounting for over 80% of funerals due to limited land availability. HDB estates and many housing estates do not allow burials, and government cemeteries have a mandatory exhumation policy after 15 years. Most funeral companies handle both options and can advise on the process and costs for each.

### Can I pre-plan a funeral in Singapore?

Yes, several funeral companies in Singapore offer pre-need or pre-planning services, allowing individuals to arrange and pay for their own funeral in advance. This reduces the burden on family members at the time of death and allows the individual to specify their wishes regarding rites, casket, and arrangements. Companies such as Singapore Casket and Ang Chin Moh have structured pre-planning programmes.

### How do I handle the death of a foreign national in Singapore?

If the deceased is a foreign national, the funeral company will need to coordinate with the deceased's home country's embassy or consulate, arrange for an autopsy if required, and manage the repatriation of remains or ashes. The process typically takes longer and involves additional documentation. Funeral companies such as Direct Funeral Services and Singapore Casket have experience with repatriation cases.

### Are funeral company prices regulated in Singapore?

There is no government-regulated price list for funeral services in Singapore, which is why price transparency from the provider is important. The Consumers Association of Singapore (CASE) and the Funeral Industry Standards Council (FISC) provide some guidelines, but pricing ultimately varies by provider. Always request a written, itemised quote to avoid unexpected charges.
