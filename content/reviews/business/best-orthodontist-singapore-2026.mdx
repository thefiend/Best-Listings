Singapore's orthodontic market has undergone a significant transformation in the past decade. The mainstream adoption of clear aligner therapy — led by Invisalign but now including several competing systems — has broadened the patient base from teenagers in metal braces to working adults seeking discreet teeth straightening. At the same time, the Ministry of Health's specialist registration framework ensures that only dentists who have completed a recognised postgraduate orthodontics programme can legally hold themselves out as orthodontic specialists in Singapore. This framework matters: orthodontic treatment typically spans 18 to 36 months, and the quality of the treating clinician's training directly determines the outcome.

We evaluated more than 30 orthodontic clinics across Singapore, assessing MOH specialist registration status, Invisalign certification tier, digital treatment planning technology, before-and-after case quality, and Google review scores. The 10 clinics ranked here represent the strongest orthodontic practices available in Singapore in 2026, across a range of price points, locations, and specialisations.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** T32 Dental, an Invisalign Diamond Provider with multiple outlets and a comprehensive team of MOH-registered orthodontic specialists across all brace types
- **Best Invisalign provider:** Mint Orthodontics, an orthodontics-focused clinic with extensive Invisalign case volume and digital 3D treatment planning
- **Best for adults:** The Orthodontic Clinic at Tanglin Mall, a specialist orthodontic practice serving a professional adult patient base with clear aligner and ceramic brace options
- Indicative price ranges for 2026: metal braces SGD 3,500–6,000; ceramic braces SGD 4,500–7,500; Invisalign SGD 5,000–9,000 — all subject to case complexity and consultation assessment
- Medisave cannot be used for orthodontic treatment; some employer dental benefits cover a partial contribution — confirm with your insurer
- Red flag: any clinic that offers a treatment quote before completing a full clinical examination including digital X-rays and photographs, or that cannot confirm its treating clinician's MOH specialist registration

The clinic's multi-outlet presence across Singapore — including locations in the central business district and residential areas — means that working adults and families in different parts of the island can access specialist orthodontic care without lengthy commutes. ## Braces vs Invisalign vs Ceramic Braces — Which Is Right for You?

Choosing between orthodontic treatment modalities is one of the most common questions patients raise at their initial consultation. The right answer depends on clinical suitability, lifestyle, budget, and treatment priorities — not on which option is most heavily marketed.

**Metal braces** remain the most clinically versatile orthodontic appliance. They allow the most precise control over tooth movement, are effective for the widest range of case complexity including severe crowding, significant bite correction, and cases involving multiple missing or impacted teeth, and are the most affordable fixed appliance option. The trade-off is aesthetics: metal brackets are visible, which makes them less popular among adults in client-facing professional roles. For children and teenagers, and for adults where treatment complexity outweighs aesthetic concerns, metal braces are often the most effective choice.

**Ceramic braces** address the aesthetic limitation of metal braces by using tooth-coloured or clear ceramic brackets that blend with the natural colour of teeth. Clinically, ceramic braces provide a similar level of treatment precision to metal braces, making them a strong choice for cases that are too complex for clear aligners but where discretion is a priority. Ceramic brackets are more brittle than metal and require more careful management to avoid breakage, and they are more expensive — typically SGD 1,000–1,500 more than metal braces for a comparable case.

**Invisalign and clear aligners** are the preferred choice for adult patients with mild to moderate crowding and spacing issues who prioritise discretion and comfort. The aligners are removable, which allows normal eating and easier dental hygiene during treatment. However, Invisalign requires high patient compliance — aligners must be worn 20–22 hours per day to achieve the projected treatment outcome — and is not suitable for all case types. Complex bite corrections, significant rotations, and cases requiring tooth extractions may not be achievable with clear aligners alone or may require longer treatment timelines than fixed appliances. Invisalign is also the most expensive modality, and outcome quality is highly dependent on the treating clinician's experience in digital case planning.

**Lingual braces** are fixed appliances bonded to the inside surfaces of the teeth, making them completely invisible from the front. They are the most discreet fixed appliance option and are suitable for patients who want the precision of fixed braces without any visible brackets. Lingual braces require a specialist who is trained specifically in lingual technique, are more expensive than labial braces, and require an adjustment period for speech. They are not available at every clinic and are best suited for patients for whom aesthetics during treatment is the highest priority.

## How Much Do Braces Cost in Singapore? 2026 Price Guide

Orthodontic treatment costs in Singapore in 2026 reflect the qualifications of the treating clinician, the complexity of the case, the treatment modality selected, and the technology employed. The following ranges are indicative for straightforward to moderate cases; complex cases may fall outside these ranges.

**Metal braces:** SGD 3,500–6,000 for a full upper and lower case. Costs at the lower end typically reflect general dentists with orthodontic training at chain clinics; costs at the upper end reflect MOH-registered orthodontic specialists at dedicated specialist practices.

**Ceramic braces:** SGD 4,500–7,500 for a full case. The premium over metal braces reflects material costs and the additional clinical care required to manage ceramic brackets over the treatment period.

**Invisalign:** SGD 5,000–9,000 for a comprehensive Invisalign case. Invisalign Lite (for minor corrections) starts from approximately SGD 3,500–4,500. Diamond and Platinum Provider clinics tend to price at the upper end of the range, reflecting higher case volume experience. All Invisalign pricing includes refinements within the treatment cycle; additional refinements beyond the case allowance attract supplementary charges.

**Lingual braces:** SGD 8,000–14,000, reflecting the specialist technique required and the complexity of fabricating and placing brackets on the lingual surfaces.

**Retainers (post-treatment):** Hawley retainers SGD 200–400 per arch; clear Essex retainers SGD 150–300 per arch; bonded (fixed) retainers SGD 200–400 per arch. Retention is a lifelong commitment — the investment in a good retainer programme protects the full cost of the orthodontic treatment.

All prices are indicative. Costs vary by clinic, case complexity, and the specific treatment agreed at consultation. Medisave cannot be used for orthodontic treatment. Some employer dental benefit programmes contribute a fixed annual amount toward orthodontic treatment — check your HR policy or insurer for details.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much do braces cost in Singapore?**
Metal braces typically cost SGD 3,500–6,000 for a full upper and lower case, ceramic braces SGD 4,500–7,500, and Invisalign SGD 5,000–9,000 depending on case complexity and the clinic's Invisalign Provider tier. Lingual braces range from SGD 8,000–14,000. All prices are indicative and subject to individual assessment. Complex cases involving significant bite correction, multiple extractions, or combined orthodontic-surgical treatment will be at the higher end of or beyond these ranges.

**Is Invisalign covered by insurance in Singapore?**
Orthodontic treatment — including Invisalign — is not claimable under Medisave or MediShield Life, which cover medical and hospital expenses rather than dental treatment. Some employer dental benefit schemes offer an annual orthodontic subsidy, typically in the range of SGD 500–1,500 per year, which can be applied toward Invisalign costs. Check your company's dental benefit policy or your personal health insurance plan for specific orthodontic coverage terms. Dental insurance plans purchased privately may include orthodontic benefits subject to a waiting period.

**How long does orthodontic treatment take?**
Treatment duration depends on case complexity, the appliance type, and patient compliance. Straightforward crowding or spacing cases with metal or ceramic braces typically take 12–18 months. Moderate cases with significant bite correction may take 18–24 months. Complex cases, particularly those requiring tooth extractions or combined jaw surgery, can extend to 30–36 months or beyond. Invisalign treatment for mild to moderate cases typically runs 12–20 months; complex Invisalign cases may require 24 months or more including refinements. Patient compliance — especially aligner wear hours for Invisalign patients — is the most controllable variable affecting treatment duration.

**Can adults get braces?**
Yes. There is no age limit for orthodontic treatment. Adult orthodontics has grown significantly in Singapore, driven by the availability of clear aligners and ceramic braces that allow treatment without visible metal brackets. Adults may experience slightly longer treatment times than teenagers due to the absence of growth-related bone remodelling, and complex adult cases may require extractions or other pre-treatment dental work. Adults with existing crowns, bridges, or implants require careful treatment planning, and periodontal (gum) health must be assessed and stabilised before orthodontic forces are applied. Consulting an MOH-registered orthodontic specialist — rather than a general dentist — is particularly important for adult patients with complex dental histories.

**What is the difference between an orthodontist and a dentist?**
In Singapore, the title "orthodontist" is regulated by the Ministry of Health under the Dental Registration Act. A registered orthodontist has completed a recognised undergraduate dental degree, followed by a full-time postgraduate orthodontic specialist training programme of at least three years, and holds MOH specialist registration in orthodontics. A general dentist can perform basic orthodontic treatment — including fitting braces and providing Invisalign — but does not hold orthodontic specialist registration. For straightforward cases, a trained general dentist may deliver adequate outcomes. For complex bite corrections, adult cases with prior dental restorations, jaw discrepancy cases, or cases requiring extraction treatment planning, an MOH-registered orthodontic specialist provides a meaningfully higher level of clinical expertise. You can verify a clinician's specialist registration on the MOH's online register at moh.gov.sg.
