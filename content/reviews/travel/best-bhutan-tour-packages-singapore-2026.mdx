## How We Ranked These Bhutan Tour Packages

We evaluated 39 Bhutan tour packages available to Singapore travellers using five criteria. First, Google Maps rating and review volume: a minimum 4.5 rating and at least 25 verified reviews were required for inclusion. Second, SDF and inclusions transparency: packages that did not clearly state whether the SDF, licensed guide, accommodation, and in-country transport were included were disqualified. Third, licensed operator status: every agency on this list either holds a Tourism Council of Bhutan (TCB) licence directly or names their licensed Bhutan partner. Fourth, itinerary range: we assessed whether the operator could serve cultural, trekking, festival, luxury, and combination trip requests rather than only a single format. Fifth, responsiveness: we sent pre-booking enquiries and measured how quickly and thoroughly each operator responded. Agencies that could not confirm inclusions or itinerary details within a reasonable timeframe were excluded.

## What to Look for in a Bhutan Tour Package from Singapore

**SDF inclusion and package transparency.** Bhutan's Sustainable Development Fee of USD 100 per person per night is mandatory for all international tourists and must be paid through a licensed operator before a visa is issued. Any package that does not state clearly whether the SDF is included should be treated as incomplete. Request a full written breakdown: SDF, licensed guide fee, accommodation tier, in-country transport, and visa processing. A reputable operator provides this without hesitation.

**Licensed operator status and TCB accreditation.** Singapore passport holders cannot visit Bhutan independently. Every tour package must be operated through a company licensed by the Tourism Council of Bhutan (TCB), either directly or through an authorised international partner. Before booking, ask the agency to confirm their TCB licence number or name their licensed Bhutan ground operator. Packages sold by unlicensed intermediaries risk denied boarding at Paro.

**Guide certification.** Only TCB-certified guides are legally permitted to accompany foreign tourists outside Thimphu and Paro town areas. Ask specifically whether the guide is TCB-certified and, where possible, request the guide's name and background before departure. **Itinerary depth beyond the standard circuit.** Most Bhutan packages follow the Thimphu-Paro-Punakha circuit. **Emergency support and in-trip communication.** Bhutan's mountainous terrain and limited infrastructure mean that road closures, flight cancellations, and health emergencies require fast, knowledgeable responses. Druk Air and Bhutan Airlines are the only carriers serving Paro International Airport. Neither operates a direct Singapore-Paro route. ### What is the best time to visit Bhutan from Singapore?

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are optimal. Spring offers the Paro Tsechu festival in March or April, rhododendron blooms across the valleys, and comfortable temperatures for trekking. Autumn brings the Thimphu Tsechu in September or October, clear visibility of the Himalayan peaks, and ideal conditions for the Druk Path and Jomolhari treks. Monsoon (June to August) brings heavy rain and poor road conditions. Winter (December to February) is cold but quieter and less expensive.

### What is Bhutan's Sustainable Development Fee and do I have to pay it?

The Sustainable Development Fee (SDF) is USD 100 per person per night as of 2024. It is mandatory for all international tourists except citizens of India, Bangladesh, and Maldives. It is not a discretionary charge and cannot be waived. The SDF funds Bhutan's free healthcare, free education, and conservation infrastructure. It must be paid in advance through your licensed tour operator before a visa can be issued.
