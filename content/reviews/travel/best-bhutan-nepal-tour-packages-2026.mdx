Combining Bhutan and Nepal into a single tour is one of the most rewarding multi-destination itineraries available to Singapore travellers, placing the world's last Himalayan Buddhist kingdom beside the trekking capital of Asia in a single trip. The logistics are complex: Bhutan's mandatory Sustainable Development Fee, licensed guide requirements, and limited airline access must be coordinated with Nepal's peak trekking seasons, Kathmandu cultural circuits, and Everest region flights. We evaluated 50 combined Bhutan-Nepal tour operators accessible to Singapore travellers, scoring each on itinerary coherence, guide quality, SDF and visa handling, accommodation standards, and verified customer reviews. This list covers the 10 best Bhutan and Nepal combined tour packages for 2026, ranked to help Singapore travellers book with confidence.

**Key Takeaways**

- **Best overall:** Bhutan Best Travel and Tour, a Singapore-based specialist with 1,299 Google reviews and fully customised Bhutan-Nepal combined packages
- **Best Nepal-Bhutan adventure specialist:** Brothers International Tours, with dedicated adventure itineraries covering trekking in both destinations
- **Best Singapore-based Bhutan-Nepal specialist:** Druk Asia, operating from Albert Street with 313 Google reviews and deep dual-destination expertise
- Combined Bhutan-Nepal tours from Singapore typically run 10 to 18 days, with the most popular formats pairing 5 to 7 nights in Bhutan with 4 to 7 nights in Nepal
- Bhutan's SDF of USD 100 per person per night must be pre-paid through a licensed operator before a visa can be issued; always confirm whether this is included in your quoted price
- The most efficient routing from Singapore is Singapore to Kathmandu (direct or via Bangkok), then Kathmandu to Paro (Druk Air or Bhutan Airlines), before returning to Singapore from Kathmandu

## What to Look for When Booking a Bhutan-Nepal Combined Tour

**SDF transparency across the full itinerary.** Bhutan's Sustainable Development Fee of USD 100 per person per night is mandatory and must be paid before a visa is issued. Combined tour quotes that do not explicitly list the SDF as included are misrepresenting the true cost. Always ask for an itemised breakdown covering Bhutan SDF, licensed guide fees, accommodation categories, inter-country flights, and Nepal guide and logistics separately.

**Licensed Bhutan operator status.** All foreign tourists entering Bhutan must travel through a licensed operator registered with the Tourism Council of Bhutan (TCB). If your Singapore-based agency is not itself a TCB licence holder, confirm which licensed Bhutan partner they use. Unlicensed arrangements can result in denied entry at Paro Airport, which would collapse the entire combined itinerary.

**Itinerary sequencing and inter-country logistics.** The Kathmandu to Paro routing is the only air connection between Nepal and Bhutan, operated by Druk Air and Bhutan Airlines. The best combined tour operators plan itineraries around available flight schedules and factor in buffer days in case of weather delays at Paro, which has one of the world's most weather-sensitive airport approaches.

**Guide quality in both destinations.** A Bhutan-Nepal combined tour involves two separate guide teams operating under different licensing bodies. Strong operators brief Singapore travellers on guide certifications in both countries and assign guides with demonstrated cultural knowledge rather than generic tour guides with limited specialist depth.

**Post-booking communication and in-trip support.** Multi-destination Himalayan travel carries a higher operational complexity than single-country tours. When something changes, such as a Paro weather delay, a Kathmandu road closure, or a last-minute hotel issue, you need an operator who can respond in real time. Singapore-based operators like Bhutan Best Travel, Druk Asia, and Brothers International offer this by default.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should a combined Bhutan and Nepal tour be?

The minimum practical duration for a combined Bhutan-Nepal tour is 10 days, allowing 5 to 6 nights in Bhutan and 4 nights in Nepal. Most Singapore travellers choose 12 to 16 day itineraries to include more depth in both destinations, such as adding Punakha and Bumthang in Bhutan or Pokhara and a short Annapurna foothills trek in Nepal. Festival-period travel or serious trekking in either country warrants 18 to 21 days.

### Which country should I visit first, Bhutan or Nepal?

Most operators recommend visiting Bhutan first, then Nepal. Bhutan's altitude (Paro is at 2,200m, and many cultural sites are higher) provides a gentler acclimatisation than Nepal's Everest region treks. Starting in Bhutan also keeps the more logistically complex destination, which requires visa pre-processing and SDF payment, at the beginning of the trip when planning is still fresh. Finishing in Nepal at a lower altitude with more flexible transport options is operationally easier.

### What is the total cost of a Bhutan-Nepal combined tour from Singapore?

A standard 12-day combined Bhutan-Nepal tour from Singapore typically costs SGD 5,000 to SGD 8,000 per person, covering flights from Singapore, inter-country Paro-Kathmandu flight, SDF, licensed guides in both countries, accommodation, and in-country transport. Budget group tours with Nam Ho or Chan Brothers may be lower; luxury private itineraries with Beyond Footprints including top-tier Bhutan lodges start from SGD 12,000 to SGD 20,000 per person.

### Do Singapore passport holders need a visa for both Bhutan and Nepal?

Yes. A Bhutan visa is required and must be processed through your licensed tour operator before travel; there is no on-arrival or self-application option for Singapore citizens. Nepal issues on-arrival visas for Singapore passport holders at Tribhuvan International Airport or at the Tribhuvan overland border crossings. The Bhutan visa typically takes 3 to 5 working days after SDF payment is confirmed.

### How do I fly from Singapore to start a Bhutan-Nepal combined tour?

The most common routing is Singapore to Kathmandu (direct flights available on Singapore Airlines and Nepal Airlines), then Kathmandu to Paro on Druk Air or Bhutan Airlines for the Bhutan leg, then returning Paro to Kathmandu for the Nepal leg before flying back to Singapore. Some itineraries reverse this, flying Singapore to Kathmandu first for Nepal, then Kathmandu to Paro for Bhutan, and returning directly from Paro to Kathmandu to Singapore.

### What is the best time of year for a Bhutan-Nepal combined tour?

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the best periods. Spring aligns with Bhutan's Paro Tsechu festival (March or April), clear mountain views, and Nepal's peak Annapurna and Everest trekking season. Autumn offers Bhutan's Thimphu Tsechu, excellent visibility, and ideal Nepal trekking conditions after the monsoon clears. Both peak seasons require booking at least 3 months in advance for festival periods.

### Can I trek in both Bhutan and Nepal during the same trip?

Yes, though most travellers limit serious trekking to one destination per combined trip. Common formats include a multi-day Nepal trek paired with day hikes in Bhutan (such as the Tiger's Nest approach), or a Bhutan cultural and trekking circuit paired with Kathmandu sightseeing and a Pokhara day hike in Nepal. Full multi-day treks in both destinations require at least 18 to 21 days total. Operators like Brothers International Tours and Tribe Tours specialise in these dual-trek itineraries.

### Are group tours or private tours better for Bhutan-Nepal combined trips?

Private tours offer greater flexibility: custom dates, personalised itineraries, and the ability to adjust pacing in real time. Group tours offer lower per-person pricing and a social experience, particularly suited to solo travellers or those on a tighter budget. For a combined Bhutan-Nepal tour, BestThingReview.com recommends private tours due to the higher logistics complexity across two destinations, where unexpected delays are more common and itinerary flexibility has more value.

### What should I pack for a combined Bhutan-Nepal tour?

Key items include: layers for altitude changes (Bhutan's high passes and Nepal's trekking zones reach 3,000 to 5,000m), comfortable walking shoes suitable for monastery stairs and uneven trails, sun protection for high-altitude UV exposure, cash in USD (for SDF payment confirmation if needed) and Bhutanese ngultrum and Nepalese rupees for daily use, and a good-quality daypack for trekking days. Bhutan's festivals require modest dress for temple visits, so light long-sleeved clothing in neutral tones is practical.

### What red flags should I watch for when booking a Bhutan-Nepal combined tour?

Red flags include: no explicit SDF statement in the quote; inability to name their licensed Bhutan TCB partner; packages priced below SGD 3,500 per person for 10 days including flights (likely omitting SDF or using substandard accommodation); vague itinerary descriptions with no hotel names; no refund or amendment policy; and agencies with fewer than 10 verifiable Google reviews.
